02 — Essential Elements

Six elements every UGC portfolio needs

  • A clear opening statement that tells brands your niche and outcome in one sentence
  • Video work front and center — playable, not thumbnails or screenshots
  • Brand logos, rates, social proof, and a single clear CTA

After reviewing thousands of creator portfolios across our network, these are the six non-negotiable elements that appear in every portfolio that consistently wins brand deals.

01. A clear opening statement

The first line of your portfolio should tell a brand exactly what you do and who you do it for. Not your life story. Not a list of platforms. One sentence: niche, content type, and the outcome you deliver.

  • State your niche and content type upfront
  • Mention the outcome brands care about
  • Skip the generic "passionate creator" intro
  • Keep it under 20 words

02. Video work front and center

Your best three to five videos should be the first thing a brand sees after your intro. Not thumbnails. Not screenshots. Playable videos with native sound. If a brand manager has to click through to another site or request file access, you have already lost them.

  • Lead with your strongest piece, not your newest
  • Include a mix of content types (testimonial, unboxing, tutorial)
  • Make sure every video loads and plays instantly
  • Remove anything older than 12 months unless it went viral

03. Brand logos and collaboration history

Even if you have worked with two brands, show them. Logos create instant credibility. A portfolio without brand names feels like a resume without job titles. Group them visually so a scanning eye catches them in under two seconds.

  • Use recognizable logos, not just text names
  • Group by industry if you have 5+ brands
  • Include spec work clearly labeled as such
  • Update after every new collaboration

04. Rates and availability

Brands do not want to play email ping-pong to find out your pricing. Include at least a starting rate or a range. Creators who show rates get 40% more inbound inquiries in our experience, because brand managers can immediately see whether the budget fits.

  • Show starting rates, not exact quotes
  • List turnaround time for standard deliverables
  • Mention if you offer usage rights separately
  • Include your preferred contact method

05. Social proof and metrics

If your video hit 500K views, say so. If a brand's CPA dropped 30% after running your creative, include that number. Social proof converts hesitant brand managers into paying clients faster than any bio ever will.

  • Include view counts on standout videos
  • Show performance metrics where available
  • Add client testimonials even if they are short
  • Link to live posts so brands can verify

06. A single clear CTA

Every portfolio needs exactly one call to action. Not three. Not a paragraph of options. One button or one email address. The easier you make it to reach you, the more brands will actually do it.

  • Use a specific email, not a contact form
  • Add a booking link for quick calls
  • Place the CTA after your video section
  • Repeat it at the bottom of the page
03 — Structure

The ideal portfolio structure, top to bottom

  • Think of your portfolio as a funnel — each section pulls the brand manager deeper
  • Hero, featured reel, brand logos, video grid, about/rates, contact CTA
  • Make the next action obvious at every step

Think of your portfolio as a funnel. Each section should pull the brand manager deeper into your work and make the next action obvious. Here is the order that works.

  1. Hero Section. Your name, one-line positioning, and a professional photo. This is not a headshot from your phone camera. Invest in one good photo.
  2. Featured Reel. Your single best video, auto-playing with sound off. This is the piece that makes a brand manager stop scrolling and start watching.
  3. Brand Logos. A clean row of logos from brands you have worked with. Even two or three logos change the perception of your portfolio entirely.
  4. Video Grid. Your top 5 to 8 videos organized by content type or niche. Each video needs a label: brand, format, and platform.
  5. About and Rates. A brief bio focused on what makes you different, your starting rates, turnaround time, and the types of content you specialize in.
  6. Contact CTA. A clear, prominent way to reach you. An email address, a booking link, or both. No contact forms that disappear into a void.
04 — Mistakes

Six portfolio mistakes that cost you brand deals

  • Leading with follower count instead of content quality
  • Using PDFs or Google Drive folders instead of a web page
  • Each mistake is fixable in under an hour

We see these errors in the majority of portfolios that come through our creator intake process. Each one is fixable in under an hour, and fixing them can make a dramatic difference in how brands respond to your work.

MistakeWhy it hurts
Leading with follower countBrands hiring UGC creators care about content quality, not your audience size. Your follower count is irrelevant when the brand is running your video as a paid ad. Lead with your work, not your metrics.
Using a PDF or Google Drive folderPDFs do not play video. Google Drive links require access requests and feel unprofessional. Both break when you update your work. A web-based portfolio stays current and loads instantly on any device.
Showing everything you have ever madeMore is not better. A portfolio with 40 videos tells a brand you cannot curate. Show your 5 to 8 strongest pieces. Every extra video that is not excellent actively weakens the ones that are.
No context on each videoA video without a label is just a clip. Add the brand name, the content type (testimonial, tutorial, unboxing), and the platform it was made for. Context turns a reel into a case study.
Outdated work from 2+ years agoUGC styles change fast. Content that looked fresh in 2024 can feel dated today. If your portfolio includes work from before 2025, remove it unless it has exceptional performance metrics attached.
No mobile optimizationOver 70% of brand managers first open your portfolio on their phone. If your layout breaks on mobile, your text is too small to read, or your videos do not autoplay, you are losing most of your audience.
05 — Niches

What winning portfolios look like in each niche

  • A beauty portfolio and a tech portfolio require fundamentally different approaches
  • Tech UGC is the highest-paying niche at $500–$2,000 per video
  • Niche specialists get picked over generalists

A beauty portfolio and a tech portfolio require fundamentally different approaches. Here is what we look for in each niche when evaluating creators for brand campaigns.

Beauty and Skincare

The strongest beauty portfolios lead with close-up application shots and before-after sequences. Lighting quality matters more here than in any other niche. Show texture, show glow, show the product in use.

  • Key elements: Get-ready-with-me format, close-up product shots, natural lighting, skin texture visibility
  • Pro tip: Include both "raw skin" and "finished look" videos. Brands want to see you can sell transformation, not just application.

Food and Beverage

Food UGC portfolios need to make viewers hungry. That means overhead shots, steam, drizzles, and the satisfying first bite. Audio matters enormously in this niche: the crunch, the sizzle, the pour.

  • Key elements: Overhead angles, ASMR audio, recipe walkthroughs, appetizing presentation
  • Pro tip: Show at least one video with a full recipe flow and one that is purely aesthetic. Brands need both educational and aspirational content.

Tech and SaaS

Tech UGC is the highest-paying niche, with rates reaching $500 to $2,000 per video. Your portfolio needs to demonstrate that you can explain complex products simply and make screen recordings feel dynamic.

  • Key elements: Screen recordings with face overlay, demo walkthroughs, problem-solution structure, clean audio
  • Pro tip: Include at least one video where you explain a technical feature to a non-technical audience. This is the skill brands pay premium rates for.

Fitness and Wellness

Fitness portfolios succeed when they show energy and authenticity. Avoid overly polished gym footage. Brands in this space want real workouts, real sweat, and genuine reactions to products.

  • Key elements: Workout demonstrations, supplement unboxings, lifestyle integration, energetic delivery
  • Pro tip: Show yourself using the product in a real workout, not just holding it and talking. Action shots outsell talking heads in this niche by a wide margin.

06 — Analysis

What we actually look for when reviewing portfolios

  • First impression in 0–3 seconds: clean layout and clear positioning
  • Content quality in 3–15 seconds: hook, lighting, audio
  • Range, consistency, and professionalism in 15–60 seconds

When a creator applies to join OKAD's network of 700+ creators, our team evaluates their portfolio against a specific set of criteria. Here is the internal framework we use.

First Impression (0 to 3 seconds)

Does the portfolio look professional at a glance? We check for a clean layout, a clear name and positioning statement, and whether we can immediately tell what kind of content this person creates. Portfolios that open with a wall of text or a generic bio get closed immediately.

The strongest portfolios we see have a single sentence above the fold that tells us the creator's niche and their angle. Something specific, like "I make testimonial-style ads for DTC skincare brands" rather than "UGC creator with a passion for storytelling."

Content Quality (3 to 15 seconds)

We watch the first five seconds of the top video. If the hook is weak, the lighting is poor, or the audio is unclear, we have already formed an opinion. The opening clip in your portfolio carries disproportionate weight because it is the only piece most reviewers will watch in full.

We specifically look for: natural delivery (not reading from a script with dead eyes), good lighting (natural light works perfectly), clean audio (no background noise, no echo), and a strong opening hook that would stop someone scrolling on their phone.

Range and Consistency (15 to 30 seconds)

We scan the remaining videos to check for range. Can this creator do different formats? Testimonial and unboxing and tutorial? Or is every video the same talking-head setup? Range matters because brands need versatility within a campaign.

At the same time, we look for consistency. Every video should feel like it was made by the same creator in terms of quality level, even if the format varies. A portfolio with one amazing video and four mediocre ones tells us the amazing one might have been a fluke.

Professionalism (30 to 60 seconds)

Finally, we check the practical details. Are rates listed? Is there a clear way to contact this person? Do they mention turnaround time? Is their bio written in clear, grammatically correct English? These details do not seem creative, but they signal whether this person will be reliable to work with on a real campaign with real deadlines.

09 — FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How many videos should a UGC portfolio include?+
Five to eight is the sweet spot. Enough to demonstrate range, few enough that every piece is genuinely strong. We review hundreds of creator portfolios each month at OKAD, and the ones that convert best are tightly curated rather than comprehensive.
Should I include spec work or only paid brand deals?+
Absolutely include spec work, especially if you are early in your career. Just label it honestly. A well-produced spec video for a brand you admire shows initiative and skill. What matters is the quality of the content, not whether someone paid for it.
Do I need a website or can I use a portfolio platform?+
A dedicated portfolio platform built for UGC creators is usually better than a general website builder. Platforms designed for creator portfolios handle video playback, mobile responsiveness, and quick updates far better than a Squarespace site you built yourself.
How often should I update my portfolio?+
After every significant project. At minimum, review it monthly and swap out your weakest piece for your newest strong work. A portfolio that has not been updated in six months signals to brands that you are not actively working.
What if I only have content for one niche?+
That is actually a strength, not a weakness. Brands prefer specialists. A portfolio focused entirely on beauty UGC or tech reviews tells a brand that you understand their audience deeply. Generalist portfolios get overlooked more often than niche ones.
Should I include pricing in my portfolio?+
Yes. At minimum, include a starting rate or a price range. Brands respect transparency, and it filters out inquiries that would never match your budget. Creators who display rates tend to attract more serious leads with faster turnaround on bookings.
How do brands actually discover UGC portfolios?+
Through three main channels: direct outreach from creators who include their portfolio link in pitch emails, creator directories and marketplaces, and organic search. Having a web-based portfolio that search engines can index dramatically increases your inbound opportunities.
