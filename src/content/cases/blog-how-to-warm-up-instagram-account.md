You create a new Instagram account for your brand campaign. You post three Reels on day one. By day two, the account is restricted. By day three, it is gone.

This happens constantly. Instagram's detection systems are built to catch bot accounts, and a new account that immediately starts posting at volume looks exactly like a bot. The solution is not to post less — it is to warm up the account properly before posting anything.

This protocol comes from managing creator accounts across campaigns that produce [60 videos per month](/cases/your-christian-date-broll-content/) and coordinate [22 creators simultaneously](/cases/datemyage-dating-app-scripts/). When you run content at that scale, losing an account to a preventable ban is expensive. The warm-up takes 4 days. Skipping it can cost you weeks.

## Why Instagram Bans New Accounts

Instagram tracks three signals to identify bot accounts:

**1. IP behavior.** Logging in from a different IP address every session — which happens with most consumer VPNs — flags the account as suspicious. Bots rotate IPs. Real users do not.

**2. Activity patterns.** Posting immediately after account creation, following hundreds of accounts in minutes, or liking dozens of posts in rapid succession all trigger automated restrictions. The system looks for inhuman speed and volume.

**3. Device fingerprinting.** Instagram tracks which device created the account and which devices access it. Creating multiple accounts from the same device in a short timeframe raises flags. So does switching between devices during the first week.

Understanding these signals tells you exactly what the warm-up needs to accomplish: make the account look like a real person who browses casually before starting to post.

## Before You Create the Account

Complete this checklist before opening Instagram:

- **Disable geolocation access** for Instagram in your device settings. The app does not need your precise location, and providing it when your VPN says you are in a different country creates a conflict.
- **Install a static IP VPN.** Not a regular VPN — a static one that assigns the same IP address every time you connect. This is critical. Instagram treats each new IP as a potential new location, and frequent location changes trigger reviews.
- **Prepare a Gmail address** for registration. Gmail accounts have high trust scores with Meta's systems. Avoid disposable email services.
- **Limit account creation to one per day.** If you are launching a multi-creator program, stagger account creation across days.

## Creating the Account

You have two options:

**Option A: Through Account Center (preferred).** If you or the creator already has an Instagram account, add a new account through Meta's Account Center. This links the new account to an existing identity, which significantly reduces ban risk. Navigate to Settings → Account Center → Add Account.

**Option B: Fresh registration.** If no existing account is available, register with a Gmail address. Use a real-sounding name, set age to 18+, and skip the phone number prompt if possible. Instagram sometimes requires phone verification — if it does, see the troubleshooting section below.

Once created, do not touch the posting features. Do not upload a Reel. Do not even set up your bio yet — just close the app and let the account exist for a few hours.

## The 4-Day Warm-Up Protocol

For the next four days, your only job is to use Instagram like a normal person who is not yet ready to post. The goal is to train the algorithm on your niche while building a behavior pattern that looks human.

### Daily Sessions (4 per day, 10-15 minutes each)

**Session structure:**
1. Open Instagram with VPN connected
2. Go to the Reels tab
3. Scroll through content in your niche — like, comment, and save posts that match the type of content you will publish
4. Use the Search tab to find niche-specific keywords (e.g., "anti-aging supplements," "skincare routine," "wellness tips")
5. Click into profiles, browse their content, follow 2-3 per session

**What to engage with:** Content that matches your planned posting topics. If your creator account will post about skincare, scroll skincare Reels. If it is about fitness products, watch fitness content. This trains Instagram's recommendation engine to associate your account with that niche — which matters when your content starts going out.

**What to avoid:** Do not follow more than 10-15 accounts per day during warm-up. Do not like more than 30-40 posts per day. Do not comment on more than 10-15 posts. These are rough ceilings — staying below them keeps your activity within normal-user range.

### Creator Cross-Engagement

If you are running multiple creator accounts for the same campaign:

- Exchange account handles between 3-5 creators
- Follow each other's accounts
- During scrolling sessions, share Reels to each other via DM
- This creates the social graph signals that Instagram uses to verify real users — genuine accounts share content with friends

This step is particularly valuable when managing [large creator networks](/cases/canvas-tech-ugc-without-new-shoots/). The cross-engagement pattern helps new accounts build trust faster.

### Profile Setup (Day 2-3)

Midway through the warm-up, set up your profile:

- Add a profile photo (personal photo or brand-relevant image)
- Write a bio that matches your niche ("Reviewing the best anti-aging products" or "Wellness tips for real life")
- Prepare your first 3-4 Reels in drafts — do not publish them yet

## Post-Warmup Posting Schedule

After 4 days of engagement-only activity, start posting with a gradual ramp:

| Period | Posts per Day | Engagement Sessions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1-4 (warm-up) | 0 | 4 × 10-15 min | Scroll, like, comment, follow |
| Days 5-8 | 1 | 4 × 10 min | 1 session immediately after posting |
| Days 9-14 | 2 | 4 × 10 min | 2 sessions after each post |
| Days 15-19 | 3 | 4 × 10 min | Continue engagement after posting |
| Day 20+ | 4 (full volume) | As needed | Account is established |

**Critical rule:** Maintain at least 1 hour between posts. Posting two Reels back to back looks automated.

**Continue engagement sessions after posting.** When you publish a Reel, stay on the platform for 10-15 minutes afterward — scroll, like, reply to any comments on your post. This tells Instagram that posting is part of your normal usage pattern, not the only thing you do.

## Engagement Multipliers

Once you are posting regularly, add these tactics to accelerate growth:

### Keyword-Triggered Giveaways

On 1 out of every 3 posts, offer a free resource in exchange for a comment. Add text during editing:

*"Comment GLOW and I will send you my anti-aging supplement checklist."*

The keyword should match your post's topic. A post about sleep supplements uses "SLEEP." A post about skincare uses "GLOW." This drives comments, which signals engagement to the algorithm.

Rules for giveaway execution:
- Do not create the guide until at least 7 people request it
- Send the guide link via DM, then reply to their comment confirming delivery
- Limit to 30 DMs per day to avoid triggering spam filters
- For posts exceeding 10K views with ongoing requests, put the link in your bio instead

### Description Call-to-Action

On another 1 of every 3 posts, direct viewers to open the description for additional content. Examples:

- "The full recipe is in the description"
- "Read how I dealt with this in the description below"

This trains your audience to engage with the description field, which Instagram counts as a meaningful interaction.

## VPN Rules

Your VPN discipline determines whether the account survives:

- **Always connect VPN before opening Instagram.** Never open the app without it.
- **Never change VPN servers** during the first 30 days. Same server, same IP, every time.
- **If you forget and open Instagram without VPN**, close the app immediately. Reconnect VPN. Wait 5 minutes. Reopen. One slip is usually fine — repeated slips trigger reviews.
- **Use the same device.** Do not access the account from your phone on Monday and your laptop on Tuesday during the warm-up period. After 30 days, you can add devices gradually.

## Troubleshooting

**Cannot create an account:** Return to the pre-setup checklist. Try the Account Center method first. If that fails, try fresh registration with Gmail. If Gmail registration fails, try from a different device. If all devices fail, purchase a dedicated static IP address — contact your VPN provider or manager about covering this cost.

**Phone number requested:** Try the Account Center method to bypass. If unavailable, purchase a US virtual number through Google Voice. As a last resort, ask someone with a US number to help with verification.

**Account restricted after posting:** You posted too fast. Stop posting for 48-72 hours. Continue engagement sessions only. Resume with 1 post per day and rebuild gradually.

**Account banned:** If the ban is permanent and appeals fail, start fresh on a different device with a new email. Do not reuse the same email, phone number, or device that was associated with the banned account.

**Nothing works from your phone:** Try an anti-detect browser like [Dolphin Anty](https://dolphin-anty.com/) on a laptop. This masks your device fingerprint. The downside: you can only access the account through that browser going forward. Use this as a last resort after exhausting all other options.

## Cross-Platform Warm-Up Differences

If you are running creator accounts across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube simultaneously, the warm-up protocols differ:

| Platform | Warm-Up Duration | First Post Timing | Ramp to Full Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 4 days | Day 5 | Day 20 (gradual increase) |
| TikTok | 1-2 days | Day 2-3 | Day 3 (post all content immediately) |
| YouTube Shorts | 4 days | Day 5 | Day 5 (post all 4 daily from start) |

Instagram is the strictest platform for new accounts. TikTok is more forgiving — a shorter warm-up works, and you can post at full volume almost immediately. YouTube Shorts falls in between: warm up like Instagram, but post at full volume once you start.

The key difference: Instagram penalizes rapid posting on new accounts. TikTok and YouTube penalize inconsistent posting. Adjust your strategy per platform rather than applying one protocol everywhere.

## What This Looks Like at Scale

When you manage 10, 20, or 50 creator accounts for a brand campaign, the warm-up protocol becomes an operational process. Each new creator gets the same checklist. Each account follows the same 4-day warm-up. Each ramp-up follows the same posting schedule.

A lost account means lost momentum, lost audience data, and wasted warm-up time.
