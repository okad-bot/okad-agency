Most UGC creators struggle to land brand deals not because their content is bad, but because their pitches are. Brands receive dozens of creator pitches daily. The ones that get responses share specific characteristics. The ones that get ignored share different ones.

This guide breaks down how to write brand pitches that actually get read, get responses, and lead to paid work.

## Why Most Brand Pitches Fail

Before covering what works, here is what does not:

**Generic openers.** "Hi, I love your brand and would love to collaborate!" — This says nothing. The brand knows you did not research them because the same sentence applies to any company.

**Long introductions.** Three paragraphs about your journey as a creator before mentioning what you can do for them. The brand does not care about your story yet — they care about their problem.

**No portfolio link.** Describing your content style in words instead of showing it. Brands need to see your work immediately. If they have to search for it, they will not.

**Vague pricing.** "Let me know your budget" puts the work on the brand. Having a clear rate (even if negotiable) signals professionalism.

**Mass-sent templates.** Brands can tell. If your pitch could apply to any brand without changing a word, it will not work for any brand.

## The Pitch Structure That Works

A brand pitch should be 100–150 words. That is it. Four elements, each doing specific work:

### 1. Personalized Hook (1–2 sentences)

Reference something specific about the brand's recent content, a product launch, or their current marketing:

- "I noticed your recent TikTok series featuring customer transformations — the before/afters are strong but the hooks could be tighter."
- "Your Instagram Reels are consistently good, but I noticed you haven't tested the talking-head format that's working well in [their niche] right now."
- "I've been using [product] for three months and already have footage — would love to turn it into content for your ads."

This proves you actually looked at their brand and have something specific to offer.

### 2. Your Relevant Experience (1–2 sentences)

Not your full bio. Just the parts that matter to this brand:

- "I create [canvas UGC](/blog/blog-what-is-canvas-ugc/) for DTC beauty brands — my recent work for [similar brand] achieved a 52% hook rate and ran for 3 weeks before fatigue."
- "I specialize in tech product demos. I've created 40+ canvas UGC assets for SaaS brands in the last 6 months."

Mention metrics if you have them. Brands care about results, not follower counts. Look at case studies like OKAD's [high-ROAS UGC campaign](/cases/high-roas-ugc-ads/) to understand the kind of results brands track — then reference similar metrics in your pitch.

### 3. Portfolio Link (1 line)

One link to 2–3 relevant samples. Not your entire portfolio — just the work most relevant to this brand:

- "Here are two recent pieces in your niche: [link]"
- "Portfolio with beauty UGC samples: [link]"

Make it easy. One click, they see your best relevant work.

### 4. Clear Call to Action (1 sentence)

Tell them exactly what to do next:

- "Would a 15-minute call this week work to discuss a test project?"
- "Happy to create a free test video with your product — just send it over."
- "My rate for 3 canvas UGC videos is $XXX. Want me to send a detailed brief?"

## How to Find the Right Person to Pitch

Pitching the wrong person wastes everyone's time:

- **Small DTC brands (1–20 employees):** Pitch the founder or whoever runs social media. Check LinkedIn and the company's About page.
- **Mid-size brands (20–200 employees):** Pitch the Marketing Manager, Social Media Manager, or Creative Director. Find them on LinkedIn.
- **Large brands:** Pitch the agency, not the brand directly. Identify which agency handles their paid media and reach out to account managers or creative leads.
- **Agencies:** They manage multiple brands and need constant creator supply. One agency relationship can lead to 5–10 brand accounts.

## Follow-Up Strategy

One pitch is rarely enough. Most deals close on the follow-up:

- **Day 1:** Send initial pitch
- **Day 4–5:** Follow up if no response. Keep it short: "Hi [Name], following up on my note about UGC for [Brand]. Happy to answer any questions — [portfolio link]."
- **Day 10–12:** Final follow up. "Last note on this — if UGC isn't a priority right now, no worries. I'll check back in a few months."

Three touches maximum. If no response after three, move on. The brand might come back later when they are ready.

## Tracking Your Pitches

Keep a simple spreadsheet:

| Brand | Contact | Platform | Date Sent | Follow-Up | Response | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand X | @name | IG DM | Oct 1 | Oct 5 | Interested | Call scheduled |
| Brand Y | name@co | Email | Oct 1 | Oct 5 | No reply | Follow up #2 |

This prevents double-pitching, tracks your response rate, and shows which channels and pitch angles work best.

## Pitch Examples by Niche

Generic pitches fail because they lack specificity. Here are concrete pitch openers tailored to high-demand niches:

### Beauty / Skincare

"Hi [Name], I noticed [Brand] is running TikTok ads with product demos but hasn't tested the 'get ready with me' format that's converting well for other skincare DTC brands right now. I create canvas UGC for beauty brands and recently produced a set of 6 GRWM-style assets that averaged a 47% hook rate. Here are two samples in your product category: [link]."

### Tech / SaaS

"Hi [Name], I saw your latest LinkedIn campaign for [product feature] — the messaging is solid but the format could work harder. Screen-recording UGC with a talking-head walkthrough is outperforming static demos in SaaS ads right now. I specialize in [tech UGC](/blog/blog-tech-ugc-guide/) and have produced 30+ product demo videos for B2B tools. Relevant samples: [link]."

### Food / Beverage

"Hi [Name], your Instagram content looks great but I noticed you're not running UGC-style ads on Meta. Recipe-format and first-taste-reaction videos are getting strong results for food brands this quarter. I create that style of content and have recent work for a similar product: [link]."

### Health / Supplements

"Hi [Name], I've been using [product] for two months and have authentic footage I'd love to turn into ad content. Supplement brands running story-based UGC are seeing 2–3x better engagement than studio-shot ads. Portfolio with health niche work: [link]."

The pattern is the same in every niche: specific observation about their current content, a gap you can fill, proof you can fill it.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Response Rates

Even experienced creators sabotage their pitches with avoidable errors. Here are the most damaging ones, ranked by impact:

**1. Pitching the wrong person.** Emailing info@brand.com or DMing the brand's social account often means your pitch never reaches the decision-maker. Spend 2 minutes on LinkedIn finding the marketing manager, creative director, or founder. A pitch to the right person at a 10% response rate beats a pitch to a generic inbox at 0.5%.

**2. Leading with yourself instead of their problem.** "I'm a UGC creator with 3 years of experience and a passion for storytelling" — the brand stopped reading after five words. Lead with an observation about their content or marketing gap. Make them the subject, not you.

**3. Sending your entire portfolio.** One link with 2–3 relevant samples. Not your website, not a Google Drive with 50 videos. The more you send, the less they watch. Curate ruthlessly for relevance.

**4. No pricing signal.** Brands receive pitches from creators charging $50 and creators charging $500. They want to know the range before engaging. Include a ballpark ("My rate for 3 videos is $XXX") so they can self-qualify. Our [UGC rates and pricing guide](/blog/blog-ugc-creator-rates-pricing/) helps you set competitive rates.

**5. Giving up after one message.** Over 60% of responses come from follow-ups, not initial pitches. The follow-up sequence above exists for a reason — use it.

**6. Pitching brands that do not use UGC.** Check whether the brand runs UGC-style ads before pitching. Look at their TikTok ad library, Meta Ad Library, or Instagram Reels. If they only run polished studio content, they may not be in the market for creator content yet. Brands already investing in [video ads for DTC](/blog/blog-dtc-video-ads-guide/) are far warmer leads because they understand the format.

## Advanced Follow-Up Techniques

The basic follow-up timeline above covers the sequence. Here are techniques that increase conversion on each touchpoint:

**Add value on follow-up.** Do not just ask "did you see my email." Instead: "Saw you just launched [new product] — I sketched out a quick concept for a UGC ad angle: [one sentence concept]. Happy to chat if it resonates."

**Switch channels on follow-up #2.** If you emailed first, follow up via LinkedIn DM. If you DMed on Instagram, find their email. Multi-channel touches show initiative without being pushy.

**Time your follow-ups around brand activity.** Follow up when the brand posts a product launch, runs a sale, or publishes new content. Tying your follow-up to their activity shows you are paying attention and makes the conversation timely.

**Track response patterns to optimize timing.** Some niches respond better on Tuesday mornings. Others on Thursday afternoons. After 50+ pitches, review your spreadsheet data to identify which days and times yield the highest response rates.
