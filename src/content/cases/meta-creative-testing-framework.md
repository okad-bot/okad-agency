Creative is the biggest lever left in Meta ads. Targeting is mostly automated now, so the ad itself decides who sees it and who buys. That makes testing creatives the main job, not a side task.

This is the process I use with clients, the same one behind the AI app case where 160+ videos brought cost per customer 35% below the account average.

## Step 1. Test ideas first, not colors

The biggest wins come from a different angle, not a different button. Test in this order:

1. **Angle.** Why someone buys: saves time, fixes a pain, looks better, beats a competitor.
2. **Format.** Talking head, demo, testimonial, before and after, screen recording, B-roll with text.
3. **Hook.** The first 1 to 3 seconds. Same body, different opening.
4. **Details.** Captions, music, CTA, length.

Most accounts I audit spend their testing on step 4 and never test step 1.

## Step 2. Build ads from modules

Shoot or generate one body and write 3 to 5 hooks for it. 13 clips can become 72 ads this way. [How modular production works](/blog/blog-high-volume-ugc-production).

## Step 3. Decide how many ads to test

For most accounts spending $3,000 to $30,000 a month, a healthy rhythm is 10 to 20 new ads every one to two weeks. Fewer and you don't find winners; more and each ad gets too little spend to read. The full math is in [how many ad variations you actually need](/blog/blog-ad-creative-testing-how-many-variations).

## Step 4. Set up the test

- One testing campaign, separate from scaling campaigns.
- One ad set per concept, 3 to 6 ads inside (the hook versions).
- Optimize for the event you actually want (purchase, trial, lead).
- Give each ad enough spend to judge: roughly 1 to 2 times your target cost per purchase before you decide.

## Step 5. Read the right numbers

| Metric | What it tells you | Rough benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Hook rate (3-sec views / impressions) | Does the opening stop the scroll | 25 to 35% is solid |
| Hold rate (ThruPlays / 3-sec views) | Does the body keep attention | depends on length |
| CTR (link) | Does the ad make people want more | around 1% and up |
| Cost per purchase / lead | Does it sell | your target CPA |

If hook rate is low, change the hook. If hook rate is fine but CTR is low, the body or offer is the problem. More in [hook rate explained](/blog/blog-hook-rate-explained).

## Step 6. Kill, fix or scale

- **Kill:** spent 2x target CPA with no conversions, or hook rate far below the rest.
- **Fix:** good hook rate, weak CTR. Keep the hook, rewrite the body.
- **Scale:** at or under target CPA. Move it to the scaling campaign and make 3 to 5 new hooks for the same body.

Scaling without breaking ROAS is its own topic: [how to scale spend](/blog/blog-how-to-scale-ad-campaigns-roas).

## Step 7. Refresh before fatigue

Winners usually fade in 2 to 3 weeks. Plan the next batch while the current one still works. Warning signs and fixes: [why good ads stop working](/blog/blog-ad-fatigue-prevention).
