The debate between UGC and stock video usually gets framed as a style preference. It is not. It comes down to one question: what makes a person stop scrolling and watch.

Both formats have a place in a media plan, but they are not interchangeable, and treating them as equal options -- pick whichever is cheaper or faster to produce -- leads to wasted ad spend. This post uses performance data, not opinion, to compare where each format wins and where it falls short.

## The Authenticity Gap

Users decide whether to keep watching within the first 0.5 to 1 second of a video. On social platforms, this decision happens almost entirely on pattern recognition, not content.

Stock footage has a recognizable look: even lighting, generic models, staged scenarios, smooth camera movement. Viewers have seen thousands of ads built from the same footage libraries, so the brain tags it as "advertisement" almost instantly. That tag triggers the scroll reflex before the message even registers.

UGC does not carry those visual cues. Handheld framing, a real person talking into the camera, imperfect lighting -- these match the organic content already in the feed. The ad does not look like an ad, so it survives past the first second, which is the only point where most ads fail.

This is not an aesthetic argument. It is a measurable one, and the data below shows how much it affects performance.

## Performance Data: UGC vs Stock Video

The table below compares the two formats across the metrics that matter most for paid social and direct response campaigns.

| Metric | UGC | Stock Video |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate (CTR) | 2-3x higher | Baseline |
| Cost per acquisition (CPA) | 30-50% lower | Baseline |
| Hook rate / thumb-stop rate | 40-60% higher | Baseline |
| Engagement rate (likes, comments, shares) | 2-4x higher | Baseline |
| Creative fatigue lifespan | 7-14 days | 3-7 days |
| Cost per asset | $100-$500 | $20-$500 (clip) + editing |
| Production time | 5-10 days | 1-3 days |

A few of these are worth unpacking:

- **Hook rate** measures how many viewers watch past the first few seconds. Because UGC does not read as an ad, more viewers stay past the point where stock footage loses them. We cover the mechanics of this in [hook rate explained](/blog/blog-hook-rate-explained/).
- **Creative fatigue lifespan** is how long an ad keeps performing before audiences tune it out. Stock footage blends into the general noise of generic advertising faster, so it fatigues sooner even with less total exposure.
- **Production time** favors stock video, since licensing a clip takes minutes compared to briefing, filming, and editing a UGC creator. This is the one category where stock has a clear structural advantage.

## Where UGC Wins

UGC performs best in contexts where the viewer is deciding, in real time, whether to trust a product or brand.

- **Social media ads** on TikTok, Meta, and YouTube Shorts, where the native format of the platform is creator content, not polished commercials.
- **Direct response campaigns** optimized for clicks, sign-ups, or purchases, where every extra second of attention compounds into conversions.
- **Product testimonials**, where a real person describing a real experience carries more weight than a voiceover over b-roll.
- **Retargeting**, where the viewer already knows the brand and responds better to a relatable person than a repeat of the same polished spot.

## Where Stock Video Wins

Stock video is not obsolete, and dismissing it outright is its own kind of bias. It wins in contexts where polish, not authenticity, is the point.

- **Brand awareness campaigns**, where the goal is visual consistency and a premium look rather than an immediate click.
- **B2B marketing**, where buyers often expect a more corporate tone and UGC can read as unprofessional in that context.
- **Corporate video and investor-facing content**, where production value signals stability and credibility.
- **Website hero sections**, where a smooth, high-production clip sets the tone for a brand's visual identity on first load.
- **Scenarios requiring locations, settings, or shots** that would be impractical or expensive to film with a UGC creator -- aerial shots, specific international locations, or large-scale environments.

In these cases, the audience is not being asked to trust a stranger's recommendation. They are evaluating a brand's overall positioning, and production polish does real work there.

## The Hybrid Approach

The brands getting the most out of their creative budget are not choosing one format exclusively. They are using both, deliberately, in the parts of the funnel where each one performs.

A common structure uses a UGC hook in the first one to three seconds to stop the scroll, then transitions into stock or product b-roll to demonstrate the product in more polished detail. This captures the attention benefit of authenticity while still giving the ad room to show production quality where it matters.

At the channel level, the same logic applies: UGC for social ads where the format needs to blend into the feed, and stock or in-house footage for the website, sales decks, and brand-level assets where consistency and polish carry more weight. Authenticity is also not limited to video -- the same principle shows up in how reviews and testimonials are used across a funnel, covered in [social proof in ads](/blog/blog-social-proof-ads-guide/).

## Cost Analysis: True Cost Per Conversion

Comparing cost per asset is the wrong comparison. The number that actually affects a budget is cost per conversion.

Take a simple example. A $300 UGC video that drives a 2 percent CTR and converts at 5 percent of clicks is generating meaningfully more purchases per thousand impressions than a $50 stock clip converting at 0.5 percent CTR with the same conversion rate on clicks. Run both through $1,000 in media spend at a $1 CPM, and the UGC asset can produce four times the conversions, even though the stock clip cost six times less to produce.

This is why UGC's lower CPA in the data table above matters more than its higher per-asset price. The asset cost is a rounding error next to the media budget it unlocks or wastes. ## Bottom Line

For paid social ads optimized for conversions, UGC outperforms stock video by a wide margin on CTR, CPA, hook rate, and creative lifespan. The format matches what viewers already expect from the platforms where these ads run, and that alone accounts for most of the performance gap.

Stock video still has a real place in brand-level content: website hero sections, corporate presentations, B2B contexts, and any scenario requiring production value or locations that are impractical to film with creators.

Most performance marketers should invest primarily in UGC for anything running through paid social, and treat stock footage as supplementary b-roll rather than the primary creative. The brands that build both into their workflow, rather than picking one, get the authenticity that drives clicks and the polish that supports the brand around it.
