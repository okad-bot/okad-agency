Most brands evaluate [UGC agencies](/blog/blog-what-is-ugc-agency/) by looking at their portfolio. That is only one dimension. Here are 8 questions that reveal whether an agency can actually deliver results for your specific situation.

## 1. How Large Is Your Creator Network?

The size and diversity of a creator network directly affects the quality and variety of content you receive. An agency with a small roster will recycle the same faces across clients, making your ads look generic — and risking audience overlap if competitors use the same agency.

What to look for:

- **Minimum 50 vetted creators** across multiple demographics, niches, and languages
- A range of ages, ethnicities, and communication styles that match your target audience
- Creators experienced with your content format — talking head, product demo, lifestyle, voiceover

**Red flag:** the agency uses the same 5–10 creators for every client. Ask to see their full roster breakdown by demographic and content type. At OKAD, we maintain a network of 600+ creators specifically to avoid this problem — every brand gets matched with faces and voices that fit their audience.

### What "Vetted" Actually Means

Not every creator on a roster is production-ready. Ask what vetting looks like: do they review past work, run test shoots, check delivery reliability? A large but unvetted network creates more problems than a smaller curated one.

## 2. Do You Have Experience in My Vertical?

A UGC agency that produces beauty content will approach a fintech campaign very differently — and may lack the compliance knowledge to do it safely. Vertical experience affects scripting, creator selection, legal review, and platform strategy.

Key considerations:

- **Regulated industries** (finance, health, supplements, alcohol) require specific disclaimer language and claim restrictions. An inexperienced agency risks ad rejections or worse.
- **B2B vs. B2C** requires different creator profiles, tones, and formats. A B2B SaaS testimonial looks nothing like a skincare unboxing.
- **Ask for 2–3 examples** from your specific niche. Not adjacent categories — your actual vertical.

If the agency has no relevant experience but is otherwise strong, that is not automatically a dealbreaker. But they should be honest about the learning curve and price accordingly.

## 3. What Is Your Standard Turnaround Time?

Turnaround affects your ability to test and iterate. Slow delivery means stale ads, missed seasonal windows, and delayed learnings.

| Scenario | Typical turnaround | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard batch (5–10 videos) | 5–7 business days | From approved brief to final delivery |
| Rush delivery | 2–3 business days | Expect 25–50% premium |
| Monthly retainer clients | 3–5 business days | Creators are pre-briefed on brand |
| Revisions | 1–2 business days per round | Confirm how many rounds are included |

Ask how they handle revision cycles. Some agencies include two rounds in their base price. Others charge per revision. Knowing this upfront prevents scope disputes later.

Also ask what happens when a creator misses a deadline. A good agency has backup creators assigned and a process for re-shooting without resetting the timeline.

## 4. How Transparent Is Your Pricing?

UGC pricing varies wildly, and the cheapest quote is rarely the best deal. The goal is to understand exactly what you are paying for before production starts.

Common pricing models:

- **Per-video:** $150–$500+ per finished video, depending on complexity and creator tier
- **Monthly retainer:** fixed deliverable count at a predictable cost, usually with priority scheduling
- **Project-based:** one-time campaigns with a defined scope and timeline

What should be included in the base price:

- Scripting and creative direction
- Creator sourcing and management
- Filming and basic editing (cuts, captions, music)
- One to two revision rounds

Common add-ons to watch for:

- Rush fees
- Additional revision rounds beyond the included limit
- Music licensing for commercial tracks
- Extended usage rights (beyond the initial term)
- [Whitelisting](/blog/blog-ugc-whitelisting-guide/) setup and management

For detailed cost benchmarks, see our [pricing page](/pricing/). A transparent agency will provide a line-item breakdown rather than a single lump sum.

## 5. Do You Handle Whitelisting?

Whitelisting — running paid ads from a creator's personal account instead of your brand account — consistently outperforms brand-account ads. The data is clear:

- **50% lower CPA** compared to brand-account ads
- **2.4x higher CTR** due to the native, personal feel
- Better audience trust and engagement signals

If the agency already manages creator relationships, they should also handle whitelisting logistics: permissions, contracts, access codes, and ongoing account management. Splitting this responsibility between your team and the agency creates friction and delays.

Key questions:

- Do you manage whitelisting permissions and contracts directly with creators?
- How many of your creators are approved for whitelisting?
- What platforms do you support (Meta, TikTok, both)?
- How long does whitelisting access last per campaign?

For a deeper breakdown, read our [UGC whitelisting guide](/blog/blog-ugc-whitelisting-guide/).

## 6. How Do You Approach Creative Testing?

Production without testing is only half the job. An agency that delivers videos but does not help you figure out which ones work — and why — leaves the most valuable part of the process to you.

Questions to ask:

- **How many variations per concept?** You want at least 3–5 hook variations per winning body. See our guide on [how many ad variations to test](/blog/blog-ad-creative-testing-how-many-variations/) for the full framework.
- **What variables do they test?** Hooks, CTAs, formats, creators, thumbnails — each should be tested in isolation.
- **How do test results feed back into production?** The best agencies use performance data from the current batch to inform the next creative brief. Winners get iterated. Losers get analyzed for what went wrong.

A clear testing process is the difference between an agency that produces content and one that produces results. If the agency cannot explain their testing methodology in concrete terms, that is a warning sign.

## 7. Who Owns the Content Rights?

Content rights determine where, how long, and in what context you can use the videos you paid for. Get this in writing before production starts — not after.

Key terms to clarify:

- **Full buyout vs. time-limited license.** Full buyout means you own the content indefinitely. Time-limited licenses (90 days, 6 months, 1 year) are cheaper but require renewal or re-negotiation.
- **Platform restrictions.** Some contracts limit usage to specific platforms. If you plan to run the same creative on Meta, TikTok, YouTube, and your website, confirm that all channels are covered.
- **Whitelisting access duration.** Even if you own the content, whitelisting access through a creator's account may have a separate expiration.
- **Derivative works.** Can you re-edit, remix, or use clips from the videos in other content? Some contracts restrict modifications.

If you need help structuring the brief that covers these requirements upfront, our guide on [how to brief a creative agency](/blog/blog-how-to-brief-a-creative-agency/) includes a rights section template.

## 8. What Reporting Do You Provide?

Reporting separates production shops from strategic partners. The depth of reporting an agency provides signals how invested they are in your outcomes versus just delivering files.

Three tiers of reporting:

- **Minimum (delivery tracking):** production status, revision tracking, timeline adherence, asset delivery log. This is table stakes.
- **Better (performance data):** CTR, CPA, ROAS, and hook rate on live ads. The agency pulls data from your ad accounts or receives shared access.
- **Best (strategic insights):** analysis of what is working and why, recommendations for the next batch, trend identification across multiple brands in your vertical.

Ask for a sample report before signing. If the agency only provides a Google Drive folder with video files and no context, you are buying production, not strategy.

## Making the Decision

Weight these eight questions based on your situation. The right priorities depend on your ad spend level and team capacity.

- **Under $5K/month in ad spend:** pricing transparency and turnaround time matter most. You need affordable content delivered fast enough to keep your campaigns fresh.
- **$5K–$10K/month:** creator network diversity and content rights become important. You are running enough volume that audience fatigue from repeated faces is a real risk.
- **$10K+/month:** creative testing methodology and reporting are the differentiators. At this budget, the agency that helps you find winners faster will pay for itself in lower CPA.

No agency will score perfectly on all eight dimensions. The goal is to identify which factors are non-negotiable for your business and which ones you can work around. Ask the questions, compare the answers, and make the decision based on data — not portfolio aesthetics alone.
