Every photo book app promises the same thing: pick a template, drag in your photos, done. In practice, you spend an evening cropping, rearranging, deleting duplicates, and wondering if that sunset belongs on page 12 or page 14. Most books stall here, half-finished in a Shutterfly account.

[ReMeet](https://remeet.cc/) takes a different approach. You send your photos. A designer picks the best ones, lays out every page, and sends you the first draft in 48 hours. No editor, no templates, no drag-and-drop. The book looks like a designer made it because one did.

This article explains how the service works, what it costs, and why we built it.

## The Problem with Template-Based Photo Books

Template photo book tools — Shutterfly, Mixbook, Chatbooks, Apple Photos, Google Photos — are built around a self-service editor. You upload your photos, pick a layout, and arrange everything yourself.

For people who enjoy that process, it works. For everyone else, it creates a task that sits on the to-do list for months. The common failure points:

- **Photo selection.** You have 2,000 photos from a trip. The book needs 50. Choosing which 50 is the hardest part, and no template helps with that.
- **Layout decisions.** Which photo is a full-page spread? Which three go on the same page? Templates give you grids, but the editorial judgment — what goes where and why — is still on you.
- **Time.** Even experienced users spend 4 to 8 hours on a 30-page book. Most people don't have that time, so the book never gets made.

The result: millions of photo libraries full of trips, birthdays, and milestones that never become anything tangible. The photos exist, but they live on a phone screen.

## How ReMeet Works

ReMeet replaces the editor with a person. Here is the actual process:

**Step 1 — Order.** Choose a small book (up to 30 pages, $29) or a big book (up to 100 pages, $79). Both are PDF-first — you get a digital book you can keep, share, or print.

**Step 2 — Send your photos.** Share a link to your album on iCloud, Google Photos, or Google Drive. Or upload a zip. Up to 300 photos for a standard order. If you have thousands and don't want to pick, add the selection add-on ($25) and the designer will sort through up to 3,000.

**Step 3 — The designer works.** A human designer — not a template engine — reviews your photos, selects the strongest shots, groups them into a narrative, and designs every page. Typography, spacing, photo sizing, and page flow are all done by hand.

**Step 4 — First draft in 48 hours.** You receive a PDF preview by email. Two rounds of edits are included — reply with what you want changed.

**Step 5 — Approve and download.** Once you are happy, you get the final PDF. That is the complete product. If you want a physical copy, hardcover printing starts at $69 with US and Canada shipping included.

The key difference: you never open an editor. You never drag a photo into a slot. The designer makes the decisions that make the book look good — the same decisions that cause most DIY books to stall.

## What the Design Actually Looks Like

This is not a glorified auto-fill. Each book is designed from scratch based on the photos you send. The designer considers:

- **Pacing.** A travel book starts with arrival and builds through the trip. A baby book follows growth. The page order tells a story.
- **Photo hierarchy.** The best shot from a set gets a full page. Supporting shots share a spread. Duplicates and blurry photos are removed.
- **White space.** Not every inch is filled. Breathing room between photos makes each one stand out.
- **Typography.** Titles, dates, and captions are set in clean type that fits the mood of the photos.

The result is closer to a magazine layout than a template grid. That is the point.

## ReMeet vs. Template Photo Book Services

The comparison is not "which editor is better" because ReMeet has no editor. The comparison is between doing the work yourself and having a designer do it.

**Shutterfly, Mixbook, Apple Photos, Google Photos:** You pick the photos, choose the template, arrange the layout, adjust the crops. The tool is the product — you are the designer. Pricing is per printed book; the design tool is free because you are the labor.

**ReMeet:** You send photos. A designer does the rest. You review, request changes, and approve. Pricing is for the design; printing is separate and optional.

If you enjoy the creative process of building a photo book and have the time, a template tool is the right choice. If you want the book to exist but don't want to spend the hours, ReMeet is the alternative.
