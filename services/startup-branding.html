Our Process

Four focused phases over three weeks. We designed this workflow specifically for startups: fast enough to keep up with your launch timeline, thorough enough to produce a brand system that lasts through multiple growth stages.

Step 01

Discovery & Positioning

We start with a 60-minute strategy session to understand your product, your market, your competitors, and your vision. We audit your competitive landscape to identify visual white space, meaning the colors, styles, and approaches your competitors are not using. We define your brand positioning: what you stand for, who you serve, and how you want to be perceived. This phase prevents the most common startup branding mistake, which is building a visual identity that looks like everyone else in your category. You walk away with a positioning brief that guides every design decision that follows.

Step 02

Concept Exploration

Based on the positioning brief, we develop two to three distinct visual directions. Each concept includes a preliminary logo, color palette, and typography pairing presented in context on mockups of your actual touchpoints: website header, app icon, business card, social media profile. You see how each direction feels in the real world, not just as abstract design exercises. We present these in a live review session where we walk through the strategic reasoning behind each option, so you can make an informed decision rather than picking based on personal taste alone.

Step 03

Design & Refinement

Once you select a direction, we refine every element to production quality. The logo gets fine-tuned for optical balance at all sizes. The color system is expanded with full tint and shade ranges. Typography is specified with exact sizing scales for web headings, body text, and UI components. We apply the refined identity to key brand touchpoints and present a second review round. Two rounds of revisions are included, though most clients need only one because the strategic groundwork in earlier phases means we are already aligned on direction and details.

Step 04

Delivery & Brand Playbook

You receive the complete brand package: all logo variants in SVG, PNG, and PDF formats, the full color and typography specifications, the brand guidelines document, social media templates, and the pitch deck template. Everything is organized in a shared drive with clear folder structure and naming conventions. We also schedule a 30-minute handoff call where we walk your team through the guidelines and answer any questions about implementation. If you are working with external developers or designers, we are available for a follow-up call to brief them directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the branding process take?
Our standard timeline is three weeks from kickoff to final delivery. Week one covers discovery and positioning. Week two is concept exploration and direction selection. Week three is design refinement, production of all deliverables, and the handoff call. If you have a tight launch deadline, we offer an accelerated two-week track for an additional fee. We will confirm feasibility during the initial strategy session based on project scope.
How much does startup branding cost?
Our startup branding packages range from $3,500 to $8,500 depending on the scope of deliverables and complexity. The base package at $3,500 includes logo, color system, typography, and a concise brand guidelines document. The full package at $8,500 adds the social media kit, pitch deck template, extended guidelines with tone of voice, and additional application mockups. We scope every project individually during the strategy call so you only pay for what you actually need.
What if I already have a logo but need everything else?
That is a common starting point. Many startups have a logo created early on but lack a cohesive brand system around it. We can work with your existing logo and build the color system, typography, guidelines, and templates around it. If the existing logo needs refinement to work well within the new system, we will recommend specific adjustments during the discovery phase. This approach typically falls at the lower end of our pricing range since we are building the system rather than creating the core mark from scratch.
How many revisions are included?
Two full rounds of revisions are included in every package. A revision round covers changes to any deliverable: logo adjustments, color modifications, typography swaps, layout changes to templates, or updates to the guidelines document. In practice, most clients need only one revision round because our discovery and concept exploration phases ensure we are aligned on direction before we invest in production-quality design. If additional revisions are needed beyond the included rounds, we accommodate them at a transparent hourly rate.
Can you help with naming our startup?
Naming is not included in our standard branding packages, but we offer it as an add-on service. The naming process involves competitive analysis, linguistic screening, domain availability checks, and trademark preliminary searches. We typically present five to eight name candidates with rationale for each. If you need naming, let us know during the strategy call and we will scope it as a separate phase that runs before the visual identity work begins. Naming adds approximately one week to the overall timeline.
What files and formats do I receive?
You receive all logo variants in SVG (vector, infinitely scalable), PNG (transparent background, multiple sizes), and PDF (print-ready) formats. The brand guidelines are delivered as a PDF document and an editable Figma file. Social media templates come in Figma format with Canva-compatible exports. The pitch deck template is delivered in Google Slides and Figma. Color specifications include HEX, RGB, and HSL values. Everything is organized in a shared Google Drive folder with clear naming conventions. You own all files with full commercial usage rights.
What do the brand guidelines actually cover?
The guidelines document covers logo usage rules (minimum size, clear space, placement on different backgrounds, what not to do), the complete color system with primary, secondary, and accent palettes plus accessible pairings, typography hierarchy with specified sizes and weights for headings, body text, and UI elements, and visual examples showing the brand applied to common touchpoints. The full package also includes tone of voice direction with writing samples, photography and illustration style guidance, and iconography recommendations. It is a practical reference that any designer or developer can follow without needing to ask you questions.
