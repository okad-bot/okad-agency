Foot care · Retail launch at Walmart

Two creams. A Walmart launch. Not one new shoot.

ZenToes was launching two foot creams at Walmart and needed ads fast. Instead of booking creators, we logged every clip the brand already had, wrote ads around what was really on camera and edited them into a full test batch.

Q1 · Why not just shoot new content?

Because the footage was already there.

The brand had folders of creator videos from earlier campaigns: bedroom routines, a ballerina, cracked-heel demos, clips filmed in Walmart. Nobody had looked at them as raw material for new ads. A launch can't wait for casting, shipping and reshoots.

For marketersReusing licensed UGC as b-roll cuts cost and time per ad; the bottleneck moves from production to ideas.

What we got
  • Creator clips from past campaigns
  • Product photos: studio, flatlay, shelf
  • Two creams with different jobs
  • A launch date at Walmart
The brief
  • Ads for both creams, existing footage only
  • Voiceover and music-plus-text videos
  • Static ads alongside the videos
  • Every ad ends at the Walmart shelf
Q2 · How do you find ads inside old footage?

We logged every shot before writing a line.

Every clip got a code and a one-line description: M for Magnesium, U for Urea, L for lifestyle. That catalog became the alphabet. A concept could only use shots that exist, so nothing written on paper fell apart in the edit.

For marketers64 shot codes across 10 creators. Each concept lists its b-roll sequence by code, with a note on why every shot is there.

Q3 · Two creams in one batch?

Two products, two different jobs.

The Magnesium cream sells a feeling: tired feet and better sleep. The Urea cream sells a visible fix: cracked heels, smooth again. So each got its own angles, its own footage and its own kind of proof.

Q4 · Which formats?

Four ways to tell it, tested side by side.

The same footage can carry a spoken story, a silent text ad, a whole persona or a meme. Running them together shows which language the audience answers to, not just which clip.

Voiceover

A first-person story

25–40 s over b-roll, written word for word and ready to record.

Music + text

No voice, three lines

Lo-fi or trending audio; on-screen text carries the whole story.

Character

One creator, one life

The dancer, the mom, the night-shift worker: one person's clips become a persona.

POV hook

Meme first, product second

"POV: your husband steals your foot cream." Looks native to the feed.

Q6 · How is one ad built?

Five beats, ending at the shelf.

Short ads leave no room to wander. Every script follows the same skeleton, so a viewer gets the pain, the reason it works and where to buy it before the music ends.

Q7 · What if the footage doesn't fit?

We flagged the gaps instead of faking them.

A fixed library means some ideas hit missing shots. Every concept was marked before editing, so the client saw upfront which ones were ready and which needed a workaround.

Green or amber

Ready vs needs a fix

Green concepts went straight to the edit. Amber ones carried a note: which shot is missing and what replaces it.

Burned-in text

Captions on captions

Some lifestyle clips already had subtitles. New text sits on a solid background so the two never fight.

Missing shots

Swap, don't stretch

No office desk, no kitchen floor at 2AM. We used the closest real shot or moved the idea into a static ad.

no surprises in the edit
Q8 · Why is Walmart in every ad?

The store is part of the proof.

For a launch, "where do I get it" matters as much as "why". Shelf, cart and storefront shots turn $9.99 into a reason to try it today, not a reason to add it to a wishlist.

Q9 · And the static ads?

Same ideas, no video needed.

Statics carry the angles that work in one glance: before and after, a price, a meme. They also caught the ideas that had no footage: the corporate-girl concept had no office shots, so it became a starter-pack static instead of a weak video.

For marketersEach static mirrors a video angle, so a winning message can be confirmed across formats, not just in one creative.

one message, four layouts

"This is NOT dry skin": the same explanation of why cracks come back, tested in four layouts and colour schemes, so the test reads design, not message.

two backgrounds

A habit calendar with the Walmart logo: light against brand purple.
