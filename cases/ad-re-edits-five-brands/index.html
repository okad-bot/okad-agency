Q1 · Why re-edit instead of shooting?

The footage is fine. The ad is tired.

An ad stops working long before the footage does. People have seen the first three seconds, not the creator. Change the hook, the order and the sound, and the same person can sell again. It takes days instead of weeks and costs a fraction of a new shoot.

Q2 · Skincare · Breakout + Aid

Fifteen ads, five formats.

Breakout + Aid sells microneedle dots and collagen masks. They had UGC from six creators and footage from their store launch. I sorted it into five formats and cut 15 ads, most with no text at all, because skincare sells on texture and result.

From the client"Love the playfulness of the music for ASMR." "This one is my favourite."

Q3 · Dating app 40+ · DateMyAge

Winning faces, new words.

The brand's best ads kept featuring the same 22 creators. Instead of finding new ones, I wrote 24 new scripts and laid them over the footage that already worked. Each script was cut for this app and for its sister app, 48 videos in the plan. Seven scripts got an AI voiceover, the rest followed the slide style the audience already responded to.

For marketersIf a face keeps winning, the audience trusts that face. Give it a new message before you replace it.

Hooks
  • "Single after 45? That doesn't mean you failed. It means you know what you want."
  • "I'm done wasting time. I'm looking for a real partner."
  • "Dating apps today feel exhausting. Swiping. Ghosting. Empty chats."
  • "Your life has changed. Your dating app should too."
Q4 · Career training · Dreambound

Ten ads inside strict rules.

Dreambound helps people find training for jobs in health care: medical billing, phlebotomy, medical assistant. The brief was strict. Grab attention in the first seconds, sell one specific program, and never promise a job or name a school. I used their creator footage, site recordings and AI voiceover to build talking heads, a podcast-style ad and a phlebotomist POV for TikTok, Meta and Snapchat.

From the client"Looks great!"

Hooks
  • "You don't need college."
  • "I work in health care, but I've never touched a patient, and I make money from home."
  • "No experience? No problem."
  • "Wanna know a secret about the medical field? It's easier to get into than you think."
Q5 · Dog supplements · TiPaw

Three habits, one gut.

TiPaw makes supplement chews for dogs and had creator videos from across Europe: dogs rolling in grass, licking paws, scratching. On their own those clips were cute and forgettable. I turned them into longer story ads around one question: what do these three behaviors have to do with your dog's gut? The answer comes with an AI look inside the gut, and only then the chew.

the structure
  • "Discover the connection between these 3 dog behaviors and their gut health"
  • Each behavior shown with real creator footage
  • "Here's what most dog parents don't realize"
  • AI b-roll of the gut: "it triggers inflammation throughout their entire body"
  • The chew as the fix for the gut balance
Q6 · Gut health superfood · US brand

Red flags first, the jar later.

A US gut health brand had creator testimonials and product shots, all of them opening on the jar. I rebuilt them around things people feel but don't talk about: bloating after every meal, avoiding plans, the full feeling that never goes away. Then an AI look inside the body explains why, and only then the product. About 20 edits a month, with 8 AI b-roll clips made for the scripts.

For marketersIn a category full of "clean ingredients" claims, a list of symptoms stops the scroll faster than a list of benefits.

four story formats
  • Red flags, symptoms people recognize in themselves
  • Gut and brain, a science fact with an AI visual
  • Week by week, progress told by a creator
  • A gift story, the product as something you share
