Q1 · Why a content factory and not SMM?

More videos, a fifth of the cost.

Before starting I put three options on the table: classic SMM, a content factory or a mix. Per video, the factory came out about five times cheaper than SMM, because scripting, shooting and editing happen in batches. The client chose 40 videos a month.

For marketersA store with hundreds of products doesn't need one perfect video. It needs a video for every hero product, every month, on every platform.

Q2 · What does a collectibles video look like?

Talk like a fan, not like a shop.

Collectors know the lore better than any copywriter. So every script was built around a fan reference, an inside joke or a character's catchphrase. Captions like "We call him DoomGuy", "Cowabunga!" and "You need to nerd out" do more than any product description.

Q3 · Blogger or product?

I started with a face. The products won.

The first cycle was built around a local creator: reactions, unboxing at the table, memes. It brought views, but the figures were doing the work. So in my December report I moved the focus from the blogger to the store and its products, and the audience to Europe.

Q4 · How do you shoot 46 videos in one day?

By zones, not by brands.

The first shoot gave me one clear lesson: plan by zones, not by brands. Every move between sets costs time on light and backgrounds. For February I planned 46 scenarios with exact product codes and grouped them by zone: the turntable, the shelf wall, the table, the hands. Fallout, TMNT, Jurassic Park, Doom, Demon Slayer, Lord of the Rings, all in one day.

For marketersWrite the shot list around the set, not around the catalog. Changing products is fast. Changing the set is what eats the day.

Q5 · What came out of it?

YouTube took off, TikTok woke up.

Numbers from my report for 29 November to 20 December. The new videos found their audience on YouTube first. TikTok profile visits nearly doubled and most videos passed 1,000 views. Instagram lost some followers, on purpose: I cleaned out old giveaway accounts from outside the store's market.
