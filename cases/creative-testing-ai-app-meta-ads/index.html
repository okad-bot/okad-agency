Q1 · Why weren't their ads working?

People had seen the same ads over and over.

The client is an AI app that turns a few selfies into professional headshots. It spends a large monthly budget on Meta ads and wanted to roughly double it. The ads couldn't keep up: almost all of them were still images, and only one or two videos had ever worked.

For marketersAccount ran on fatigued statics; top creatives at frequency ~2; no creative diversity, no read on why winners won.

Q2 · How do you know what to say in an ad?

We don't guess. We read what customers already wrote.

Before writing a line, we study why people buy and what stops them. Their own words become the script.

For marketersAbout half of the first 70 concepts trace to a specific data point: buyer segments, review themes, objection clusters from ad comments.

1 · We read

What buyers already say

Buyer surveys, reviews, comments under the ads, past ad results and what competitors run.

2 · We find

Why they buy, and what stops them

Who the buyer is, where they use the product, and the three doubts that kill the sale.

3 · We write

Scripts in their words

Every concept points back to something a real customer said or did. No brainstorm guesses.

Buyers kept mentioning where their “before” photo came from: a car, a couch, a bathroom mirror.PATTERN IN AD COMMENTS

Real starting points sell the result. We asked creators for their actual car, couch and bathroom selfies and put them next to the output. "Left was taken in a car" became a whole series.

“What happens when you show up for the interview?”THE #1 FEAR IN AD COMMENTS

People don't fear looking worse. They fear looking different. So the message became "same person, same face, just professionally lit" — never "a new you".

Most buyers are working professionals: sales, healthcare, founders, real estate — and most use the photo on LinkedIn.FROM THE BUYER DATA

Viewers should recognise themselves in two seconds. Scripts name real jobs — realtor, sales lead, founder — and show the headshot where it lives: a LinkedIn profile, a listing site.

Q4 · Why don't your videos look like ads?

Because ads get skipped. We copy what people watch for fun — and hide the proof inside.

An ad that looks like an ad gets skipped. So every format comes from organic TikTok and Reels — question stickers, trend memes, street-style signs, a FaceTime call — and almost every one carries the proof: the selfie next to the headshot.

Q5 · What makes a frame work?

Every part of the screen has one job.

The overlays are designed in-house: same caption style, same before/after rings, the creator's real name on every profile. Viewers learn the visual language and read each frame in a second.

read in one second
  1. Hook in the top third. A native question sticker, read before the face.
  2. Face in the centre, eye-line to camera, a real room behind.
  3. One idea per caption, white boxes like organic posts.
  4. Proof: before → after avatars in red and green rings, real name and job title.
  5. Soft CTA in the caption, never a banner.
  1. "Before" profile on top — the old photo in a real LinkedIn layout.
  2. Creator points at the change, so the eye follows her hand.
  3. Green highlight on the outcome: "increased profile views in 2 weeks".
  4. "After" profile below, same name, same bio, only the photo differs.
  5. Bottom 20% left clear for the platform's buttons; key text stays inside the 4:5 crop.

Q7 · What makes a script sell?

Short, personal, in the customer's own words.

This product is bought on impulse by busy professionals. So scripts hit a feeling in the first second, prove it with a real photo, and finish in under 20 seconds.

Openings that sold
  • "Interview in 2 days. No headshot." 0.4X
  • "Recruiters notice your photo first." 0.45X
  • "From my worst selfies to my best headshot." 0.55X
  • "My LinkedIn views tripled." 0.65X
Openings we dropped
  • "Recruiters judge you in 100 ms." 0 sales
  • "Your career is not a coincidence." 3.9X
  • "Does it actually look like you?" 1.2X
  • Anything longer than 20 s 1.25X

The pattern: personal and specific beats statistics and lectures. "You've been putting this off" makes people feel seen; "studies show" makes them scroll.
