Q1 · Why not just generate the photos?

Because AI shrinks the crib.

Generated nurseries look great until a parent notices the crib is the size of a dollhouse. For furniture that is a returned order. So every visual started from a real customer photo, and every result went through a size check against the product's real measurements before it became an ad.

Dresser

Austin 5-drawer dresser, 34" tall. The model should be roughly twice its height.

Crib

Same rule, a different room. "Daily life with a child is busy enough. Your crib shouldn't add to it."

Q2 · What do parents need to see?

Safety first, then the dream nursery.

Parents of newborns buy with their worries, not with their eyes. So the carousels answer the worry before showing the room: safe finishes, stable build, someone checking every crib. Then comes the picture they want in their own home.

Q3 · How does a carousel sell one product?

One product, one story per swipe.

Each product got its own carousel. A cover with a feeling, a close-up of the wood, a real customer review, and a final slide with the button. Reviews are the slide people stop on, because they're written by parents, not by the brand.

Top: the brand carousel. Bottom: the dresser carousel, plus a slide from the crib set.

Q4 · And video?

Real couples, real assembly.

Three product Reels a month. The one I like most shows what every parent fears: putting the crib together. A couple unboxes it, builds it on the floor and ends with a hug. "Assembly has never been so easy and yet felt so secure."

For marketersFurniture reviews always mention assembly. If people talk about it in the comments, show it in the ad before they ask.
