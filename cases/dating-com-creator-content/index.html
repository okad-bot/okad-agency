Q1 · Why is this hard to advertise?

Because this audience doesn't want to be sold to.

Singles in their 40s and 50s scroll past anything that looks like an app ad. They stop for honesty: eating dinner alone in a big city, a first date after fifteen years of marriage, a dad's secret to 38 years together. So the brief was content first, brand last.

For marketersVideos with a CTA were edited with a pause before it, so the same file can run as an organic post or as a paid ad.

What we got
  • A list of content topics
  • An audience of 45 to 60
  • A request for trends and reactions
  • A product we signed up for ourselves
The brief
  • First-person stories, not advice
  • Native editing, no agency polish
  • Formats from the feed, not from ads
  • Brand at the end, after a pause
Q2 · Where did the scripts come from?

We graded every topic and rewrote the weak ones.

The starting list read like blog titles. We scored each of 26 topics out of 10, wrote down why, and turned it into something a real person would say to camera. Two hooks per script, so the opening can be tested too.

For marketersSeven rubrics: relationships after 35, loneliness, real stories, POV memes, film breakdowns, save-worthy lists and trend reactions.

Q3 · Why so many formats?

Seven formats, so the feed never repeats itself.

A brand feed of one person talking gets skipped by week two. We mixed formats so every post feels like something people already watch, and so the brand learns which one its audience answers to.

Talking head

One person, one confession

Loneliness, first dates, the five-minute test. Native subtitles and a title card.

Street interviews

Strangers in New York

"What's the loneliest thing about living here?" Real answers, no script.

Lists and films

Podcast

Two sides of one question

A real couple argues it out at the table. Roles swap from clip to clip.

Text hooks

The words do the talking

Silent acting, one line on screen. Built for Zendate's Instagram.

Q4 · What is the podcast format?

Two people, one table, opposite answers.

A real couple at a table with coffee, arguing one dating question per episode: age, texting, who pays, starting over after a divorce. One plays the doubter, the other the straight talker, and the comments pick a side.

For marketersTen episodes, each aimed at a user segment from the client's data: companion, romantic or transactional. Roles rotate between episodes so the same two faces never feel repetitive.

Setup

Built for subtitles

Seated, elbows on the table, bright mugs, no black clothes. A quarter of the frame left free at the top for captions.

Delivery

Hook exact, rest alive

The first 2–3 lines word for word. After that, a real conversation with interruptions and laughs, reactions 30–40% brighter than life.

Roles

Never the same pair twice

Cocky and gentle, teased nice guy, tough love, tender. The dynamic changes every episode.

Q5 · And Zendate?

A second audience: people far from home.

For Zendate we wrote 45 short text hooks for expats and the Asian diaspora: mom's matchmaking, accents, culture clashes on a first date, being "cute but short". Creators act each one out without a word; the line on screen carries the joke.

For marketersEvery hook was tagged by content pillar (culture, identity, trust and safety, product), creator profile, and a note on why it spreads: who forwards it and what the comments will argue about.

Q6 · How do you brief ten different creators?

One brief per format, down to the gesture.

A silent clip lives or dies on a face. A couple dialogue dies if both people play the same role every time. So each format got its own brief, and the editors got one too.

Silent hooks

Readable with sound off

"Look at the camera, serious, 2–3 seconds. Look away. Smile like you amused yourself." Each gesture is one beat of the line.

Couple talk

Roles that rotate

Cocky and gentle, teased nice guy, tough love. Reactions 30–40% brighter than life; only the hook said word for word.

Editing

Native, not polished

A 3–7 second title card, small CapCut-style subtitles, 2–3 b-roll inserts, moody music at night, light music for lists.
