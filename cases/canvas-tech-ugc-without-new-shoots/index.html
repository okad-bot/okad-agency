whywhy brands overpay for canvas and tech UGC

Brands pay UGC creators for every attempt.

I run OKAD, a creative agency that makes and tests video ads for apps, so I see this setup every week. A brand pays per video plus a bonus per winner. Most organic videos don't take off, and the brand pays for all of them anyway.

So I asked one question. What if the footage is the only thing you buy from a creator, and you do the hooks, the text, the formats and the volume yourself? To test it I used the cheapest footage I had: my own camera roll.

Paying per video
  • You pay for every attempt, including the ones nobody watches.
  • You wait. "I'll send it tomorrow" for five days straight.
  • When a format wins, you need the same person for ten more versions, on their schedule and at their price.
  • One creator going quiet for two weeks stalls a whole account.
step 1build a UGC b-roll library from your camera roll

A UGC b-roll library: 35 clips from my camera roll.

Nothing was filmed for this. Me at my laptop, mirror selfies, coffee in a cafe, a walk in Lisbon, a plane seat, a tech event with a robot dog. Most clips are 5 to 8 seconds long.

tag every clip by mood
happy shocked neutral upset angry

Then add the setting (home, cafe, street, event) and the action (talking, gesture, mirror, screen). It takes an hour once. After that every idea starts with "I need a shocked face at a laptop" and I find the clip in seconds.

For marketersThe footage needs no script, no brief and no studio light. It needs variety in settings, outfits and moods, and a face that looks like the audience you want.

step 2copy canvas UGC and tech UGC formats that already win

I didn't invent hooks. I borrowed winning UGC formats.

I watched what was taking off on canvas UGC and tech UGC accounts, saved 2 or 3 examples of each trend, and rebuilt the structure with my own clip and my own topic. Copy the setup, the punchline, the text placement and the length. Never someone else's footage or exact words.

step 3edit UGC videos in one of three formats

Three UGC video templates. Every video fits one.

15 seconds max, no single clip longer than 7. Trending audio on top. I set up a simple bot that takes a trend, writes 3 text options for my topic and cuts 1 to 3 videos from the tagged clips. You can do the same by hand in CapCut. The bot only made it faster.

A

Reaction + hook

One clip, text on top or at the middle. The fastest type. I made most videos this way: text trends, memes, hiring calls.

B

Reaction + product

A reaction clip, then a screen recording of the product. Emotion first, proof second, in one video.

C

Reaction + screenshots

One background clip, text at the top, screenshots cycling every 2 seconds below. For interfaces, results and reviews.

step 6recycle winning UGC hooks with Instagram trial reels

Same hook, different clip.

I published most videos as Instagram trial reels, which are shown to people who don't follow you yet. That's the fairest test, no loyal audience inflating the numbers. When a hook won, I didn't wait for new footage. I put the same text on another clip from my roll.

For marketersOrganic is a numbers game. Most videos land in the low thousands and a few explode, and you can't predict which. A fixed clip library is what makes volume cheap: a new video costs a new line of text, not a new shoot.

step 7test the UGC formats on a brand-new Instagram account

The format travels. The followers don't have to.

To make sure this wasn't about my existing audience, I opened a new account with zero followers and a different look, moved the winning hooks over almost word for word, and cut them from b-roll the same way. From the first posts, every reel got 340 to 1,600 views.

for brandsUGC for apps without paying per video

Buy UGC footage once. Make the videos yourself.

For each account you want to grow, buy 30 to 120 clips from one creator in a single shoot. Ask for variety, not polish: different outfits, rooms and moods, a few talking-to-camera shots, a few reactions, and screen recordings of your product. Then cut them into trending formats week after week.

Paying per video
  • You pay for every video, plus bonuses on winners.
  • Speed depends on the creator's schedule.
  • When a format wins, you ask for more and wait.
  • The creator disappears and the account stalls.
  • A few ideas tested a month.
Buying a clip library
  • One batch of raw clips per account.
  • A new video takes minutes once a trend appears.
  • A winning hook goes on 5 more clips the same day.
  • The footage is already yours.
  • Dozens of hooks and offers tested a month.

checklistsave this

Canvas and tech UGC checklist: nine things to do this month.

  • Buy or collect 30 to 120 varied clips per account

faqcanvas UGC and tech UGC questions

Questions brands ask me most.

What is canvas UGC?

Canvas UGC is organic content for apps posted on fresh, brand-dedicated accounts, the "blank canvas". Creators usually post daily short videos there and get paid per video plus bonuses for videos that take off. I show how to get the same kind of content from a one-time clip library instead.

What is tech UGC?

Tech UGC is user-generated video for apps, SaaS and AI tools: a real person reacting to a product, showing the screen, or talking about a problem the product solves. It works best when it looks like a normal post, not an ad.

Do I need to hire UGC creators for every video?

No. I recommend buying footage once: 30 to 120 varied clips per account from one creator. Then you cut those clips into trending formats yourself, so a new video costs a new line of text instead of a new shoot.

How many clips do I need for one account?

I ran my test on about 35 clips from my own camera roll. For a brand account I suggest 30 to 120 clips with different outfits, rooms, moods, reactions, talking-to-camera shots and screen recordings of the product.

Does this work on a brand-new Instagram account?

Yes. I moved my winning hooks to a new account with zero followers, cut them from b-roll the same way, and every reel got 340 to 1,600 views from the first posts.

Which offer works best to collect UGC creators?

A job offer. "I'm hiring tech UGC creators, paid and remote" brought 5,471 keyword requests. A free portfolio tool brought 229 with the same clips and the same account.

What tools do I need?

A phone with old clips, CapCut for editing, Instagram trial reels for testing, and ManyChat to turn comments into DMs with a form. I added a simple bot to write text options and cut videos faster, but it is optional.
