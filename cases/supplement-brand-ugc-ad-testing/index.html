Q1 · What do you test first?

The reason to buy, not the creator.

One product solves different problems for different people. Someone wants clearer skin, someone wants to stop feeling bloated, someone just wants to keep up at the gym. I split the scripts into angles: flavor, gut health, skin, energy, immunity, lungs, gym and a special offer. Each angle got its own creator and its own setting.

Skin

"Glow support"

Outdoors, natural light, no makeup talk. Just the result.

Gut and energy

"Day 1: lighter"

A morning run with a day by day timeline on screen.

Lungs

"Your lungs work for you"

A guy, a dog, a ball. Health shown as play.

Gym

"What they've been missing"

A male angle for a product most people think is for women.

Q2 · Why three hooks per video?

The first second decides everything.

If the hook fails, the ad never gets a chance to sell. So every creator video left the edit in three versions with the same body and a different opening. The media buyers launched all three, and the data picked the winner instead of my taste.

"UNLESS YOU DO THIS"A WARNING OVER A PRODUCT SHOT

Curiosity. A spoon of gel and half a sentence. You stay to find out what "this" is.

"and my face is proof"A CLOSE-UP SELFIE

Proof. The result is on screen before any claim is made.

"this is my sixth jar"A MIRROR SELFIE

Loyalty. Someone who bought it six times is a better argument than any ingredient list.

Q3 · How do you show something you can't see?

An AI look inside the body.

Minerals and gut health happen where no camera goes. So between the creator shots I cut in short AI visuals of the body, with one simple line on top: "magnesium helps your body". Then back to the creator, with a timeline that makes the change feel real: day 1, week 2.

For marketersSupplement ads live on claims, and claims are what platforms restrict. Showing a routine and a feeling, then letting a creator say it in her own words, kept the ads both believable and safe.

Q4 · What did creators actually film?

A shot list with a story arc.

Every creator got a brief for 10 to 15 second b-roll clips, filmed on a phone during a normal day. The clips weren't random. Together they move from heavy and uncomfortable to light and more like yourself, so an editor can build a before and after from any set.

from one gut health brief
  • Post-meal discomfort, a hand on the stomach
  • Adjusting a tight waistband after dinner
  • Clearing takeout boxes, wiping the counter
  • A simple breakfast with the jar on the table
  • Walking outside, lighter body language
  • A mirror check, more comfortable in your clothes

Twelve shots in the full brief. The jar appears naturally, not in every frame.
