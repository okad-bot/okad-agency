Q1 · Why not keep the ads they had?

Because "one click" doesn't make anyone laugh.

The brand's live ads led with the mechanism: "one click and their face is added". Competitors sold jokes or step-by-step demos. Nobody showed the part people actually buy the card for: the reaction when it lands.

For marketersMeta Ad Library audit of the brand and two competitors. Rule we applied: if a hook says how the product works, rewrite it as what someone feels.

What we got
  • Live ads built on the feature
  • Digital e-cards and printed cards
  • Different offers in the UK and US
  • Customers who keep and frame cards
The brief
  • Hooks about the moment, not the tech
  • A real printed card on camera
  • Push the 3-for bundle offer
  • "Face swap" only, never "AI"
Q2 · Where did the stories come from?

Seven ideas, and the client's notes on each.

We pitched 7 video and 7 static concepts as hooks and visual direction only, before writing a single script. The founder's comments on the pitch turned into plot points.

For marketersEvery concept came with a source map: what we took from the client's ads, what from competitors and what was new. Empty territory we found: reactions, keepsakes, pets and wall art.

1 · We audit

What everyone already runs

Brand and competitor ads, hook by hook: features, jokes, tutorials. Then what nobody shows.

2 · We pitch

Concepts before scripts

Seven video ideas with four hooks each, plus seven statics. Cheap to change, fast to approve.

3 · We merge

Client notes into the plot

The founder knows what customers do with the cards. That knowledge went straight into the scripts.

"Can we position it around a physical card rather than digital?"CLIENT NOTE ON THE GROUP-CHAT IDEA

The card arrives first, then the chat explodes. She photographs the printed card on the kitchen table, sends it to the family, and five more orders follow that week.

"Many customers keep their cards. Some have framed them."CLIENT NOTE ON THE FRIDGE IDEA

Became The Fridge Test. The one card that survived among the kids' drawings, a neighbour who asked if it was a painting, and a friend who framed hers.

"It may encourage bulk purchases."CLIENT NOTE ON THE BIRTHDAY HACK

Twelve cards in one evening, on the 3-for offer. A stack ordered home for the year, and cards posted straight to relatives who live far away.

Q3 · Why nine videos?

Three creators, three stories, never the same script.

Each creator told each story her own way: a different hook, a different relative, different details. The test shows which story sells and which voice tells it best, without nine copies of one video.

For marketersFour story types across nine videos: group chat (social proof), fridge (keepsake), wallpaper (daily visibility), birthday hack (bundle and planning).

Q5 · How do you film a reaction on cue?

We designed the group chat before the shoot.

Reactions sell this product, but a real family won't reply on cue. We made the chat screens ourselves and sent them to creators to show on camera, together with a shot list for each of them.

Shared b-roll

Same for every creator

Browsing the site, picking a design, the face appearing, the cart with pricing, the group-chat screen.

Per creator

Shots for her story

The fridge walk, an unboxing, a fanned stack of cards; or the lock screen and an unlock for the wallpaper story.

Brief

Light, sound, tone

Daylight, a mic, bright clothes, eyes on the lens. Plus a tone line: "nan", "warm mum", "friend with the best tips".

Q6 · What does every story hit?

Five beats, from guilt to hero.

Every script starts with a small, familiar failure and ends with the creator as the thoughtful one in the family. The product sits in the middle, as the reason things changed.

Details we wrote in
  • A "Sorry for your loss" card with "Happy Birthday" in pen
  • Up since Christmas among the kids' drawings
  • Survived two phone updates and a storage-full panic
  • "Mum, when did you become so thoughtful?"
Checked against the site
  • Real order: design first, then photo
  • Right offer per market: £10 or $12
  • Delivery that exists in that country
  • "Face swap", never "AI"
