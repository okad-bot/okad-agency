Your Christian Date · Instagram, TikTok, YouTube · organic

800K views on one Reel. Shot as b-roll.

Your Christian Date is a dating app for Christians. Instead of buying finished UGC videos one by one, I ordered raw footage from creators: one-liners to camera and a library of everyday b-roll. My editors turned that footage into 60 videos a month. Then I shot one special series, a first-timer in church, and it became the account's biggest hit.

Q1 · Why order b-roll instead of finished videos?

One shoot, dozens of videos.

A finished UGC video is used once. Raw footage can be cut again and again. So the brief asked creators for footage only: 4K, 60 fps, no cuts, no edits, no music. Two tasks per creator. First, short lines to camera. Second, a set of everyday moments a Christian single would actually have.

Task 1 · one-liner

A hook to camera

"If this offends you, it might be the truth." Eight scripted lines per creator.

Task 2 · b-roll

Reading the Bible

Couch, blanket, morning light. No text, no talking.

Task 2 · b-roll

Singing in the car

The kind of moment people film themselves anyway.

Task 2 · b-roll

Smiling at the phone

Swiping, laughing at a message, waking up happy.

I cast

Real people, 35 to 65

I shortlisted 26 creators on Backstage who look like the app's audience, not like influencers. Rates were $275 to $700 per package.

I brief

Lines plus a shot list

Hooks like "Christians, stop doing this!" and "This or That: Christian edition", plus a list of moments to film.

I tag

A library, not a folder

Every clip went into a catalog with the creator, setting and mood, 390 rows in total, so an editor finds a shot in seconds.

Q2 · How does one clip become many videos?

Same footage, a different line on top.

The text carries the idea. The footage carries the feeling. The exact same shot of a woman reading her Bible became a joke in one video and a wish in another. That is how a small pool of footage fed 60 videos a month without looking repetitive.

"Ever feel like there is a girl Bible and..."SAME SHOT, VIDEO ONE

Became a meme. Her color-coded Bible next to his plain one. Light, relatable, easy to share with a friend.

"Someone who wants to love God with you"SAME SHOT, VIDEO TWO

Became a wish list. "Stay single until..." then a line per clip: someone who will pray with you, someone who puts God above you.

"Take her to church, not your bed."A WINNER, RE-CUT WITH NEW FOOTAGE

Became a template. When a line worked, I rewrote it in a few versions and laid it over other creators' footage, ending on "Check the link in bio".

Q3 · What was the special video?

A first-timer in church.

Next to the b-roll, I wrote one series for a single actor. "POV: You go to a Catholic Church for the first time." He shot it against a plain backdrop, and my editors placed him inside a church. Lines like "I swear I blinked and we switched chapters" and "Because it seems like everyone already knows it".

These were the top videos of the whole project. One Reel did around 250K in its first month, then kept going and reached about 800K three weeks later. So I did it again in the Vatican.

For marketersThe joke is never about faith. It's about being new and awkward, and everyone has been new somewhere. The app comes in only at the end: "I wish there was a space to ask all this out loud."

Three buckets, one footage pool.

After the church series took off, I split January and February into three kinds of video. Each bucket reused footage I already had, plus a few new lines from the actor.

Q5 · What came out of it?

One hit, a steady tail behind it.

Numbers from the client review call in March 2026. The church Reel led. Several others landed between 20K and 100K, and an ordinary Reel held at one to two thousand views.

learned

Funny beats preachy

Observational humor about being new did the views. Videos that lectured people about how to be a good Christian went nowhere.

learned

Footage is an asset

Ordering raw b-roll once and tagging it meant every new idea could be tested the same day, without a new shoot.

learned

Geo matters on TikTok

Autoposting put the TikTok account in the wrong region, and views came from Africa instead of the US. The hit lived on Instagram Reels. Post from the market you want.
