10 Best AI Photo Book Services in 2026

An honest comparison of AI-powered photo book services: what the AI actually does, what it costs, and who each service fits. Written by the team behind Remeet, so I'm upfront about where we sit on the list.

Photo books6 min readby the OKAD team

I run OKAD, a creative studio. Last year we built Remeet, a photo book service that uses AI to turn a messy camera roll into a designed book. It's on this list at number one, so full disclosure upfront. I've tried to make the rest of the list genuinely useful. Each service solves a different problem, and for some people another option on this list will be the better pick.

1. Remeet

Remeet is what we built for people who have thousands of photos and no time to sort through them. You upload your library and the AI handles the full pipeline: quality scoring (blur, exposure, composition, face detection), duplicate removal, event-based grouping, layout design with pacing and photo pairing, and caption suggestions. After the AI creates the initial design, a human designer reviews and adjusts it before you see a proof.

The result is a digital photo book you can view on any device. Two options: 30 pages for $29, or 100 pages for $79. The per-page cost drops significantly on the larger book. Turnaround is 72 hours for the proof.

Pick Remeet if you have a large, messy photo library and want an editorial-quality book without spending a weekend in a drag-and-drop editor. Not the right fit if you want to hand-place every photo yourself.

2. Chatbooks

Chatbooks auto-generates monthly photo books from your camera roll or Instagram. You connect your photos, the app selects recent ones, and a book shows up at your door. The whole point is zero effort. Design is basic — clean grids, not much curation — but consistency is the real value. A book a month for about $10.

Pick Chatbooks if you're a busy parent who wants photo books to just happen. No decisions, no design time. You'll get a bookshelf full of monthly volumes without thinking about it.

3. Mixbook

Mixbook gives you a solid drag-and-drop editor with AI-assisted layout suggestions. Upload your photos and it proposes page arrangements, but you keep full control to move things around. Templates are well-designed and the print quality is good. It's the middle ground between full DIY and full automation.

Pick Mixbook if you enjoy the design process but want a head start. The AI suggestions save time without taking away creative control.

4. Google Photos

If you use Google Photos, you've probably seen it suggest photo books after a trip or event. The AI groups your photos by location and date, proposes a book, and you can edit it in a simple web interface. Design is free — you pay when you order a print (softcover from about $15, hardcover from $30).

Pick Google Photos if you're already in the Google ecosystem and want something quick without installing another app. The AI suggestions are decent for trips and events, less useful for everyday life photos.

5. Artifact Uprising

Artifact Uprising is the premium choice. Beautiful papers, printing, and materials. The AI is minimal — mostly auto-placement into their templates — but the physical quality is a step above everyone else on this list. Hardcovers start around $55 and go up quickly with size and pages.

Pick Artifact Uprising if you care most about how the finished book feels in your hands. You'll do most of the design work yourself, but the print quality makes it worth it for special occasions like weddings or milestone gifts.

6. Shutterfly

Shutterfly is the mainstream default. Basic auto-fill drops your photos into templates. The editor is functional but dated. Where Shutterfly wins is price: with their frequent 40–60% off sales, a hardcover book can cost under $15. Without a coupon, prices are higher and harder to justify.

Pick Shutterfly if you're budget-conscious and willing to wait for a sale. The design won't wow anyone, but the price-to-page ratio is hard to beat during promotions.

7. Blurb

Blurb is built for people who already know what they're doing. Their desktop app, BookWright, is a real layout tool. You can also upload a finished PDF. No AI to speak of — this is a manual, professional-grade workflow. Print quality is excellent, especially for photo-heavy books. Pricing is per page and per format, starting around $20 for a small softcover.

Pick Blurb if you're a photographer, designer, or self-publisher who wants full control over every pixel. Not the right fit if you want the service to make design decisions for you.

8. Snapfish

Snapfish is similar to Shutterfly: simple editor, basic auto-fill, frequent promotions. The AI does little beyond placing photos in templates. The main draw is price. Snapfish regularly runs deals that make small photo books nearly free (just pay shipping). Quality is acceptable for casual books, not for premium gifts.

Pick Snapfish if you want the cheapest option and don't mind basic design. Good for everyday family albums where volume matters more than polish.

9. Montage

Montage combines AI video and photo books. Upload your photos and it creates a video montage alongside a printable book. The AI selects and sequences photos, adds music to the video version, and generates a matching printed layout. It's a niche offering, but a clever one for events like graduations or birthdays where you want both a video to share and a book to keep.

Pick Montage if you want a hybrid video and print keepsake from the same set of photos. Not the best choice if you only care about the printed book.

10. Motif

Motif (by Shutterfly) integrates directly into Apple Photos on Mac. It uses Apple's on-device intelligence to suggest photos and layouts. If you already organize your library in Apple Photos, it's the most seamless print option for the Apple ecosystem. Design quality is mid-range — better than Shutterfly's main app, not as refined as Mixbook or Artifact Uprising.

Pick Motif if you live in the Apple ecosystem and want to go from Photos.app to a printed book without leaving your Mac. The integration is the entire value proposition.

How to choose

  1. How many photos do you have? If thousands, you need real AI curation (Remeet, Chatbooks). If dozens, any editor works.
  2. How much time do you want to spend? Zero: Remeet or Chatbooks. Some: Mixbook or Google Photos. Full control: Blurb or Artifact Uprising.
  3. Digital or printed? Remeet is digital-first. Most others are print-first.
  4. Budget? Under $15: Snapfish or Shutterfly with a coupon. $29–$79: Remeet. Premium: Artifact Uprising or Blurb.
  5. Special occasion or everyday? Wedding books: Artifact Uprising or Blurb. Monthly family albums: Chatbooks. Year-in-review: Remeet or Google Photos.

Questions people ask

What makes a photo book service AI-powered?

AI-powered photo book services use machine learning to automate one or more steps: selecting the best photos from a large library, removing duplicates and blurry shots, grouping images by event or date, generating layouts, and sometimes writing captions. The depth varies — some just auto-fill templates, others handle the full design pipeline.

Do AI photo books look good?

It depends on the service. Basic auto-fill (Shutterfly, Snapfish) arranges photos in templates but rarely produces something you'd call well-designed. Services like Remeet, which pair AI curation with human designer review, get much closer to what a professional would create. The best results come from AI doing the sorting and a human doing the taste check.

How much do AI photo books cost?

Prices range from free design with paid printing (Google Photos, around $15 for a softcover) to $29–$79 for AI-curated digital books (Remeet) to $50–$150+ for premium printed hardcovers (Artifact Uprising, Blurb). Chatbooks starts at about $10/month for auto-generated mini books. Most services run frequent promotions.
