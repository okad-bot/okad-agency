How to Warm Up a New TikTok Account [2026 Protocol]

The day-by-day TikTok account warm-up protocol that signals 'real user' to the algorithm. Covers niche training, engagement patterns, posting schedules, and the mistakes that get new accounts shadowbanned.

Guide10 min readby the OKAD team

You just created a new TikTok account. You have your content ready. The temptation is to post immediately and start collecting data. Do not do that.

TikTok’s algorithm evaluates new accounts aggressively in their first days. An account that registers and starts posting within minutes looks like a bot. An account that browses, follows creators, watches videos to completion, and behaves like a normal user for several days before posting looks like a person. The algorithm treats these two accounts very differently.

This guide covers the exact warm-up protocol we use when launching creator accounts for brands — the same process behind campaigns that scaled to 60 videos per month and networks of 5,471 creators.

Why Warm-Up Matters

TikTok’s spam detection system watches three things on new accounts:

1. Activity patterns. Real users browse before they post. They scroll the For You page, follow accounts they like, watch videos to the end, and interact gradually. Bots register and immediately start uploading content or following hundreds of accounts.

2. Niche signals. The algorithm needs data to classify your account. When you search for keywords, watch specific content categories, and follow accounts in a vertical, TikTok learns what your account is about. Without this training period, the algorithm has no context for distributing your content — so it shows your videos to a random, uninterested audience.

3. Device and network consistency. Accounts that switch between devices, change IP addresses, or log in from different locations raise flags. TikTok tracks device identifiers, IP addresses, and usage patterns. Consistency signals legitimacy.

Skipping warm-up does not just reduce your reach. It can trigger a shadowban — where TikTok suppresses your content from the For You page without telling you. A shadowbanned account can post daily for weeks and never break a few hundred views. If you suspect your account is already restricted, our TikTok shadowban guide covers detection, recovery, and prevention.

The Day-by-Day Protocol

Day 1–2: Browse and Follow

Your only job is to look like a real user discovering TikTok for the first time.

  • Search your niche. If your account is about skincare, search “skincare routine,” “anti-aging tips,” “best serums 2026.” If it is about a product, search the product category. Do 5–8 searches across both days.
  • Follow ~30 accounts in your vertical. Not all at once — spread follows across sessions. Follow creators, brands, and commentators in your niche. Mix large accounts (1M+) with smaller ones (10K–100K).
  • Scroll the For You page for 10–20 minutes per session. Do 2–3 sessions per day. Watch videos to completion — do not skip through. Like 10–15 videos per session. Leave 3–5 genuine comments per session.
  • Do not post anything. No videos, no stories, no reposts.

The goal: TikTok’s recommendation engine starts showing you niche-relevant content on the For You page. By the end of Day 2, your feed should look like your content vertical.

Day 3–4: Deepen Engagement

You are still not posting. But your engagement becomes more intentional.

  • Follow 10–15 more accounts in your niche (total ~40–45 by end of Day 4).
  • Interact with content more deliberately. Save videos. Share videos to your “favorites.” Reply to comments on popular posts in your niche. Watch longer videos (30–60 seconds) all the way through.
  • Use the Discover tab. Browse trending sounds and hashtags in your vertical. This trains the algorithm on what content formats are relevant to your account.
  • Complete your profile. Add a profile photo (real person or branded image), write a bio that includes niche keywords (e.g., “Reviewing the best skincare products” or “Daily wellness tips”), and add a link if you have 1,000+ followers on another platform.

By end of Day 4, your For You page should be almost entirely niche-relevant content. If it is still showing random videos, continue the engagement pattern for 1–2 more days before posting.

Day 5: First Post

Your account is warmed up when your For You page consistently shows content in your niche and your recommendations are in the correct language and region.

  • Post one video. Your best-performing format — the one most likely to generate watch time and engagement.
  • Stay on the app for 15–20 minutes after posting. Browse, like, comment. This signals that you are a user who posts and engages, not a bot that uploads and disappears.
  • Monitor performance over 24–48 hours. A warmed-up account’s first video should reach at least 200–500 views in the first 24 hours. If it stays under 100, your warm-up may not be complete — continue the Day 3–4 protocol for another 2 days before posting again.

Day 6–10: Scale Posting Gradually

Do not jump from 1 post to 4 posts overnight.

  • Day 6–7: 1 post per day. Continue 2 engagement sessions daily.
  • Day 8–10: 2 posts per day. Maintain engagement sessions.
  • After Day 10: Scale to your target posting frequency (typically 2–4 per day for brand accounts).

This gradual ramp prevents the sudden activity spike that triggers spam detection on accounts less than two weeks old.

Mistakes That Get New Accounts Killed

Posting within hours of account creation. The single most common mistake. TikTok expects browsing behavior before posting behavior. An account that posts within its first hour is flagged.

Bulk following. Following 100+ accounts in one session looks automated. Spread follows across multiple days and sessions. Never follow more than 30 accounts in a single day during warm-up.

Switching devices or IP addresses. Log into the account from one device and one network. If you are using a VPN for region targeting, keep the same VPN server throughout the warm-up period. Changing IP addresses mid-warm-up is a red flag.

Using automation tools. No scheduling apps, no auto-likers, no follower bots. During warm-up, every action must be manual. You can introduce scheduling tools after your account has 2–4 weeks of manual activity and stable distribution.

Skipping engagement after posting. Posting and immediately closing the app signals bot behavior. Always stay on the platform for at least 10–15 minutes after each post, engaging with other content.

Warm-Up at Scale: Managing Multiple Accounts

When running creator accounts for brand campaigns, the warm-up challenge multiplies. Each account needs individual attention.

For the YourChristianDate campaign, we managed a pipeline that produced 60 videos per month across multiple creator accounts. Each account followed the same warm-up protocol, but with niche-specific engagement tailored to the dating and lifestyle vertical.

When OKAD managed the faceless UGC campaign for a drawing app, new accounts were warmed up with engagement focused on art, creativity, and digital drawing content. The niche training during warm-up directly influenced how the algorithm distributed the first videos.

At larger scale — like the Canvas Tech UGC network with 5,471 creators — warm-up becomes a standardized onboarding step. Every new creator account follows the same protocol checklist before posting their first piece of content. The system works because the protocol is simple enough to follow consistently across hundreds of accounts.

Batch warm-up tips for multiple accounts:

  • Stagger account creation. Do not create more than 1–2 accounts per device per day.
  • Use separate devices or browser profiles for each account during warm-up.
  • Track warm-up progress in a shared spreadsheet: account name, creation date, warm-up start, FYP verification date, first post date.
  • Do not rush accounts that are not showing niche-relevant FYP content. An extra day of warm-up is cheaper than a shadowbanned account.

Platform-Specific Warm-Up Differences

The protocol above is for TikTok, but if you are launching accounts across multiple platforms simultaneously, the warm-up differs:

TikTok vs Instagram

Instagram requires a longer warm-up — 4 full days before the first post, with a slower posting ramp (1 post/day for the first 4 days, then 2/day for days 5–10, then 3/day). Instagram also requires a static IP VPN — changing IP addresses triggers account restrictions faster than on TikTok. The engagement pattern is similar: scroll Reels, like, comment, follow accounts in your niche. For the full Instagram-specific protocol, see our Instagram account warm-up guide.

TikTok vs YouTube Shorts

YouTube is less sensitive to warm-up timing. After creating a Google account and YouTube channel, 4 days of browsing Shorts in your niche is sufficient. The key difference: YouTube allows full posting volume from day one after warm-up (all 4 daily videos immediately), while TikTok requires the gradual ramp described above.

Cross-Platform Warm-Up Schedule

If you are launching on all three platforms:

  • Day 1–4: Warm up all accounts simultaneously (browse, follow, engage on each platform)
  • Day 5: First TikTok post (1/day)
  • Day 5: First Instagram post (1/day)
  • Day 5: First YouTube Shorts (all 4 videos)
  • Day 6–10: Ramp TikTok to 2/day, Instagram stays at 1/day
  • Day 11–15: TikTok at target volume, Instagram ramps to 2–3/day

After Warm-Up: Maintaining Account Health

Warm-up is not a one-time event. The behaviors that protect your account during warm-up should continue throughout its life:

  • Engage daily. Even on days you do not post, spend 5–10 minutes scrolling, liking, and commenting. Accounts that only post and never browse lose distribution over time.
  • Post consistently. Gaps of more than 3–4 days can reduce your algorithmic priority. If you need to pause, continue engagement activity even without posting.
  • Monitor your analytics. A sudden drop in views (below 50% of your average) can indicate a soft shadowban. If this happens, reduce posting frequency and increase engagement for 3–5 days.
  • Keep your content niche-consistent. An account warmed up on skincare content that suddenly starts posting about cars confuses the algorithm. Stay in your lane, especially in the first 30 days.
  • Avoid content with watermarks. Reposting videos with other platforms’ watermarks (Instagram Reels logo, CapCut branding) triggers suppression. Always upload clean, watermark-free content.
  • Respond to comments. Accounts that receive comments but never reply signal disengagement. Reply to at least the first 5–10 comments on each video within the first hour of posting.

Warm-Up Troubleshooting

FYP still random after 4 days: Your engagement was too broad. Reset by doing 3–4 sessions of 15 minutes each, exclusively searching and watching content in your target niche. Avoid the Discover tab — go directly to Search.

First video got zero views: Check if the video was flagged for review (you will see a notification). If not, the account may need more warm-up time. Wait 48 hours, continue engagement, then post a different video.

Account restricted after first post: You likely triggered a spam filter — common if the first post was a product promotion or contained a link. For the second attempt, post organic, non-promotional content first. Product-focused content should wait until the account has 5–10 posts with stable view counts.

Multiple accounts on one device getting restricted: Do not run more than 2–3 accounts from the same device. If accounts keep getting flagged, switch to a different device for new accounts and use the restricted device only for already-established ones.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to warm up a new TikTok account?

A proper warm-up takes 3 to 5 days of consistent, human-like activity before your first post. Some operators extend this to 7 days for accounts that will post at high volume. The goal is to generate enough browsing, following, and engagement data that TikTok classifies your account as a real user rather than a bot or spam account.

Can I skip the warm-up and just start posting?

You can, but your first videos will likely reach a tiny audience or get suppressed entirely. TikTok monitors new accounts for bot-like behavior. An account that registers and immediately starts posting — especially at volume — triggers spam detection. Warm-up costs you a few days but saves weeks of fighting low distribution.

Does warm-up guarantee my videos will go viral?

No. Warm-up ensures your account is not flagged as spam and that the algorithm understands your niche. It gives your content a fair shot at the test audience. Whether a video goes viral depends on the content itself — hook quality, watch time, and engagement. Warm-up removes the platform penalty, not the creative challenge.

Do I need to warm up every new account separately?

Yes. Each account is evaluated independently by TikTok. Warm-up activity on one account does not transfer to another, even on the same device. If you are running multiple creator accounts for a brand, each one needs its own warm-up cycle with its own niche-specific engagement pattern.

What happens if my account gets shadowbanned during warm-up?

If you followed the protocol and still see suppressed reach, stop all activity for 24 to 48 hours. Then resume normal browsing — no posting — for another 2 to 3 days. Most shadowbans on new accounts lift within a week. If the account remains restricted after 10 days, it is usually faster to start fresh with a new account on a clean device and email.
