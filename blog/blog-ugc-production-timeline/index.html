How Long Does UGC Production Take? Timeline From Brief to Live Ads [2026]

Week-by-week UGC production timeline: from brief and strategy to creator filming, editing, and ad delivery. What speeds things up, what slows them down, and realistic timelines for statics, video, and AI-assisted content.

Guide8 min readby the OKAD team

Six out of ten brands that book a call with us ask the same question within the first five minutes: how long does this take?

The honest answer: 2-3 weeks for the first batch of UGC ads. 7-10 days for every batch after that. 5-7 business days for statics only. And 24-48 hours for AI-assisted static content.

But those numbers mean nothing without understanding what happens during each phase, what determines whether your project lands at the fast end or the slow end, and what you can do as a client to keep things moving.

Here is the full timeline, broken down by week and by deliverable type.

The Standard UGC Timeline: First Batch

The first batch always takes longer than subsequent ones. This is not padding — it is the strategy and setup work that makes every future batch faster.

Week 1: Strategy, Audit, and Creator Casting

Days 1-2: Brief and strategy. You share your brief — product details, target audience, key messages, existing ads that work, competitive landscape. We turn this into a creative strategy document: which angles to test, what formats to produce, and the hook concepts that will lead each ad.

If you have existing performance data (winning ads, audience insights, CPA benchmarks), this phase moves faster. If we are starting from zero, we run a competitor audit to identify what is already working in your category.

Days 3-5: Creator casting. We match creators to your brand based on demographics, content style, and category experience. This includes reviewing portfolios, checking availability, and confirming rates. For most projects, we cast 2-5 creators from our network of vetted talent.

Creator casting is often the first bottleneck. If a specific ethnicity, location, or look is required — and the creator pool for that criteria is small — this phase can take an extra 2-3 days.

What you owe at this stage: Approved brief, product samples shipped to creators, brand assets (logos, fonts, color codes), and approval of the creative strategy document.

Week 2: Scripting and Filming

Days 6-8: Script writing and approval. We write scripts based on the approved creative strategy. Each script follows the hook-body-CTA structure optimized for paid social. You review and approve — or request changes.

This is the most common delay point. When clients take 3-5 days to approve scripts instead of 1-2 days, the entire timeline shifts by that same amount. Fast approvals are the single biggest accelerator in UGC production.

Days 8-12: Creator filming. Approved scripts go to creators with detailed shot lists, brand guidelines, and reference examples. Creators film and submit raw footage within 3-5 days. Professional creators with established workflows deliver in 2-3 days. Newer creators sometimes need an extra day or two.

What you owe at this stage: Script approvals within 24-48 hours. Every day of delayed approval adds a day to the delivery timeline.

Week 3: Editing, Review, and Delivery

Days 13-15: First cut editing. Raw footage is edited into finished ads: color correction, text overlays, captions, music, sound design, and format optimization for each platform (9:16 for Reels/TikTok, 1:1 for feed, 4:5 for Stories).

Days 15-17: Client review and revisions. You review the first cuts. Most projects include 2 rounds of revisions. Common revision requests: text changes, music swaps, pacing adjustments, alternate hooks. Structural changes (re-shooting, adding new scenes) take longer and may require a revision fee.

Days 17-18: Final delivery. Approved ads are exported in all required formats and delivered via shared drive. Files include finished ads, clean versions (no text overlays), and raw footage if requested.

Subsequent Batches: Why They Are Faster

Every batch after the first takes 7-10 business days instead of 14-21. Here is why:

Strategy is established. The creative strategy, brand voice, and testing framework from Batch 1 carry forward. We are iterating on proven angles, not starting from zero.

Creators are cast and vetted. The creators who performed well in Batch 1 are on retainer. No casting phase needed — scripts go directly to proven talent.

Brand guidelines are documented. Editing templates, text styles, music preferences, and format specs are saved from Batch 1. The editing team produces consistent output without re-learning the brand each time.

Client knows the process. Approval cycles get faster as clients learn what to look for and how to give actionable feedback. First-batch reviews often take 3-5 days. By Batch 3, most clients approve within 24 hours.

What Slows Things Down

In three years of high-volume UGC production, the same bottlenecks appear on every delayed project:

1. Slow client approvals. This is the number one cause of timeline slippage. A 3-day delay on script approval pushes filming back 3 days, which pushes editing back 3 days. What should be a 3-week project becomes a 5-week project with zero production delays — just approval delays.

2. Unclear briefs. When the brief is vague (“make it fun and engaging”), the strategy phase takes longer because we need to extract specific direction through back-and-forth. A detailed creative brief with target audience, key messages, and reference examples cuts the strategy phase in half.

3. Specific creator requirements. “We need a male creator, 35-45, British accent, with a dog, who looks like he shops at Waitrose” — requirements like this narrow the casting pool dramatically. Every constraint adds days to the casting phase. Flexibility on non-essential criteria speeds everything up.

4. Product shipping delays. Creators cannot film with a product they have not received. International shipping adds 5-10 days. Plan for this — ship samples the day you sign the contract, not after scripts are approved.

5. Scope changes mid-production. Adding new deliverables, changing creative direction after filming starts, or requesting re-shoots mid-edit resets the timeline. Define scope clearly upfront and save new ideas for the next batch.

What Speeds Things Up

Conversely, projects that deliver ahead of schedule share common traits:

Existing brand assets. Logos, fonts, color codes, product images, and brand guidelines ready on Day 1 eliminate 1-2 days of asset collection.

Previous winning ads. Sharing your top-performing ads gives us a head start on strategy. We reverse-engineer what works and build on it instead of starting from scratch.

Pre-approved scripts. Some clients write their own scripts or have internal copywriters draft them. When scripts arrive approved, we skip straight to casting and filming — saving 3-5 days.

AI-assisted production. For static ads and some video formats, AI tools compress the production timeline dramatically. What takes a designer 2-3 days to produce manually takes 2-3 hours with AI-assisted workflows. This is why AI-generated ad content is the fastest-growing format in our production mix.

Standing creator relationships. Retainer clients with established creator teams skip casting entirely. Scripts go directly to creators who already know the brand, have product on hand, and can film within 48 hours of receiving a brief.

Rush Delivery: When You Need Ads This Week

Sometimes you cannot wait 2-3 weeks. Product launch this Friday. Competitor just launched a campaign you need to respond to. Holiday season starting and the creative pipeline is empty.

Rush delivery compresses the standard timeline to 5-7 business days for video and 2-3 days for statics. Here is how it works:

  • Rush fee: 25-50% premium on production cost
  • Compressed strategy: Streamlined brief, no full competitor audit
  • Pre-vetted creators only: No casting search, immediate assignment from existing network
  • Parallel editing: Editing begins on first-delivered footage while remaining creators are still filming
  • Single revision round: One round of changes, 24-hour turnaround

Rush delivery works best when the brand has clear direction and existing assets. It does not work well for brands that need extensive strategy or are producing their first-ever ad campaign.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to get UGC ads from an agency?

The first batch of UGC ads typically takes 2-3 weeks from brief to delivery. This includes strategy and competitor audit (3-5 days), creator casting and scripting (3-5 days), filming by creators (3-5 days), and editing plus revisions (3-5 days). Subsequent batches on an ongoing retainer take 7-10 days because the strategy, brand guidelines, and creator relationships are already established. Static-only deliverables can be produced in 5-7 business days.

Can I get UGC ads faster than 2 weeks?

Yes. Rush delivery in 5-7 business days is possible with a 25-50% rush fee. This works by compressing the strategy phase (using a streamlined brief), casting from a pre-vetted creator pool, and running editing in parallel with filming. AI-assisted static ads can be delivered in 24-48 hours from brief to final files. The fastest path is always statics — if you need ads live this week, start with static formats.

What do I need to provide to start UGC production?

At minimum: product samples or access to your service, brand guidelines or existing ad examples you like, target audience description, key selling points, and any specific messaging requirements. The more preparation you provide upfront, the faster production moves. The biggest delay in most projects is waiting for client approval on scripts or creative direction — fast approvals mean fast delivery.

How often should I get new UGC content?

Most brands on Meta and TikTok need fresh creative every 2-4 weeks to stay ahead of ad fatigue. Brands spending over $10,000 per month on ads typically need new batches every 2 weeks. The ideal setup is a monthly retainer that delivers 10-30 new creatives per cycle, with a mix of new concepts and variations on proven winners. This keeps the ad account fed without production gaps.
