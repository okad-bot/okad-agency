AI Video Ads vs UGC Ads: Which Performs Better?

AI video ads vs UGC performance data from $471K in ad spend. When AI statics beat UGC by 11% on CPA, when UGC still wins, and a framework for choosing.

Guide8 min readby the OKAD team

The AI-versus-UGC debate has devolved into two camps shouting past each other. AI evangelists claim UGC is dead. UGC defenders insist AI creatives are soulless. Neither camp is showing their data.

We have both. Across campaigns totaling over $471K in spend, 695 ads tested, and projects spanning AI video, AI product photography, UGC creator content, and hybrid approaches, we have direct performance comparisons. The answer is not what either camp wants to hear.

The Data: $471K in Ad Spend, Head-to-Head

The cleanest comparison comes from InstaHeadshots — an AI headshot app where we ran both AI-generated static ads and UGC video ads simultaneously, at scale.

Campaign overview:

  • Total spend: $471,970
  • Total ads: 695
  • Formats tested: AI-generated statics, UGC videos, hybrid formats
  • Platform: Meta (Facebook + Instagram)

AI statics outperformed UGC on cost per acquisition by 11%. The top-performing AI concept hit ROAS 2.26. At nearly half a million dollars in spend, this is not a fluke — it is a statistically significant result across hundreds of ads.

But before declaring AI the winner, look at why it won in this specific case.

Why AI Won for InstaHeadshots (and Might Not for You)

InstaHeadshots is an AI headshot app. The product literally generates AI images. Showing AI-generated headshots in the ad is a live product demo — the creative is the proof. This creates a structural advantage for AI creatives that does not exist for most products.

Three factors drove the AI advantage:

1. Speed of message delivery. A static image communicates the before/after transformation in under one second. UGC video required 3-5 seconds to deliver the same message. On Meta, where the average thumb-stop window is 1.5 seconds, that speed advantage compounds across millions of impressions.

2. Testing volume. We could produce 50 AI static variations in the time it took to produce 5 UGC videos. More variations meant faster iteration toward winning concepts. By the time the UGC batch was refined, AI statics had already gone through three optimization cycles.

3. Product-format alignment. When your product is AI-generated images, the ad format and the product are the same thing. The viewer sees the ad and thinks “that looks good — I want that for my LinkedIn profile.” There is no trust gap to bridge.

When UGC Still Wins: The Lookalikey Data

For Lookalikey, a dating app, we produced 9 UGC videos with 3 creators. The result: ROAS 2.20.

Dating apps require social proof. A real person saying “I found someone on this app” is fundamentally more persuasive than any AI-generated visual. The product’s value proposition is inherently personal — it cannot be demonstrated with a product shot or animation.

The pattern holds across other campaigns:

SmallTalk2Me (language learning app): 2-4M impressions per video creative. UGC-style content showing real people using the app drove engagement. The product requires showing human interaction — a person speaking, getting feedback, improving. AI cannot replicate genuine human struggle and progress.

Health and supplement brands: Trust-dependent categories where the buyer needs to believe a real person experienced a real result. UGC from actual users outperforms polished production in these verticals every time.

The Volume Advantage Reverses for UGC

There is a counterintuitive dynamic here. SmallTalk2Me produced 12 posts and 30 videos per month — a volume that would seem to favor AI production. But the content required real human interaction with the product, and the 2-4M impressions per video creative justified the investment in creator production.

When individual UGC videos drive millions of impressions, the per-impression cost of creator content drops to a level where AI’s cost advantage disappears. The cost calculation only favors AI when you are producing high volumes of content that each gets moderate distribution. For content that goes deep rather than wide, UGC often wins on economics too.

The Decision Framework: AI vs UGC vs Hybrid

After running these campaigns, we use a three-variable framework to decide the format mix for any new client:

Variable 1: Trust Requirement

How much does the purchase decision depend on believing a real person’s experience?

  • Low trust required → AI-favored. Products where the product itself is the proof: apps with visual output, physical goods, tools, software. The buyer evaluates the product directly, not through someone else’s experience.
  • High trust required → UGC-favored. Health, finance, dating, personal care, education. The buyer needs to believe the outcome is achievable for them personally. A real person’s story bridges that gap.

Variable 2: Comprehension Speed

How quickly can the value proposition be communicated?

  • Instant comprehension → Statics/AI-favored. Before/after, product beauty shots, simple comparisons. If the viewer gets it in under 2 seconds, a static or short AI animation is more efficient than a 15-second UGC video.
  • Requires explanation → Video/UGC-favored. Complex products, multi-step solutions, products with non-obvious benefits. These need a narrator or demonstrator to walk the viewer through the value.

Variable 3: Variation Volume Needed

How many creative variations does your testing cadence require?

  • High volume (40+ variations/cycle) → AI-favored. At this volume, UGC production costs become prohibitive. We produced 80 fresh ads from old footage for Five Brands and 60+ ad variations for ZenToes — both would have been economically impossible with traditional UGC production.
  • Low volume (5-15 variations/cycle) → Format-neutral. At lower volumes, the per-asset cost difference between AI and UGC is less significant than the performance difference.

The Hybrid Model: Best of Both

The most sophisticated advertisers do not choose between AI and UGC. They deploy both strategically within the same funnel.

Prospecting (top of funnel): AI creatives. High volume, fast testing, rapid iteration. The goal is thumb-stopping and click-through, not deep persuasion. AI’s volume advantage matters most here — you need to test dozens of hooks, compositions, and value propositions to find what resonates with cold audiences.

Retargeting (middle/bottom of funnel): UGC. The viewer already knows the product. Now they need trust, social proof, and emotional connection. A real creator sharing their experience converts warm audiences more effectively than another product shot.

Scaling winners: Whichever format won at the testing stage gets scaled — regardless of whether it is AI or UGC. We have seen AI statics scale to six figures in spend, and we have seen UGC videos do the same. The format is less important than the message-market fit.

But production cost is not the right metric. Cost per conversion at scale is what matters.

A $500 UGC video that converts 2x better than a $50 AI static is the better investment. Conversely, producing 60 AI variations to find one that converts at $54 CPA is more profitable than producing 5 UGC videos that average $61 CPA — because you found the winner faster and spent less total budget on testing.

The real savings from AI are not in per-asset production cost. They are in testing velocity — the ability to iterate through more concepts faster and converge on winners with less wasted spend.

The Testing Velocity Advantage in Practice

Testing velocity deserves its own section because it is the most underrated factor in the AI-versus-UGC decision.

Consider two scenarios for a brand spending $30K per month on Meta ads:

Scenario A (UGC-only): You produce 8-10 new UGC videos per month. Each requires briefing, creator matching, filming, revision, and approval. Timeline: 2-3 weeks from brief to deployed ad. You run one testing cycle per month with 8-10 new concepts.

Scenario B (AI + UGC hybrid): You produce 5 UGC videos and 40 AI variations per month. The UGC takes the same 2-3 weeks. The AI variations are ready in 2-3 days. You run 3-4 testing cycles per month, with each cycle informing the next.

After three months, Scenario B has tested 135 concepts versus Scenario A’s 30. The probability of finding a breakout winner — the kind that scales to six figures in spend — is fundamentally higher with more shots on goal. This is not theoretical. It is how we achieved the $54.45 CPA on InstaHeadshots — through rapid iteration that would have been impossible at UGC production speed alone.

What the Data Actually Shows

After nearly half a million dollars in directly comparable spend, plus campaigns across a dozen other brands:

  1. AI wins on production efficiency and testing velocity. When you need volume, variation, and speed, AI is the clear choice.
  2. UGC wins on trust and emotional connection. When the purchase decision is personal, when the buyer needs to see themselves in someone else’s story, real creators outperform.
  3. The format matters less than the strategy. A well-art-directed AI creative beats a lazy UGC video. A genuine, well-briefed creator beats a generic AI product shot. Direction quality outweighs format choice.
  4. Hybrid outperforms single-format. The best-performing ad accounts use both — AI for volume and testing, UGC for trust and conversion. Running one format exclusively leaves performance on the table.
  5. Product-format fit determines the winner. The 11% CPA advantage for AI statics on InstaHeadshots does not mean AI statics will beat UGC for your supplement brand. Match the format to your product’s trust requirement and comprehension speed.

The question is not “AI or UGC?” It is “what ratio of AI to UGC does our specific product, audience, and funnel stage require?” The answer is always a number, not a side.

Questions people ask

Do AI ads perform better than UGC ads?

It depends on the product and format. In our InstaHeadshots campaign ($471K spend, 695 ads), AI statics beat UGC on CPA by 11% — $54.45 vs $61.01. But for Lookalikey, UGC creator videos achieved ROAS 2.20, outperforming non-creator formats. The winner depends on whether your product needs demonstration or social proof.

What is the cost difference between AI ads and UGC ads?

AI creative production costs 60-80% less per asset. AI product images run under $700 for 40+ images vs $3-8K per traditional shoot. UGC creator videos cost $150-500 per video. But cost per asset is the wrong metric — cost per conversion at scale is what matters. A $500 UGC video that converts at 2x the rate of a $50 AI ad is the better investment.

When should I use AI ads vs UGC ads?

Use AI for product-centric visuals (product photography, lifestyle scenes, before/after demos), high-volume testing where you need 50+ variations, and categories where the product is the proof (apps, tools, physical goods). Use UGC for trust-dependent purchases (health, finance, personal care), story-driven products, and when social proof drives the buying decision.

Can I use both AI and UGC ads in the same campaign?

Yes, and you should. The highest-performing ad accounts we manage use AI creatives for prospecting (broad audiences, high volume, fast testing) and UGC for retargeting (building trust with warm audiences who need a final push). Running both in the same ad set also gives the algorithm more creative diversity to optimize across.

How many ad variations do I need for effective testing?

At minimum 10-15 variations to reach statistical significance on any single variable. For serious creative testing at $15K+ monthly spend, we produce 40-80 variations per testing cycle. AI makes this volume feasible — producing 60+ ad variations without new footage, as we did for ZenToes, is only practical with AI-assisted production.
