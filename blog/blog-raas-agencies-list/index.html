RaaS Agencies: Who Offers Results-Based Creative Pricing [2026]

A guide to Results-as-a-Service agencies in 2026. How RaaS pricing works for creative production, what to look for in a RaaS partner, and how to evaluate whether the model fits your brand.

Guide5 min readby the OKAD team

The retainer model has dominated creative agencies for decades: pay a fixed monthly fee, receive a defined scope of work. It is predictable for both sides. It is also misaligned — the agency earns the same whether your ads perform or not.

Results as a Service (RaaS) fixes this alignment problem. Instead of paying for time or a fixed scope, you pay for measurable output: deliverables produced, performance targets met, or business outcomes achieved.

This guide covers how to find and evaluate RaaS agencies for creative production, what the pricing models actually look like, and when the model makes sense for your brand.

How RaaS Works for Creative Production

Traditional creative agency pricing:

“We charge $8,000 per month. That includes strategy, 15 ad concepts, and reporting.”

RaaS creative agency pricing:

“We charge $350 per finished ad concept. Strategy and reporting are included in the per-unit price. You decide the volume each month.”

The shift seems small, but it changes incentives fundamentally. Under a retainer, the agency maximizes margin by delivering the minimum scope. Under RaaS, the agency maximizes revenue by delivering more high-quality work.

Per-Deliverable Model

The most common and practical RaaS structure. You pay a fixed price per finished concept — typically $200-$500 for UGC-style ads and $500-$1,500 for produced creative.

This model works immediately because the metric is simple: a finished, approved deliverable. No baseline data needed. No attribution debate.

Performance Bonus Model

A reduced base retainer (typically 40-60% of the standard rate) plus performance bonuses tied to specific metrics. Example: $4,000 base plus $200 per concept that beats the control in a structured test.

This requires at least 2-3 months of baseline data to set fair bonus thresholds. Without baselines, bonus targets become either too easy (agency gets overpaid) or impossible (agency stops trying).

Pure Performance Model

The agency earns only when ads hit defined KPIs — typically a CPA threshold or ROAS target. This model is rare because it puts all financial risk on the agency. Most agencies will only offer it to clients they have already worked with and trust.

What to Look for in a RaaS Agency

1. Transparent Unit Economics

The agency should be able to explain exactly what each deliverable costs and what is included. A per-concept price of $400 should cover: creative strategy (angle selection, brief writing), creator sourcing, production, editing, and 1-2 revision rounds. If strategy is a separate line item on top of per-unit pricing, you are paying twice.

2. Defined Quality Standards

When payment is per deliverable, there is a risk of the agency prioritizing volume over quality. Counter this with clear quality standards: minimum production specifications, approval criteria, and a rejection process for substandard work.

3. Testing Infrastructure

A RaaS agency should be testing what it delivers. The supplement UGC ad testing project at OKAD produced 48 ad variations across 8 angles from 16 creator videos — with problem-callout hooks winning by 40% over product-first hooks. That kind of structured testing is what separates a RaaS agency that delivers concepts from one that delivers results.

4. Iteration Process

What happens when a concept underperforms? A strong RaaS agency diagnoses the failure (hook, body, CTA, or audience) and produces iterations at reduced cost. At OKAD, the gut health brand campaign tested red-flag messaging hooks against benefit-first hooks across 4 story formats — each monthly edit batch got its full testing window before kill-or-scale decisions.

5. Flexible Volume

The whole point of RaaS is flexibility. The agency should accommodate months where you need 30 concepts and months where you need 10, without penalties or minimum commitments.

When RaaS Makes Sense

Switch to RaaS when:

  • Your content needs fluctuate month to month
  • You want to test an agency relationship without a long-term commitment
  • You have clear metrics for what “good” looks like (CPA targets, ROAS floors)
  • Your current retainer delivers inconsistent volume or quality
  • You want tighter accountability on creative output

Stay on a retainer when:

  • You need consistent, high-volume output every month (30+ concepts)
  • Your agency relationship is already strong and producing results
  • You value the priority scheduling and dedicated resources a retainer provides
  • Your budget is large enough that per-unit pricing would cost more than a retainer

How to Transition From Retainer to RaaS

  1. Track current output for 2-3 months: concepts delivered, cost per concept, performance metrics.
  2. Calculate your effective per-unit cost under the retainer. If you pay $10,000/month and receive 20 concepts, your effective cost is $500/concept.
  3. Propose a hybrid as a transition: reduce the base retainer by 40-50% and add a per-deliverable fee for additional concepts. This gives both sides a transition period.
  4. Shift to full RaaS after 2-3 months of hybrid, once per-unit pricing is validated and the agency has adjusted its resource planning.

Questions people ask

What is a RaaS agency?

A RaaS (Results as a Service) agency ties its pricing to measurable outcomes rather than hours worked or a fixed monthly fee. Instead of paying a $10,000 retainer regardless of output, you pay per deliverable, per performance milestone, or per conversion-linked result. The model aligns the agency's revenue with your business outcomes — they earn more when they deliver more value.

How does RaaS pricing work for creative agencies?

RaaS pricing for creative agencies typically takes three forms: per-deliverable (pay per finished ad concept), performance-bonus (base fee plus bonus tied to CPA or ROAS targets), or pure performance (pay only when ads hit defined KPIs). The per-deliverable model is most common because it is easiest to measure and agree on. Pure performance models are rare and usually reserved for established agency-client relationships with at least 3 months of baseline data.

Is RaaS pricing more expensive than a retainer?

Per-unit costs are typically 15-30% higher under RaaS than under a retainer. But total cost is often lower because you eliminate waste — you never pay for idle capacity or underperforming months. A $10,000 retainer costs $10,000 whether the agency delivers 30 concepts or 10. A RaaS model at $400 per concept costs $4,000 for 10 concepts and $12,000 for 30 — you pay for what you actually use.

When should I switch from a retainer to RaaS?

Switch when you have at least 2-3 months of baseline data on your current retainer's output — concepts delivered, performance metrics, cost per deliverable. This data lets you set fair per-unit or per-outcome pricing. Also switch if your content needs are variable (high-volume months alternating with quiet periods) or if you want tighter accountability on creative performance.

What are the risks of the RaaS model for brands?

The main risk is that the agency cherry-picks safe, proven concepts rather than testing experimental creative that could be a breakthrough. When compensation is tied to per-deliverable metrics, there is less incentive to invest time in risky angles. Mitigate this by including an innovation component in the agreement — a portion of the budget specifically allocated to experimental concepts with different success criteria.
