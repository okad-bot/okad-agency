How to Start Faceless UGC: No-Camera Creator Guide (2026)

Start creating faceless UGC with zero on-camera experience. Equipment list, first content formats, where to find jobs, and how to build a portfolio without showing your face.

For creators6 min readby the OKAD team

You want to create UGC content but do not want to be on camera. Good news: faceless UGC is one of the fastest-growing content formats, and brands are actively looking for creators who can produce it.

This guide walks through everything: what you need, what to create, where to find work, and how to build a portfolio — all without showing your face.

What Faceless UGC Actually Is

Faceless UGC is user-generated content where the creator does not appear on camera. Instead, the video shows:

  • Hands using or demonstrating a product
  • A screen recording of an app or website
  • Close-up product footage with text overlays
  • ASMR-style sensory content
  • Before/after transformations
  • Overhead or POV process videos

The creator provides authenticity through their perspective, voiceover, text, or the way they interact with the product. The face is not needed for that authenticity to come through.

OKAD produced 1,257 faceless videos for Skribly (a drawing app) — all POV drawing content with voiceover. The result: 1.5M views and 2,000 app registrations. No creator face appeared in a single video. Read the full Skribly case study for the complete production strategy and results.

Equipment You Need

Minimum Setup ($0)

  • Your smartphone (iPhone 12+ or equivalent Android)
  • A window for natural lighting
  • A clean, clutter-free surface

This is enough to start. Do not let equipment be an excuse not to begin.

Recommended Setup ($50–$95)

  • Phone tripod with overhead arm — $15–$30. Essential for flat-lay and POV shots. Look for one with a flexible gooseneck or boom arm.
  • Ring light or LED panel — $20–$40. Consistent lighting is the single biggest quality improvement. Natural light is free but unpredictable.
  • Clip-on lavalier mic — $15–$25. For voiceover. Plugs into your phone. Massive audio quality jump over the built-in microphone.

Nice-to-Have ($50–$150 extra)

  • Phone stabilizer/gimbal for smooth handheld POV
  • Small lightbox for product photography
  • Backdrop paper or cloth for clean backgrounds
  • Screen recording software (OBS is free)

Total startup cost: $50–$245. Compare this to on-camera UGC where you might invest in ring lights, camera, wardrobe, and grooming per shoot.

Five Faceless Formats to Start With

Pick one or two to master before expanding. Trying all five at once will produce mediocre content across the board.

1. Hands-On Product Demo

Show your hands using a product — applying it, opening it, assembling it, demonstrating features. Film from slightly above your hands or use a POV chest-mount angle.

Difficulty: Easy Production time: 15–30 minutes Best for: Beauty, skincare, food, physical products

2. Screen Recording

Record your phone or computer screen as you walk through an app, website, or tool. Add voiceover or text annotations explaining what you are doing.

Difficulty: Easy Production time: 15–20 minutes Best for: Apps, SaaS, digital tools, online platforms

3. Overhead Process

Mount your phone overhead and film a process from above — cooking, drawing, organizing, unpacking. This is the format that worked for Skribly at scale.

Difficulty: Medium (requires overhead mount) Production time: 20–40 minutes Best for: Food, art, crafts, organizational products

4. ASMR / Sensory

Film close-up footage emphasizing textures, sounds, and visual satisfaction. Tapping, crinkling, pouring, peeling. Minimal or no talking.

Difficulty: Medium (requires good audio) Production time: 15–30 minutes Best for: Food, beauty, packaging, satisfying products

5. Text-Over-B-Roll

Film 5–10 seconds of B-roll footage of a product in context, then add text overlays telling a story or listing benefits. The easiest format to produce at volume.

Difficulty: Easy Production time: 10–20 minutes Best for: Any product category

Building Your Portfolio Without Showing Your Face

You need work samples before brands will hire you. Here is how to build a portfolio in one weekend:

Step 1: Pick 3 Products You Already Own

Choose products with visual appeal — skincare, a kitchen gadget, a tech accessory, a food item. Do not buy anything new.

Step 2: Create 2 Videos Per Product

Use different formats for each. For example:

  • Skincare: one hands-on application video, one ASMR texture video
  • Kitchen gadget: one overhead process video, one text-over-B-roll

That gives you 6 portfolio pieces.

Step 3: Edit to 15–30 Seconds Each

Short, punchy, well-lit. Use CapCut (free) for basic editing, text overlays, and transitions.

Step 4: Compile a Portfolio Reel

Stitch your best 3–4 clips into a 45–60 second reel. This is what you send to brands and put on your profiles.

Step 5: Post Organically

Post your portfolio videos on TikTok and Instagram. Tag the brands. Some brands discover creators this way, and even small view counts prove you can produce content.

Where to Find Faceless UGC Jobs

UGC Platforms

  • Collabstr — Filter by “product demo” and “hands-only” briefs
  • Billo — Many product-focused briefs that do not require face-to-camera
  • JoinBrands — Growing platform with faceless-friendly categories
  • Insense — Brands post briefs; many accept faceless submissions

Job Boards

  • Search “UGC creator” on Indeed, LinkedIn, and remote job boards
  • Look for keywords: “product demo,” “screen recording,” “content creator (no face required)”

Direct Outreach

  • DM brands on Instagram whose products you use
  • Email small e-commerce brands offering a test video
  • Join creator communities on Discord and Reddit where brands post needs

Pro Tip: Specialize Early

Brands hiring for faceless UGC usually need a specific format. If you become the go-to creator for “overhead cooking demos” or “app screen recordings,” you will get repeat work faster than a generalist.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Bad lighting. This kills faceless content faster than anything. Your hands, the product, and the surface need to be evenly lit with no harsh shadows. A $20 ring light solves this.

Shaky footage. Without a face to focus on, the viewer’s eye catches every wobble. Use a tripod or mount for stable shots. If shooting handheld, brace your elbows against your body.

Cluttered background. The product is the star. A messy desk, visible clutter, or patterned surface distracts. Use a clean white, black, or neutral surface.

No hook. Faceless videos need to grab attention in the first 0.5 seconds. Start with the most visually interesting moment — the final result, an unexpected action, or a bold text overlay. Do not start with setup.

Ignoring audio. Even if you are not doing voiceover, audio matters. Background noise, HVAC hum, or tinny phone-mic sound makes content feel amateur. Record in a quiet room. Use a clip-on mic for voiceover.

Questions people ask

Can I do UGC without showing my face?

Yes. Faceless UGC is a legitimate and growing content format. Brands like Skribly, which OKAD helped generate 1.5M views for, use exclusively faceless content. You show hands, products, screens, or environments instead of your face — with voiceover or text overlays.

What equipment do I need for faceless UGC?

Minimum: a smartphone and natural lighting. Recommended: a phone tripod with overhead mount ($15–$30), a ring light ($20–$40), and a clip-on microphone ($15–$25) for voiceover. Total startup cost: $50–$95. No professional camera needed.

How do I find faceless UGC jobs?

Search UGC job boards for briefs mentioning 'product demo,' 'hands-only,' 'screen recording,' or 'ASMR.' Platforms like Collabstr, Billo, and JoinBrands list faceless-friendly briefs. You can also pitch brands directly by sending a faceless portfolio reel.

How much do faceless UGC creators earn?

Beginner faceless UGC creators earn $30–$100 per video. Experienced creators earn $100–$250 per video. The lower per-asset rate is offset by faster production — faceless videos take 15–30 minutes instead of 1–2 hours for on-camera UGC.

Do I need voiceover skills for faceless UGC?

Not always. Many faceless formats use text overlays or ambient sound instead of voiceover. If you do voiceover, you do not need a professional voice — natural, conversational tone works best. Practice with 5–10 short scripts and your voice will improve quickly.
