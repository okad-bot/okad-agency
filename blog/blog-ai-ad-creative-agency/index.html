AI Ad Creative Agency: How to Choose One That Delivers

A buyer's guide for hiring an AI ad creative agency. What to ask, red flags to avoid, and how to evaluate volume, testing, and real case data.

Guide10 min readby the OKAD team

The market for AI ad creative agencies has exploded. A search for “AI creative agency” returns hundreds of options, most of which launched in the last 18 months. Some are rebranded freelancers with a ChatGPT subscription. Others are serious production operations that have been building AI-assisted workflows for years.

This guide helps you tell the difference. Whether you are a CMO evaluating agencies, a growth lead building your vendor shortlist, or a brand manager with a brief and a budget, here is what to look for, what to avoid, and how to make the right hire.

Why the Agency You Choose Matters More Now

When ad creative was purely human-made, quality was relatively easy to evaluate. You looked at a reel, assessed the craft, and made a call. AI-assisted creative changes the equation because the bottleneck shifts from production to methodology.

Any agency can generate 50 images with AI tools. The question is whether those 50 images are strategically varied, properly tested, and systematically iterated. The workflow behind the output matters more than the output itself.

This is why choosing the right agency is harder — and more consequential — than it used to be. A bad traditional agency wastes your budget on mediocre ads. A bad AI agency wastes it on high volumes of mediocre ads, burning through your audience faster.

The Five Capabilities That Matter

After working with brands across skincare, supplements, dating apps, baby products, and consumer tech, a pattern emerges in what separates agencies that deliver from those that do not.

1. Volume With Structure

The first question: can the agency produce at scale, and is that scale structured or chaotic?

Structured volume means every ad variation exists for a reason — testing a different hook, angle, format, or audience segment. Chaotic volume means they are generating dozens of random variations and hoping something sticks.

A useful benchmark: for a Swiss skincare brand, OKAD’s production system generates roughly 30 ads per day from a single UGC library. That volume is not random — it is built on a Hook Swap System where underperforming hooks get replaced while the proven ad body stays intact. The result was 2-3 months of stable orders with consistent creative supply.

Ask any agency you are evaluating: “How do you decide what variations to produce, and how do you iterate on underperformers?” If the answer is vague, keep looking.

2. Testing Methodology

Volume without testing is just waste. A good AI creative agency has a defined approach to creative testing — how they structure tests, what metrics they optimize for, and how winning patterns feed the next production cycle.

Key questions to ask:

  • How do you isolate variables when testing ad variations?
  • What is your process for identifying and improving hook rates?
  • How do test results inform the next batch of creatives?
  • What does your reporting look like?

The longevity supplement campaign is a good example of what testing methodology looks like in practice. OKAD produced 68 ads using a story-before-product format that delivered EUR 183 CPA. That format was not the first approach — it emerged from structured testing of multiple narrative angles, with the story-led format consistently outperforming product-first alternatives.

3. Real Campaign Data

This is the fastest filter. Ask for case studies with actual numbers — CPAs, ROAS, view counts, conversion metrics. Not mockups. Not “projected results.” Not screenshots of AI tools.

Here is what real data looks like:

  • Dating app (DateMyAge): 24 scripts, 22 creators, 48 videos — a systematic scripting operation, not ad-hoc content
  • Drawing app: 1.5M organic views from faceless UGC — proving the format works without creator dependency
  • YourChristianDate: 800K+ views, 60 videos per month — sustained volume using a b-roll system
  • Consumer tech (Canvas): 5,471 creators sourced, 81.8K views on the best Reel — scale at the creator recruitment level
  • Alcohol brand (Godka): 900K views from bartender UGC content

If an agency cannot show you numbers like these — with specific ad counts, performance metrics, and production details — they are selling capability, not results.

4. Hybrid AI-Plus-Human Approach

The best AI creative agencies do not replace human creativity with AI. They use AI to amplify human creative direction. This means a real creative strategist writes the brief. A real editor composes the key frames. AI handles the repetitive scaling — variations, resizing, animation, background generation.

Red flag: if the agency’s pitch is primarily about the AI tools they use, they are probably tool-dependent rather than strategy-driven. The tools change every six months. The creative methodology should not.

For a deeper look at how hybrid approaches outperform pure-AI production, see our analysis of AI video ads versus UGC and the hybrid AI-human content model.

5. Asset Reuse and Zero-Shoot Capability

One of the biggest advantages of working with an AI creative agency is the ability to produce new ads without new shoots. This dramatically reduces cost and turnaround time — but only if the agency has a system for it.

OKAD produced 80 fresh ads from existing creator footage across 5 brands — zero new shoots required. For ZenToes’ Walmart launch, 60+ ad variations came from existing footage, delivering 11 videos and 14 static ads.

Ask potential agencies: “If I give you our existing footage library, how many new ads can you produce from it?” The answer reveals whether they have a real re-edit workflow or just a standard production pipeline.

Red Flags to Watch For

Knowing what to avoid is as important as knowing what to look for.

“We use [insert AI tool name]” — Tools are commodities. Anyone can access the same AI models. What matters is the creative process wrapped around those tools. An agency whose pitch centers on Midjourney or Runway is like a construction company bragging about their hammer brand.

No performance data — If every case study is “we produced X assets” with no campaign metrics, they have never been accountable for results. Production volume without performance outcomes is just a content factory.

One-size-fits-all packages — AI creative needs vary dramatically by vertical, channel, and growth stage. An agency offering the same package to a DTC skincare brand and a dating app is not thinking about your business. Good agencies ask about your unit economics before proposing creative volume.

No testing framework — “We A/B test everything” is not a testing framework. A real framework specifies how variables are isolated, how sample sizes are determined, and how learnings feed production. Ask them to walk you through a specific test they ran and what they changed as a result.

Pure AI with no human oversight — Agencies that automate everything including creative direction produce generic work. AI content looks fake when there is no human taste layer. The best agencies use AI for scale and humans for judgment.

Slow turnaround despite AI claims — If an agency says they use AI but still takes 3-4 weeks per creative batch, they have bolted AI tools onto a traditional workflow rather than building an AI-native process.

No vertical experience — An agency that has only produced AI creatives for one industry may struggle with yours. Different verticals have different compliance requirements, audience expectations, and creative conventions. Check whether they have worked across multiple categories.

An agency that scores 4-5 on volume, testing, and data is worth a serious conversation. An agency that scores below 3 on any of those three is unlikely to deliver meaningful results regardless of how impressive their demo reel looks.

Pricing Models You Will Encounter

AI creative agencies typically use one of four pricing structures:

  • Per-asset pricing — $50-$500 per video, $0.10-$3 per AI image. Transparent but can incentivize volume over quality. Works well for defined campaigns with clear deliverables.
  • Monthly retainer — $3-15K/month depending on volume and complexity. Better for ongoing creative supply. The best retainers include a minimum asset count plus testing and iteration.
  • Performance-based — Agency takes a percentage of ad spend or a bonus tied to CPA/ROAS. Aligns incentives but requires trust and transparency on both sides. Rare in pure-play AI creative agencies.
  • Project-based — $5-20K for a defined scope (e.g., launch campaign with 40-60 ad variations). Good for testing a new agency relationship before committing.

When to Hire an AI Creative Agency

Not every brand needs one. An AI creative agency makes the most sense when:

  • You are spending $10K+ per month on paid media and need consistent creative supply
  • Your current creative production cannot keep up with campaign scaling needs
  • You want to test more variations without proportionally increasing production cost
  • You have existing footage or brand assets that are underutilized
  • You need to launch on a new platform (like Walmart, TikTok, or Amazon) quickly — as ZenToes did for their Walmart launch

If you are spending under $5K/month on media with a single product, a freelancer or in-house effort may be more cost-effective. If you are between $5-10K, a project-based engagement can test whether an agency relationship makes sense before committing to a retainer.

Questions to Ask in the First Call

Come prepared. These questions separate serious agencies from presenters:

  1. “Walk me through a recent campaign from brief to results.” — Listen for specific steps, not generalities.
  2. “How many ad variations did you produce last month across all clients?” — Tests real volume, not theoretical capacity.
  3. “What was your worst-performing campaign and what did you learn?” — Honest agencies have failure stories. Perfect track records are fiction.
  4. “If I give you our existing assets, what can you produce without a new shoot?” — Tests zero-shoot capability.
  5. “What does your first 30 days look like?” — A good answer includes onboarding, asset audit, first batch production, and initial test structure.
  6. “Who will be working on my account?” — Understand whether you get senior strategists or junior operators running prompts.

The best indicator of a good agency is specificity. Vague answers to specific questions mean they are selling aspiration rather than a proven system.

How to Run a Trial Engagement

Before signing a long-term contract, structure a paid trial:

  1. Share a real brief — not a test brief. Give the agency a real campaign need with real constraints.
  2. Provide existing assets — footage, product photos, brand guidelines. See how they work with what you already have.
  3. Set clear metrics — agree on what success looks like before production starts. Volume, turnaround, and at least one performance metric.
  4. Run for 4-6 weeks — enough time for one production cycle and one iteration based on performance data.
  5. Evaluate the system, not just the ads — did they have a structured process? Did they iterate based on data? Did the communication and reporting meet your expectations?

The trial should cost $3-8K depending on scope. If an agency will not do a paid trial, that is a signal — either they are too large to bother or too uncertain about their results.

The Bottom Line

The AI creative agency landscape is noisy, but the evaluation framework is straightforward. Look for structured volume, real testing methodology, campaign-level performance data, hybrid human-AI workflows, and proven asset reuse capability.

The agencies that deliver are the ones that have built production systems — not the ones that have bought the best AI tools. Tools are table stakes. Systems are the differentiator.

The RaaS (Results as a Service) model is increasingly how the best AI creative agencies operate — accountable for performance outcomes, not just deliverables. When evaluating agencies, look for this mindset: do they talk about ads produced or about results delivered?

When you evaluate agencies, ask for specifics. How many ads, for which brands, with what results. The right agency will have numbers ready because those numbers are what their business is built on.

Questions people ask

What should I look for when hiring an AI ad creative agency?

Focus on five areas: proven volume (can they produce 30-80+ ads per month?), a testing methodology (not just production but structured iteration), hybrid capability (AI plus human creative direction), real campaign data with CPAs and ROAS, and asset reuse systems that reduce cost over time. Ask for case studies with specific numbers, not tool demos.

How much does an AI ad creative agency charge?

Pricing varies widely. AI product photography runs $0.10-$3 per image. AI-assisted video ads range from $50-$500 per video depending on complexity. Monthly retainers for full-service AI creative production typically start at $3-5K for 20-40 ads/month and scale from there. The real question is cost per winning ad, not cost per asset.

What is the difference between an AI creative agency and a traditional ad agency?

Traditional agencies produce 5-10 polished ads per campaign cycle. AI creative agencies build production systems that generate 30-100+ variations, test them systematically, and iterate on winners. The core difference is volume and speed — AI agencies treat creative as a testable, scalable asset rather than a one-shot deliverable.

Can an AI ad creative agency work with my existing footage and assets?

Yes, and this is a key capability to evaluate. Strong agencies can take existing creator footage, product photos, or brand assets and produce dozens of new variations without new shoots. One agency produced 80 fresh ads from old footage across 5 brands with zero new shoots. Ask specifically about asset reuse workflows.

How do I know if an AI creative agency's work will perform in paid campaigns?

Ask for campaign-level metrics, not just production samples. Good agencies will share CPA data, hook rates, and performance trends. For example, a longevity supplement campaign produced 68 ads at EUR 183 CPA using story-before-product format. If an agency cannot share real performance data from real campaigns, that is a red flag.
