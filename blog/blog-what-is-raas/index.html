What Is RaaS (Results as a Service)? The Business Model Replacing Hourly Billing

RaaS — Results as a Service — is the business model where you pay for outcomes, not hours. Learn what RaaS means, how it works across industries, and why Gartner predicts 40% of marketing budgets will shift to outcome-based models by 2028.

Guide9 min readby the OKAD team

Search for “RaaS” and you will find three different definitions: Ransomware as a Service (cybersecurity), Robot as a Service (robotics), and Recovery as a Service (IT disaster recovery). None of these is the RaaS reshaping how businesses buy and sell services in 2026.

Results as a Service is the model where you pay for outcomes instead of inputs. Not hours. Not retainers. Not seats. Not project fees. You pay for what was accomplished — and the provider earns based on what they deliver.

This is not theoretical. Salesforce charges $2 per AI agent conversation. Zendesk prices at $1.50 per resolved interaction. WPP, the world’s largest ad holding company, is restructuring its compensation around performance metrics. Gartner predicts 40% of search marketing budgets will shift to outcome-based models by 2028.

The shift is real, accelerating, and touching every service industry. Here is what RaaS actually means, how it works, and why it matters now.

RaaS Meaning: The Core Concept

Results as a Service inverts the traditional service relationship. Instead of buying time, capacity, or deliverables, you buy outcomes.

Traditional model: Pay $10,000/month for an agency retainer. You get hours, meetings, and deliverables — but results are not guaranteed.

RaaS model: Pay per lead generated, per conversion driven, per support ticket resolved, per ad creative that beats the performance benchmark. If results do not come, you do not pay full price.

The structural difference is where risk sits. In traditional models, the client absorbs all performance risk. In RaaS, the provider shares that risk — which forces them to build efficient systems, optimize constantly, and deliver measurable outcomes.

How RaaS Works Across Industries

RaaS is not limited to one sector. The model appears wherever service outcomes are quantifiable:

Advertising and Creative Production

Agencies traditionally charge retainers or per-project fees. Under RaaS, compensation ties to ad performance: cost per acquisition below target, return on ad spend above threshold, or creative win rate (percentage of ads that beat the control).

OKAD runs creative production on this principle. When we produce 80+ ad re-edits from existing footage or generate 60+ video variations for a product launch, the volume and efficiency make outcome-based pricing viable. The system produces enough variations to find winners — and winners are what the client is actually buying.

AI Customer Support

The clearest RaaS example in 2026. Salesforce Agentforce charges $2 per conversation. Zendesk charges $1.50 per resolved interaction. Intercom, Ada, and dozens of AI support companies have moved to per-resolution pricing.

Why this works: AI support has near-zero marginal cost per interaction once the system is built. The provider profits at scale. The client pays only for actual service delivered. Both sides win.

Recruitment

Pay-per-hire has existed for decades, but technology is making it more precise. Modern recruitment platforms charge a percentage of first-year salary only when a candidate is hired and stays past the probation period. No hire, no fee.

Logistics and Delivery

Companies like Uber Freight and Convoy price per delivery completed, not per truck dispatched. The customer pays for the result (package delivered) not the input (driver’s time on the road).

Legal Technology

AI-powered legal document processing charges per document reviewed, per contract analyzed, or per compliance check completed. The client pays for throughput, not for a lawyer’s hours.

Why RaaS Is Happening Now

Three forces are converging in 2026 that make RaaS viable at scale:

1. AI Reduces Marginal Costs to Near Zero

RaaS requires providers to be profitable at outcome-based rates. That is only possible when the cost of producing each additional unit is low. AI makes this real across industries:

  • An AI support agent handles its 1,000th conversation at the same cost as its 10th
  • AI-assisted ad creative production generates 50 variations from one shoot day — the marginal cost of variation #50 is nearly zero
  • AI document review processes 1,000 contracts at a fraction of the cost of the first 10

When marginal costs drop, providers can afford to tie revenue to outcomes rather than inputs. This is the fundamental enabler.

2. Measurement Infrastructure Is Mature

RaaS requires both parties to agree on what counts as a result — and to measure it accurately. In 2024, the tracking infrastructure became reliable enough:

  • Attribution platforms connect ad creative to conversions with reasonable accuracy
  • AI support platforms log every interaction and resolution status
  • CRM systems track lead-to-close pipelines end to end
  • Analytics tools measure engagement, retention, and lifetime value

Without measurement, RaaS is just a handshake deal. With it, contracts can specify exactly what triggers payment.

3. Clients Are Demanding Accountability

The era of “trust us, we are the experts” is ending. CMOs face pressure to justify every line item. Procurement teams demand measurable ROI. Boards ask for cost-per-outcome, not cost-per-hour.

This demand-side pressure is as important as the supply-side enablers. Even if AI had not reduced costs, clients would still be pushing for outcome-based pricing. AI just made it financially viable for providers to accept.

RaaS vs SaaS: The Key Differences

People often confuse RaaS with SaaS because the abbreviations are similar. They are fundamentally different:

SaaS (Software as a Service): You pay for access to a tool. $99/month for the software, whether you use it once or a thousand times. The provider sells the capability.

RaaS (Results as a Service): You pay for what the tool (or service) actually accomplishes. $2 per conversation resolved, $50 per lead generated, $X per creative that beats the benchmark. The provider sells the outcome.

SaaS is moving toward RaaS in many categories. When Salesforce charges per AI agent conversation instead of per seat, that is the SaaS-to-RaaS transition in action. The product is the same — but the pricing reflects value delivered, not access granted.

How to Evaluate a RaaS Provider

Not every company claiming “results-based pricing” is genuinely operating on RaaS. Here is what to verify:

1. Clear metric definition. What exactly counts as a “result”? A lead? A qualified lead? A closed deal? A resolved ticket? Ambiguity here leads to disputes.

2. Shared measurement system. Both parties should have access to the same dashboard. If the provider controls the only measurement tool, the data is not trustworthy.

3. Volume capability. RaaS providers need scale to be profitable. An agency producing 5 ads per month cannot afford outcome-based pricing — the risk per unit is too high. An agency producing 30 ads per day has the volume to absorb variance.

4. Track record with numbers. Ask for specific case data. Not “we drove great results” but “we produced 68 ads, 40% beat the control, client CPA dropped from EUR 250 to EUR 183” — like the data from our Longevity supplement campaign.

5. Fair risk sharing. Pure pay-per-result sounds attractive but is often unsustainable. A hybrid — base fee plus performance component — protects both sides. Providers who refuse any base fee may not have the financial stability to sustain quality.

The Hybrid Model: Where Most Companies Start

Pure RaaS — 100% of compensation tied to outcomes — is rare. Most real-world implementations use a hybrid:

  • Base component (40–60%): Covers operational costs, team allocation, and minimum viable service
  • Performance component (40–60%): Tied to agreed metrics with defined thresholds

This structure works because it gives the provider enough stability to invest in quality while keeping strong incentives to deliver results. The base covers costs; the performance layer drives profit.

As trust builds and measurement matures, the performance component tends to grow. Some long-term RaaS relationships evolve to 20% base / 80% performance — but that takes 6–12 months of shared data and mutual confidence.

What RaaS Means for Buyers

If you are buying services — hiring agencies, subscribing to tools, contracting freelancers — RaaS changes three things:

1. You need measurement infrastructure. Without reliable tracking, you cannot verify outcomes. Invest in attribution, analytics, and CRM before switching to RaaS.

2. You need clear success definitions. “We want more leads” is not a RaaS-ready brief. “We need 50 qualified leads per month at under $80 CPA” is. Define metrics before signing contracts.

3. You get better alignment. When your provider earns more from your success, their incentives match yours. Strategic conversations replace invoice negotiations. Both sides focus on what actually moves the number.

What RaaS Means for Providers

If you sell services — agency, SaaS, consultancy — RaaS requires operational changes:

1. Build for efficiency. You need to deliver results at a cost that leaves margin. If producing one unit of output costs you $X, your outcome-based price must exceed $X by enough to cover variance, overhead, and profit. AI content production and modular creative systems are what make this math work.

2. Accept controlled risk. Not every deliverable will hit the benchmark. Your pricing must account for a 20–30% miss rate while remaining profitable on the portfolio. This is why volume matters — one campaign is a coin flip; 50 campaigns are a statistical distribution.

3. Invest in measurement. You need to prove results to get paid. Build dashboards, share data proactively, and make your impact undeniable.

The providers who master RaaS will capture the market as clients increasingly refuse to pay for inputs. The ones who cling to hourly billing will compete on price with every other time-based provider — a race to the bottom.

The Future of RaaS

RaaS is not a trend that will plateau. Three developments are accelerating it:

AI agents are native RaaS products. Every AI agent company launching in 2025–2026 prices per outcome. This normalizes outcome-based pricing for the entire service economy. When a CMO pays Salesforce per resolved conversation, they start asking why they pay their agency per hour.

Holding companies are restructuring. WPP, Omnicom, and Publicis — collectively managing hundreds of billions in ad spend — are moving toward performance-based compensation. When the largest agencies adopt it, mid-market agencies must follow or lose clients.

Procurement is driving the shift. Enterprise procurement teams now evaluate vendors on cost-per-outcome, not cost-per-engagement. RFPs increasingly request outcome-based pricing structures. Providers who only offer retainers or hourly rates are being filtered out at the procurement stage.

By 2028, RaaS will not be an alternative model — it will be the expected default for measurable services. The question is not whether your industry will adopt it, but whether you will be ready when your clients demand it.

Results as a Service aligns what buyers want (outcomes) with what sellers deliver (efficient, measurable work). The technology, measurement infrastructure, and market demand have all converged. Whether you are buying or selling services, understanding RaaS is no longer optional — it is the foundation of how the service economy is repricing itself.

Questions people ask

What does RaaS stand for?

RaaS stands for Results as a Service. It is a business model where the provider charges for measurable outcomes — leads, conversions, completed projects, performance benchmarks — instead of hourly rates, retainers, or flat project fees. The client pays for what they get. The provider earns more when they deliver more.

Is RaaS the same as Ransomware as a Service?

No. The abbreviation RaaS is used in multiple contexts. In cybersecurity, RaaS refers to Ransomware as a Service — a criminal model. In business and marketing, RaaS means Results as a Service — a legitimate pricing model focused on outcome-based compensation. Context determines the meaning. This article covers the business model.

How is RaaS different from SaaS?

SaaS (Software as a Service) charges for access to a tool — you pay a subscription whether you use it or not. RaaS charges for outcomes delivered through a service — you pay based on what was accomplished. SaaS sells the tool. RaaS sells the result the tool would help you achieve.

What industries use RaaS?

RaaS is growing across advertising and creative production, SaaS customer support (AI agents priced per resolution), recruitment (pay per hire), logistics (pay per delivery), legal tech (pay per document processed), and consulting (pay per outcome achieved). Any industry where results are measurable can adopt RaaS.

Is RaaS right for my business?

RaaS works best when outcomes are clearly measurable, both parties agree on what counts as a result, the provider has enough efficiency and scale to be profitable at outcome-based rates, and the client has tracking infrastructure to verify results. If any of these conditions is missing, a hybrid model — reduced base fee plus performance bonus — is a safer starting point.
