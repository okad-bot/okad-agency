Creative Agency Pricing: What Brands Actually Pay in 2026

Transparent breakdown of creative agency pricing in 2026. Per-unit costs, retainer tiers, RaaS models, and total cost comparisons between freelancers, agencies, and in-house teams.

Guide5 min readby the OKAD team

Every brand needs creative. Nobody knows what to pay for it. Agency pricing is deliberately opaque — most agencies do not publish rates because pricing varies by scope, and because opacity lets them charge more.

This guide fixes that. Here is what creative agencies actually charge in 2026, broken down by service type, engagement model, and brand size.

The rule of thumb: spend 10-20% of your monthly ad spend on creative production.

Underspending on creative is the most common budget mistake. Running $50K per month in ad spend with $1,000 per month in creative production means you are showing the same fatigued ads to an increasingly saturated audience. CPA rises, ROAS falls, and you blame the media buyer when the creative pipeline was the bottleneck.

What to Ask Before Signing

  1. What is included in the monthly retainer versus what costs extra?
  2. How many revision rounds are included per concept?
  3. What are the payment terms — net 15, net 30, or prepaid?
  4. Is there a minimum commitment period? What is the cancellation policy?
  5. Do I own all content produced, including raw footage?
  6. Is strategy included, or is it an add-on?
  7. What happens if I need more content than the retainer covers?

The best agency relationships start with transparent pricing and clear scope. If you cannot get a straight answer on what things cost before signing, that opacity will not improve after you are a client.

Questions people ask

How much does a creative agency charge per video ad?

Per-video pricing ranges from $150-$500 for standard UGC videos and $500-$2,000 for produced ad creatives with scripting, editing, and multiple formats. Hook swap edits — re-cutting the first 3 seconds of an existing ad — run $15-$50 each. The wide range reflects differences in production complexity, creator tier, and whether strategy is included. Agencies that charge under $100 per video are typically using unvetted creators or AI-only production.

Is it cheaper to hire freelancers or use an agency?

Freelancers are cheaper per unit but more expensive in total when you factor in coordination time. A freelance UGC creator charges $150-$400 per video, but you manage briefing, revisions, and quality control yourself — typically 10-15 hours per week of someone's time. An agency costs more per video ($200-$500) but bundles production management, strategy, and quality control. The break-even point is usually around 10 videos per month: below that, freelancers win on cost; above it, the agency coordination savings justify the premium.

What should a monthly creative retainer include?

A monthly retainer should include: a defined number of ad concepts (typically 10-30), creative strategy and brief writing, creator sourcing and management, video editing and format adaptation, one to two revision rounds per concept, and monthly performance reporting. Retainers that bundle these services at $5,000-$15,000 per month are the most common model for DTC brands spending $10K-$100K per month on ads.

What is the difference between per-unit pricing and a retainer?

Per-unit pricing charges you per deliverable — per video, per static ad, per concept. You pay for what you use, with no commitment beyond the project. A retainer charges a fixed monthly fee for a defined scope of work. Retainers offer lower per-unit costs and priority scheduling but require monthly commitment. Per-unit works better for brands testing the agency relationship or with variable content needs.

How much should I budget for creative if I spend $50K per month on ads?

Budget 10-20% of your monthly ad spend on creative production. At $50K per month in ad spend, that means $5,000-$10,000 per month on creative — enough for 15-30 new ad concepts per month with an agency, or strategy plus production through a mid-tier retainer. Underspending on creative relative to ad spend is the most common mistake: you end up running the same fatigued ads longer, which drives up CPA faster than the creative savings.
