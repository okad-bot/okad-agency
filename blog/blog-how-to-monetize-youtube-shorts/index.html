Monetize YouTube Shorts: 7 Revenue Streams (2026)

7 ways to earn from YouTube Shorts — Partner Program, brand deals, affiliate links, and 4 more. Real payout data and what actually works in 2026.

For creators8 min readby the OKAD team

YouTube Shorts get billions of daily views, but creators consistently ask the same question: where is the money? The direct ad revenue from Shorts is low compared to long-form video. That is the wrong comparison to make.

Shorts are a discovery engine. They build audiences faster than any other YouTube format. The monetization comes from what you do with that audience — and the creators earning real income from Shorts understand this distinction.

Revenue Stream 1: YouTube Partner Program (Ad Revenue)

The most direct path, but also the lowest per-view payout.

Requirements:

  • 1,000 subscribers
  • 10 million Shorts views in 90 days OR 4,000 long-form watch hours in 12 months

What you earn:

  • Shorts ad revenue: $0.01–$0.07 per 1,000 views (RPM)
  • A Short with 1 million views earns roughly $10–$70

This is not a primary income source. A creator consistently hitting 1 million views per Short might earn $200–$500/month from ads alone. Useful, but not life-changing. The real value of YPP is unlocking other features (memberships, Super Chat) and the credibility of being a monetized creator.

Revenue Stream 2: Brand Sponsorships

This is where Shorts income gets serious.

Brands pay creators to feature products in Shorts because the format delivers massive reach at a fraction of the cost of long-form sponsorships. Typical rates:

How to get brand deals for Shorts:

  1. Create a media kit showing your average views, audience demographics, and engagement rate
  2. List yourself on creator marketplaces (AspireIQ, Grin, CreatorIQ)
  3. Reach out to brands in your niche directly — DM their marketing team on LinkedIn
  4. Join influencer agencies that specialize in short-form content

Brands care about your niche relevance and engagement rate more than raw subscriber count. A 20K-subscriber creator in the fitness niche with 500K average views per Short is more valuable than a 200K-subscriber general creator with 50K views.

Revenue Stream 3: Affiliate Marketing

Promote products in your Shorts and earn commission on sales.

How it works:

  • Join affiliate programs (Amazon Associates, ShareASale, brand-specific programs)
  • Feature products naturally in your Shorts
  • Link to products in your channel description or pinned comment
  • Earn 3–15% commission on each sale

Realistic earnings:

  • Beginner: $100–$500/month
  • Established creator: $500–$3,000/month
  • Top performers: $3,000–$15,000+/month

The key is choosing products with high commission rates and genuine relevance to your content. A tech review channel promoting a $200 product at 8% commission earns $16 per sale. At a 0.1% conversion rate on 1 million views, that is $16,000 from a single Short.

Revenue Stream 4: Driving Traffic to Your Own Products

The highest-margin strategy. Use Shorts as a funnel to:

  • Online courses — Teach what you know, use Shorts to demonstrate expertise
  • Digital products — Templates, presets, guides, toolkits
  • Physical products — Merch, branded items, curated products
  • Services — Consulting, coaching, freelance work
  • Memberships — Patreon, YouTube Memberships, Discord communities

A single Short that drives 100 people to a $47 digital product generates $4,700. That is more than most creators earn from millions of views in ad revenue.

The funnel:

  1. Short captures attention and demonstrates value
  2. CTA directs to bio link or pinned comment
  3. Landing page converts visitors to customers
  4. Email list nurtures repeat purchases

Revenue Stream 5: UGC and Content Licensing

Your Shorts skills translate directly to paid UGC work:

  • Brands hire creators who understand short-form video
  • Your Shorts portfolio proves you can create engaging vertical content
  • Canvas UGC platforms pay $50–$300 per asset for exactly the skills Shorts require
  • Content licensing deals let brands use your organic Shorts as paid ads

This is the most underutilized revenue stream. Creators with strong Shorts portfolios are exactly what brands need for their paid advertising — our guide on UGC video ads explains what brands look for. The bridge between “YouTuber” and “paid content creator” is shorter than most people realize. You can also explore faceless UGC if you want to create brand content without showing your face.

Building a Shorts Monetization Strategy

The highest-earning Shorts creators stack multiple revenue streams:

Month 1–3: Foundation

  • Post 3–5 Shorts per week consistently
  • Focus on growing subscribers toward YPP threshold
  • Study which topics and formats get the most views
  • Start building an email list or community

Month 3–6: First Revenue

  • Apply to affiliate programs in your niche
  • Create a simple digital product or service offer
  • Reach out to small brands for sponsored Shorts
  • Optimize bio links for conversion

Month 6–12: Scale

  • Diversify across 3+ revenue streams
  • Negotiate recurring brand deals
  • Launch or expand digital products
  • Consider UGC work for additional income

Year 2+: Optimize

  • Focus on highest-ROI revenue streams
  • Build team or systems for production efficiency
  • Develop premium products for your audience
  • Leverage Shorts audience for long-form content growth

Common Monetization Mistakes

  • Waiting for YPP before monetizing — Start affiliate marketing and product promotion on day one. You do not need 1,000 subscribers to earn money.
  • Only relying on ad revenue — Shorts RPM is low. If ads are your only plan, you will be disappointed.
  • Ignoring analytics — Watch which Shorts drive the most clicks to your links. Double down on those formats.
  • Promoting irrelevant products — Your audience followed you for a reason. Promote products that align with why they are there.
  • Not having a landing page — A “link in bio” to a YouTube channel does nothing. Create a simple page that converts visitors to subscribers, customers, or leads.

Monetization Timeline: Realistic Expectations

Here is a month-by-month projection for a new Shorts creator posting 4–5 times per week in a mid-value niche (health, beauty, or lifestyle):

Months 1–2: Zero Revenue

  • Building initial content library (30–40 Shorts)
  • Average views: 500–5,000 per Short
  • Monthly total views: 15,000–100,000
  • Income: $0 (below YPP threshold, no brand deals yet)
  • Focus: finding which content formats get traction

Months 3–4: First Affiliate Income

  • Audience: 200–800 subscribers
  • Average views: 2,000–15,000 per Short
  • Monthly total views: 50,000–300,000
  • Income: $50–$200 (affiliate links in bio)
  • Focus: optimizing content formats that perform, building affiliate partnerships

Months 5–7: YPP Application Range

  • Audience: 500–2,000 subscribers
  • Average views: 5,000–50,000 per Short
  • Monthly total views: 150,000–1,000,000
  • Income: $100–$500 (affiliates + first small brand deal)
  • Focus: reaching 1,000 subscribers, creating media kit

Months 8–12: Multiple Revenue Streams

  • Audience: 1,000–10,000 subscribers
  • Average views: 10,000–100,000 per Short
  • Monthly total views: 500,000–3,000,000
  • Income: $300–$2,000 (YPP + affiliates + brand deals)
  • Focus: landing recurring brand partnerships, launching digital product

Year 2: Optimization

  • Audience: 5,000–50,000 subscribers
  • Income: $1,000–$8,000/month across all streams
  • Focus: highest-ROI revenue streams, premium brand deals, product launches

This timeline assumes consistent posting without viral breakouts. A single viral Short (1M+ views) can compress this timeline by 2–3 months by accelerating subscriber growth past the YPP threshold.

The pattern is clear across all scenarios: ad revenue is the smallest slice. Brand deals and product sales generate 5–20x more income than the YouTube Partner Program alone. This is why the smartest Shorts strategy treats the platform as a distribution channel for your actual business, not as the business itself.

Questions people ask

Can you make money from YouTube Shorts?

Yes. YouTube Shorts can be monetized through the YouTube Partner Program (ad revenue sharing), brand sponsorships, affiliate marketing, channel memberships, and driving traffic to your own products or services. Most creators combine multiple revenue streams.

How much do YouTube Shorts pay per 1,000 views?

YouTube Shorts ad revenue ranges from $0.01 to $0.07 per 1,000 views, significantly lower than long-form content. However, Shorts generate much higher view counts, and the real monetization comes from using Shorts as a funnel to higher-value revenue streams like brand deals and product sales.

How many views do you need to monetize YouTube Shorts?

To join the YouTube Partner Program for Shorts, you need 1,000 subscribers AND either 10 million Shorts views in the last 90 days OR 4,000 watch hours on long-form content in the last 12 months. You can monetize through brand deals and affiliates with any subscriber count.

Are YouTube Shorts worth it for making money?

Yes, but not primarily through ad revenue. Shorts are worth it as a discovery and growth tool. They build your audience fast, attract brand deal opportunities, and drive traffic to higher-monetization content and products. The creators earning the most from Shorts use them as the top of their revenue funnel.

Which YouTube Shorts niches pay the most?

Finance and investing Shorts have the highest RPM at $0.04–$0.07 per 1,000 views, followed by tech ($0.03–$0.06) and business ($0.03–$0.05). These niches also attract higher-paying brand deals. Entertainment and gaming have the lowest RPM ($0.01–$0.02) but compensate with much higher view counts.
