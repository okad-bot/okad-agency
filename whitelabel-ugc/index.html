01 — Overview

Creator produces, brand distributes

  • Whitelabel UGC = creator films video, delivers raw files to the brand
  • Brand controls editing, captions, distribution, and all ad placements
  • Not posted on the creator's account or the brand's organic — used as paid ad material

Whitelabel UGC is the simplest creator content model. A brand pays a creator to film a video. The creator appears on camera, demonstrates the product, follows the brief, and delivers raw video files. The brand takes those files and uses them however they want: as paid ads, on landing pages, in emails, as social proof on product pages. The creator's job ends at delivery.

The content is never posted on the creator's personal account. It's not posted on the brand's organic social either (though it can be). It's built specifically to be ad creative — raw material the brand's media team edits, tests, and scales.

This is the most common UGC production model in performance marketing. It's straightforward: the brand buys content, not distribution. The creator is a production resource. The brand handles everything else.

  • Deliverables: raw video files (MP4), typically 15–60 seconds, vertical (9:16)
  • Pricing: $150–$500 per video
  • Usage: paid ads, landing pages, social proof, email
  • Creator involvement: ends at file delivery
02 — Definition

What is whitelabel UGC?

  • Creator films content, delivers raw video files to the brand
  • The creator appears on camera but has no role in distribution
  • Brand can edit, remix, add captions, test hooks — full creative control

Whitelabel UGC is a content production model where a creator films video content based on a brand's brief and delivers the raw files. The creator appears on camera — they are a real person talking about, using, or demonstrating the product — but they have no involvement in how the content is distributed. The brand owns the files and controls everything after delivery.

A quick example

A supplement brand pays a creator $200 to film a 30-second testimonial about their protein powder. The creator films in their kitchen, talks about their morning routine, and shows themselves making a shake. They export the video and send the raw MP4 file to the brand. The brand adds captions, creates three versions with different hooks, and runs them all as paid ads on Instagram Reels. The creator is done after delivery — no posting, no account access, no ongoing involvement.

Why brands choose whitelabel UGC

  • Full creative control — the brand can edit the footage, swap hooks, add branded captions, and create multiple variants from a single shoot
  • No creator account needed — unlike whitelisting, the brand doesn't need access to the creator's social account
  • Simplest model — no ongoing relationship, no account permissions, no authorization codes. Pay, receive files, done.
  • Remix potential — one raw video can be cut into multiple ad variants: different hooks, different lengths, different CTAs
  • Multi-platform use — the same files can be used across Meta, TikTok, YouTube, landing pages, and email

What whitelabel UGC is not

  • Not whitelisting — in whitelisting, ads run from the creator's account. In whitelabel, ads run from the brand's account using the creator's video files.
  • Not Canvas UGC — in Canvas, creators post on brand-owned social accounts. In whitelabel, content is delivered as files and never posted organically.
  • Not influencer marketing — the creator's audience is completely irrelevant. The brand is buying video production, not reach.
  • Not stock video — whitelabel UGC features a real person using and talking about a specific product. It's custom, not generic.

06 — Briefing

How to brief for whitelabel UGC

  • Brief = product + messaging + hooks + tone + format + do's and don'ts
  • Give talking points, not a word-for-word script
  • Include reference videos — "like this, for our product"

The whitelabel UGC brief template

  1. Product details. What the product is, what it does, key features, and who it's for. Include a product sample or free access so the creator can actually use it. Authentic experience makes better content.
  2. Key messages. 2–3 talking points the creator must cover. Not a script — bullet points they can express naturally. "Mention the 30-day guarantee." "Show the before and after." "Reference how long it took to see results."
  3. Hooks. Provide 3–5 hook options for the creator to choose from (or use as inspiration). Specific hooks: "I tried every productivity app and this is the only one that stuck." Generic hooks that don't work: "Check out this amazing product."
  4. Tone and style. Describe the energy: casual, energetic, calm, authoritative. Link 2–3 reference videos that show the style you want. "Like creator X's video about product Y, but for our product."
  5. Format specifications. Vertical 9:16. Duration (15–45s recommended). Ad type (testimonial, demo, unboxing, before/after). Whether face-to-camera, product-only, or mixed.
  6. Do's and don'ts. What to avoid: competitor mentions, medical/legal claims, showing competing products. What to include: product name (with correct pronunciation), specific CTA language, any required disclosures.

Briefing for remix potential

Since whitelabel UGC will be edited by the brand's team, brief creators to deliver "modular" content. Ask for: the full video as scripted, plus 2–3 alternate hook openings filmed separately. This gives the brand's media team raw material to assemble multiple ad variants without re-contacting the creator. One delivery becomes three or four testable ads.

07 — Quality

Quality control and revisions

  • 1–2 revision rounds are standard (included in price)
  • Review against the brief, not against subjective preference
  • Brand guidelines must be provided upfront, not after delivery

The quality review process

  1. Check against the brief. Did the creator hit all the talking points? Is the hook clear? Is the product shown correctly? Is the duration within spec? Review objectively against the brief, not subjectively against personal taste.
  2. Technical quality. Audio must be clear (no background noise, echo, or muffled voice). Lighting must be adequate (face visible, product visible). Video must be in focus and vertical. These are non-negotiable minimums.
  3. Authenticity check. Does the content feel natural? Over-rehearsed delivery kills the UGC advantage. The slight imperfections — natural pauses, casual language — are features, not bugs. Don't ask for re-takes that make the content feel scripted.
  4. Revision requests. If revisions are needed, provide specific, actionable feedback. "Re-record the hook using option 3 from the brief" is good. "Make it feel more energetic" is vague and leads to frustration. Limit revisions to 1–2 rounds — if the brief was clear, most content should be usable on the first take.

Approval workflow

Keep the approval chain short. The person reviewing the content should be the same person who wrote the brief. Adding multiple reviewers with different opinions leads to conflicting feedback, unnecessary revisions, and content that loses its authentic feel. One reviewer, one set of feedback, one round of revisions.

08 — Volume

The volume testing approach

  • Order 10–20 whitelabel videos, test all, scale winners
  • Winner rate: ~10–20% of variants beat CPA targets
  • Remix each raw video into 2–3 hook variants before testing

How volume testing works with whitelabel UGC

  1. Order a batch. Commission 10–20 whitelabel UGC videos from multiple creators. Vary the ad types (testimonial, demo, before/after), hooks, and creator demographics. Total production cost: $1,500–$5,000 for the batch.
  2. Remix for variants. The brand's media team takes each raw video and creates 2–3 variants: different hooks spliced onto the same body, different captions, different CTA text. 10 raw videos become 20–30 testable ad variants.
  3. Test with small budgets. Launch all variants with $50–$100 per variant in ad spend. Run for 48–72 hours. This is enough data to identify winners and losers.
  4. Kill losers, scale winners. Pause any variant that misses your CPA target after the testing period. Allocate remaining budget to the 2–4 winners. Scale winners until performance degrades (creative fatigue, typically 2–4 weeks).
  5. Repeat monthly. Order the next batch. Use learnings from the previous batch to refine your briefs: which hooks worked, which ad types performed, which creator styles resonated. Each batch gets better.

09 — Mistakes

Common mistakes with whitelabel UGC

  • Ordering too few videos = not enough data to find winners
  • Over-editing raw files = destroying the authentic UGC feel
  • Vague briefs = generic content that doesn't convert
  1. Ordering too few videos. Commissioning 2–3 whitelabel videos and hoping for a winner. The realistic hit rate is 10–20%. You need at least 10 videos per testing cycle to generate reliable learning. Think in batches, not singles.
  2. Over-editing the raw files. Adding brand intros, motion graphics, cinematic color grading, or music beds that make the content feel produced. The whole value of whitelabel UGC is that it looks authentic. Add captions. Don't add a studio finish.
  3. Vague briefs. Sending the creator a one-line brief like "Talk about how much you like the product." This produces generic content that doesn't convert. Invest in specific briefs with concrete hooks, clear talking points, and reference videos.
  4. Not requesting modular hooks. Only asking for one version of the video. Always request 2–3 alternate hook openings. Hooks are the biggest lever in ad performance, and testing multiple hooks per body content multiplies your testing capacity at minimal additional cost.
  5. Treating production as a one-time project. Ordering one batch of whitelabel UGC and expecting it to work for months. Creative fatigue is real — winning ads degrade after 2–4 weeks. Plan for ongoing monthly production.
  6. Ignoring performance data. Not tracking which creators, hooks, and ad types performed best. Every batch should inform the next. If testimonial ads from female creators aged 25–34 consistently outperform demo ads, your next batch should skew toward more of what works.
10 — Glossary

Key terms

Whitelabel UGC
Creator-produced video content delivered as raw files to the brand. The creator films and the brand controls all distribution. The simplest UGC production model.
Raw files
The original video files delivered by the creator — MP4 format, no platform compression. The brand receives clean source material for editing.
Remix / variant
Creating multiple ad versions from a single raw video by swapping hooks, captions, CTAs, or trimming to different lengths. One video becomes 2-3 testable variants.
Creative fatigue
When an ad's performance declines because the target audience has seen it too many times. Typically occurs after 2-4 weeks of running at scale.
Hook
The first 1-3 seconds of the video, designed to stop the scroll. Hooks are the single biggest lever in ad performance. Test multiple hooks per body.
Modular content
Briefing creators to deliver the main video plus 2-3 separate alternate hook openings, allowing the brand to assemble multiple variants without re-shoots.
Winner rate
The percentage of tested ad variants that beat the target CPA. Typical whitelabel UGC winner rate: 10-20%.
Creative testing
The systematic process of testing multiple ad creative variants to find winners. Whitelabel UGC is the production engine that feeds creative testing.
Canvas UGC
A different model: creators post on brand-owned social accounts and are paid per view. Distinct from whitelabel, where creators deliver files.
Whitelisting
A different model: brand runs paid ads from the creator's personal account. Distinct from whitelabel, where ads run from the brand's account.
