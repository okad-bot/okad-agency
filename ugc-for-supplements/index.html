Why supplement ads are harder

Claims get ads rejected

Health claims trigger Meta and TikTok review. Scripts need real language from real users, not marketing copy that sounds like a label.

Creators must look credible

A 19-year-old reading a script about joint pain doesn't convert. Casting matters more in supplements than in any other niche.

One winning ad dies in two weeks

Supplement audiences on Meta are narrow. Frequency climbs fast, and the only fix is a steady flow of new creative.

Competitors all look the same

Same bottle, same "I tried this for 30 days" format, same green-screen. Differentiation comes from the angle, not the production.

What works in supplement UGC

Before and after routines

Not prohibited transformation claims — a real person showing what their morning looks like with the product. Relatable > dramatic.

Ingredient education

Short explainers on one ingredient: what it does, why it matters, what the research says. Positions the brand as informed, not salesy.

Unboxing and first impression

The moment someone opens the package, reads the label, smells or tastes the product. Genuine reactions stop the scroll.

Problem-solution with real language

Scripts pulled from customer reviews and Reddit threads, not from the brand brief. "I was tired of feeling bloated every afternoon" beats "supports digestive health".

Stacking and routine integration

How the product fits into a routine the buyer already has: morning coffee, gym bag, nightstand. Context sells supplements.

Hook variations at scale

3 to 5 openings per video body. One hook for the pain, one for curiosity, one for social proof. Same footage, different first two seconds, completely different results.

FAQ

Can UGC ads make health claims?

Not directly. I script around real user experiences and avoid claims that trigger ad review. "I feel less bloated" from a real customer is fine; "clinically proven to reduce bloating" in an ad is not, unless you have the documentation and disclosures.

What kind of creators work for supplements?

People who look like the buyer: gym regulars for sports nutrition, women 35+ for wellness, parents for kids' vitamins. I cast from 600+ vetted creators matched to your audience.

How many videos do I need to test?

Start with 10 to 16 videos across 3 to 4 angles, each with 3 to 5 hook variations. That gives you 30 to 80 testable ads in the first batch. My testing framework explains the math.

Do you handle compliance review?

I write scripts that avoid common rejection triggers, but final compliance review is your responsibility, especially for FDA-regulated claims. I flag anything that looks risky before production.

How fast do supplement ads fatigue?

Faster than most niches. A winner usually lasts 10 to 14 days before frequency kills it. That is why I build in batches, not one-offs, and plan the next round while the current one is still running.
