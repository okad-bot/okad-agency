01 — Overview

What are Partnership Ads?

  • Meta’s ad format that lets brands run paid ads using a creator’s identity
  • Formerly called Branded Content Ads — rebranded to Partnership Ads in 2023
  • The ad appears under the creator’s handle, not the brand’s account

Partnership Ads are Meta’s creator-powered ad format for Instagram and Facebook. They allow a brand to take content that a creator has tagged as a “Paid Partnership” and promote it as a paid ad through Meta Ads Manager. The resulting ad appears in users’ feeds with the creator’s name, profile photo, and handle — not the brand’s. This creates a native trust signal that standard brand ads cannot replicate.

Meta originally launched this format as Branded Content Ads in 2019. The rebrand to “Partnership Ads” happened in mid-2023 as part of a broader update to Meta’s creator monetization tools. The underlying mechanics remain the same: a creator publishes content with a paid partnership tag, and the brand promotes it as an ad. But the expanded toolkit now includes more granular permissions, ad-level identity controls, and improved reporting for both parties.

For brands running creative testing programs, Partnership Ads are a critical format. They let you amplify top-performing creator content — whether from Canvas UGC programs, influencer collaborations, or dedicated branded content campaigns — as paid media with the creator’s identity intact. The result is ads that feel like organic creator posts, not polished brand advertising.

  • Platform: Instagram and Facebook (Meta ecosystem)
  • Ad identity: creator’s handle appears as the ad source
  • Setup: creator tags brand, brand promotes from Ads Manager
  • Formats: Reels, Stories, Feed posts, Carousel (Instagram and Facebook)
  • TikTok equivalent: Spark Ads
02 — Setup Process

How Partnership Ads work, step by step

  • Creator publishes content with a “Paid Partnership” tag
  • Brand approves the tag and gets access to promote the post
  • Brand creates the ad in Meta Ads Manager using the partnership post

The Partnership Ads workflow involves coordination between the creator and the brand. Unlike standard ads where the brand uploads creative directly, Partnership Ads require the creator to initiate the process by publishing content and granting the brand permission to promote it. Here is the full sequence.

The setup process

  1. Creator switches to a Professional account. Partnership Ads require the creator to have an Instagram Professional account (Creator or Business). Personal accounts cannot tag paid partnerships.
  2. Brand adds creator as an approved partner. In Meta Business Suite, the brand navigates to Settings → Branded Content and adds the creator’s Instagram handle as an approved partner. This allows the creator to tag the brand in paid partnership posts.
  3. Creator publishes content with the Paid Partnership tag. When creating a post, Reel, or Story, the creator enables “Add paid partnership label” in advanced settings and selects the brand as the partner. The post goes live with a “Paid partnership with [Brand]” label.
  4. Creator grants ad creation access. The creator toggles “Allow brand partner to promote” when adding the partnership label. This gives the brand permission to use the post as an ad creative in Ads Manager.
  5. Brand creates the ad in Meta Ads Manager. The brand opens Ads Manager, creates a new campaign, and selects the partnership post as the ad creative. The brand applies its own targeting, budget, bidding strategy, and optimization goals.
  6. Ad runs under the creator’s identity. The promoted post appears in users’ feeds with the creator’s name, handle, and profile photo. A “Sponsored” label and the “Paid partnership” tag are visible, but the primary identity is the creator — not the brand.

What users see

When a Partnership Ad appears in someone’s feed, the creator’s profile photo and handle are displayed at the top of the post — exactly as if the creator had posted it organically. Below the handle, a small “Paid partnership with [Brand]” label appears, along with the standard “Sponsored” tag. The visual hierarchy prioritizes the creator’s identity, which is why Partnership Ads consistently outperform standard brand ads on trust and engagement metrics.

This is the fundamental advantage. Users scroll past brand ads reflexively. A post that appears to come from a real creator — someone they might follow or whose content they consume — gets more attention, more engagement, and more trust. The ad is still clearly labeled as sponsored, but the psychological framing is “creator content” rather than “brand advertisement.”

03 — Requirements

Requirements and permissions

  • Creator needs an Instagram Professional account (Creator or Business)
  • Brand must add creator as an approved partner in Meta Business Suite
  • Creator must explicitly grant “Allow brand partner to promote” permission

Creator requirements

The creator must have a Professional account on Instagram — either the Creator or Business account type. Personal accounts do not have access to the paid partnership tag. Switching to a Professional account is free and takes about two minutes in Instagram settings. Once switched, the creator gains access to branded content tools, insights, and the ability to tag commercial partners.

The creator must also comply with Meta’s Branded Content Policies. These prohibit misleading claims, require transparent disclosure, and restrict certain product categories (tobacco, weapons, adult content). Creators who violate these policies risk losing access to branded content tools entirely.

Brand requirements

The brand needs an active Meta Business Suite (formerly Business Manager) account with a connected Instagram Business account and an Ads Manager account. The brand must add the creator as an approved branded content partner in Business Suite settings. This creates a two-way connection: the creator can tag the brand, and the brand can promote the tagged content.

Brands also need to ensure their Ads Manager account is in good standing — no policy violations, active payment method, and verified business identity. Partnership Ads use the same campaign infrastructure as standard Meta ads, so all standard Ads Manager requirements apply.

Permission levels

PermissionWho grants itWhat it allows
Paid Partnership tagCreator (per post)Creator labels the post as a paid partnership with the brand
Approved partner statusBrand (in Business Suite)Creator can tag this brand without manual approval per post
Ad creation accessCreator (per post toggle)Brand can use this specific post as an ad creative in Ads Manager
Ad-level creator identityAutomatic (Meta)The ad displays the creator’s name, handle, and profile photo

The key distinction is that ad creation access is opt-in per post. The creator must explicitly allow the brand to promote each piece of content. This protects creators from having their identity used in ads they did not approve. It also means brands need to coordinate with creators to ensure the permission toggle is enabled before launching campaigns.

04 — Formats

Formats and placements

  • Partnership Ads work across Reels, Stories, Feed, and Carousel on both Instagram and Facebook
  • Instagram Reels are the highest-performing placement for most brands
  • Each format has different specs, best practices, and performance profiles

Partnership Ads support every major content format across both Instagram and Facebook. The format of the original creator post determines where it can be placed as an ad. A Reel can be promoted as a Reels ad. A Feed photo can run in Feed placements. This flexibility lets brands test different content types while maintaining the creator identity advantage.

Instagram Reels: the dominant format

For most brands in 2026, Instagram Reels is the primary format for Partnership Ads. Reels are full-screen, sound-on, and algorithmically distributed to users beyond the creator’s follower base. When promoted as a Partnership Ad, a Reel retains its native feel — users encounter it in the Reels feed alongside organic content. The creator identity is displayed at the top, and the ad integrates seamlessly into the browsing experience.

The performance advantage of Reels over static feed placements is significant. Reels Partnership Ads typically deliver 20–40% lower cost-per-engagement compared to feed image ads, and the format naturally supports the kind of authentic, creator-led storytelling that Partnership Ads are designed for. If you are running Partnership Ads for the first time, start with Reels.

Stories: the direct-response workhorse

Stories Partnership Ads are ideal for direct-response campaigns. The full-screen format combined with swipe-up (or link sticker) CTAs creates a short path from attention to action. Stories feel intimate and time-sensitive, which makes them effective for limited-time offers, product launches, and app install campaigns. The trade-off is that Stories have a shorter content lifespan and lower organic reach than Reels.

Facebook placements

Partnership Ads are not limited to Instagram. Facebook Feed, Facebook Reels, and Facebook Stories all support the format. For brands targeting demographics that skew older or are more active on Facebook (particularly in markets outside the US), Facebook placements can deliver significantly lower CPMs. The creative format and partnership mechanics work identically across both platforms.

05 — Comparison

Partnership Ads vs standard brand ads

  • Partnership Ads outperform standard ads on trust, engagement, and conversion
  • The creator identity is the key differentiator
  • Standard ads still have advantages in speed and creative control

The core question for most brands is straightforward: why use Partnership Ads instead of running standard ads from the brand’s own account? The answer comes down to identity and trust. When an ad appears under a creator’s handle, users process it differently than when it appears under a brand’s logo. The creator identity triggers a “content” frame rather than an “advertisement” frame, which leads to measurably different performance.

MetricPartnership AdsStandard Brand Ads
Ad identityCreator’s handle and profile photoBrand’s logo and account name
Trust signalHigh — perceived as creator contentLower — immediately recognized as brand ad
Engagement rateTypically 2–3x higher than standard adsBaseline
CTR (click-through rate)15–50% higher on averageBaseline
CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions)Comparable or slightly higherBaseline
Conversion rate10–25% higher with strong creator matchBaseline
Creative controlRequires creator coordinationFull control — brand uploads directly
Speed to launchSlower — depends on creator posting and permissionsFast — brand creates ad immediately
Best forTrust-driven campaigns, top-funnel, social proofRetargeting, promotions, brand-controlled messaging

The performance gap is most pronounced at the top of the funnel — awareness and consideration campaigns where trust is the primary barrier. Users who have never heard of a brand are significantly more likely to engage with content that appears to come from a creator they relate to than from an unfamiliar brand account. At the bottom of the funnel (retargeting, cart abandonment), the gap narrows because users already know the brand.

The trade-off is operational complexity. Standard ads give the brand complete control — upload creative, set targeting, launch. Partnership Ads require coordination with the creator, dependency on their posting schedule, and a permission-granting workflow. For brands running creative testing at scale, this operational overhead is worth the performance gain, but it requires either dedicated internal resources or an agency partner to manage.

06 — Cross-Platform

Partnership Ads vs Spark Ads

  • Partnership Ads = Meta’s creator-powered ad format (Instagram & Facebook)
  • Spark Ads = TikTok’s creator-powered ad format
  • Same concept, different platforms, different mechanics

Partnership Ads and Spark Ads solve the same problem on different platforms: allowing brands to run paid ads using a creator’s identity instead of the brand’s own account. Both formats produce ads that look like organic creator content rather than polished brand advertising. But the implementation details, authorization workflows, and platform dynamics differ significantly.

AspectPartnership Ads (Meta)Spark Ads (TikTok)

Engagement retentionPaid engagement shown separately from organicAll engagement (paid + organic) accumulates on original post

Formats supportedReels, Stories, Feed (image/video), CarouselIn-Feed video only
CTA optionsStandard Meta CTA buttons (Shop Now, Learn More, etc.)Standard TikTok CTA buttons
Audience demographicsBroader age range, strong 25–54 segmentSkews younger, strong 18–34 segment
Best forBrands active on Instagram/Facebook, multi-format campaignsBrands active on TikTok, short-form video-first campaigns

Key differences in practice

Authorization workflow. Spark Ads use a simpler auth code system — the creator generates a code in TikTok, sends it to the brand, and the brand enters it in TikTok Ads Manager. The code has a set validity period (30, 60, or 365 days). Partnership Ads require a more structured setup through Meta Business Suite, with approved partner status and per-post permission toggles. The Meta workflow is more rigid but provides tighter control over which specific posts can be promoted.

Engagement handling. This is the biggest practical difference. On TikTok, Spark Ads accumulate all engagement (likes, comments, shares from both organic and paid distribution) on the original post. This means a Spark Ad campaign can permanently boost a post’s social proof. Meta Partnership Ads separate paid engagement from organic — the ad’s likes and comments are not added to the original post’s counts.

Running both simultaneously. For brands with a cross-platform strategy, running both Partnership Ads (Meta) and Spark Ads (TikTok) with the same creator content is a common and effective approach. The same creator can produce a Reel for Instagram and a TikTok video with similar messaging, then authorize both as paid ads. 07 — Best Practices

Best practices for Partnership Ads

  • Match the creator to the audience, not to the brand
  • Brief for authenticity, not for polish
  • Test organic performance before committing ad spend

Creator selection

The creator’s identity is the ad’s primary trust signal. Selecting the right creator is the single most important decision in a Partnership Ads campaign. The ideal creator is someone whose audience overlaps with the brand’s target demographic, whose content style feels native to the platform, and whose voice is genuinely relatable to the people the ad will reach. Follower count matters far less than audience quality and content style.

Look for creators who already produce content in your product’s category or adjacent categories. A skincare brand benefits more from a creator who regularly posts skincare routines than from a general lifestyle creator with a larger following. The audience’s existing trust in the creator’s expertise in that category transfers directly to the ad’s credibility.

Content briefs

The biggest mistake brands make with Partnership Ads is over-scripting the creator. Partnership Ads work because they look and feel like organic creator content. A heavily scripted ad with brand-approved talking points defeats the purpose. The brief should define the key message, any mandatory disclosures, and the desired CTA — then let the creator produce content in their natural style.

Effective briefs include: the product or service being promoted, the key benefit to highlight (one, not five), any specific claims to avoid, the desired CTA (link in bio, swipe up, shop now), and examples of the creator’s own content that the brand likes. The brief should be one page, not ten. More creative freedom for the creator typically means better-performing ads.

Test organic before paying for paid

The most efficient Partnership Ads strategy is to let creator content perform organically first, then promote the winners. When a creator publishes a partnership post, it reaches their organic audience. Posts that generate strong engagement organically — high saves, shares, comments, retention — are statistically more likely to perform well as paid ads. This organic-first approach turns every creator post into a free creative test.

This strategy is especially powerful when combined with Canvas UGC programs, where creators are already producing high-volume content on brand accounts. The organic winners from the canvas become Partnership Ad candidates, creating a natural pipeline from organic testing to paid amplification.

Scaling

Scale Partnership Ads by adding more creators, not by increasing spend on a single creator’s post. Each creator brings a unique identity and audience signal. Running Partnership Ads from 5–10 different creators simultaneously gives you multiple trust signals to test against different audience segments. The same product message delivered by different creators performs differently with different demographics.

08 — Measurement

Measurement and optimization

  • Track creator-level performance, not just campaign-level aggregates
  • Key metrics: engagement rate, hook rate, CTR, CVR, ROAS
  • Meta brand lift studies measure the trust advantage quantitatively

Measuring Partnership Ads requires a different lens than standard ad measurement. Because the creator’s identity is a key variable, you need to track performance at the creator level — not just at the campaign or ad set level. Two Partnership Ads with identical targeting but different creators can produce wildly different results. The creator is the creative variable.

Key metrics to track

Brand lift studies

Meta offers brand lift studies that measure the incremental impact of your Partnership Ads on brand awareness, ad recall, and purchase intent. These studies compare a test group (people who saw the ad) against a control group (similar people who did not) and quantify the lift attributable to the campaign. For Partnership Ads specifically, brand lift studies typically show a higher lift in ad recall and favorability compared to standard brand ads — a direct measurement of the trust advantage.

Brand lift studies require a minimum spend threshold (typically $30,000+ per study) and are best suited for larger campaigns. For smaller-budget campaigns, focus on engagement rate differentials and CTR comparisons between Partnership Ads and standard ads running the same product message to the same audiences.

Attribution and reporting

Partnership Ads use Meta’s standard attribution windows (1-day click, 7-day click, 1-day view). Both the brand and the creator can see performance data for partnership content: the brand sees full ad-level metrics in Ads Manager, and the creator sees organic and paid reach insights on their post. This dual visibility makes it easy to align on what’s working and what needs to change.

For brands running Partnership Ads alongside Spark Ads on TikTok, maintaining a unified cross-platform dashboard is essential. Track creator-level performance across both Meta and TikTok to identify which creators drive results on which platform. Some creators perform better on Instagram; others excel on TikTok. The data tells you where to allocate spend and which creators to scale.

09 — Glossary

Key terms

Partnership Ads
Meta's ad format that lets brands run paid ads on Instagram and Facebook using a creator's identity. The ad displays the creator's handle and profile photo as the source. Formerly called Branded Content Ads.
Branded Content Ads
The original name for Partnership Ads, used by Meta before the mid-2023 rebrand. The mechanics are identical; only the naming changed.
Paid Partnership tag
The disclosure label that appears on creator posts when they tag a brand as a commercial partner. Required by Meta's branded content policies and by advertising regulations in most jurisdictions.
Ad creation access
A per-post permission toggle that the creator enables to allow the brand to use their post as an ad creative in Meta Ads Manager.
Meta Business Suite
Meta's business management platform (formerly Business Manager) where brands manage Pages, Instagram accounts, ad accounts, and branded content partnerships.
Brand Collabs Manager
Meta's marketplace for connecting brands with creators. Provides search, outreach, and partnership management tools. Integrated into Meta Business Suite.
Professional account
An Instagram account type (Creator or Business) that provides access to branded content tools, insights, and monetization features. Required for Partnership Ads.
Spark Ads
TikTok's equivalent of Partnership Ads. Allows brands to promote existing creator posts as paid ads on TikTok, retaining all engagement on the original post.
Hook rate
The percentage of viewers who watch past the first 3 seconds of a video ad. A key early indicator of creative effectiveness for Reels and Stories Partnership Ads.
Brand lift study
A Meta measurement tool that quantifies the incremental impact of an ad campaign on brand awareness, ad recall, and purchase intent by comparing exposed vs. control groups.
Creative testing
The practice of systematically testing multiple ad creatives — hooks, formats, creators, angles — to identify top performers before scaling spend. See the full guide at /guide.
Canvas UGC
A delivery model where creators post content on brand-owned social accounts and get paid per view. Top organic performers from Canvas UGC programs often graduate to Partnership Ads.
10 — FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What are Partnership Ads?+
Partnership Ads are Meta's creator-powered ad format for Instagram and Facebook. They allow a brand to promote a creator's post as a paid ad while keeping the creator's identity (name, handle, profile photo) as the ad source. The ad appears as creator content with a 'Paid partnership' and 'Sponsored' label.
What happened to Branded Content Ads?+
Branded Content Ads were rebranded to Partnership Ads in mid-2023. The underlying mechanics are the same — creator tags brand, brand promotes the post. Meta updated the name as part of a broader refresh of its creator monetization tools.
Do creators need a certain follower count?+
No. There is no minimum follower count to use Partnership Ads. The creator needs an Instagram Professional account (Creator or Business type) and must comply with Meta's branded content policies. Follower count is irrelevant to eligibility — what matters is account type and policy compliance.
How are Partnership Ads different from Spark Ads?+
Partnership Ads run on Meta (Instagram and Facebook). Spark Ads run on TikTok. Both use the creator's identity as the ad source. Key difference: Spark Ads accumulate all engagement (paid + organic) on the original post, while Meta Partnership Ads separate paid engagement from organic. Authorization workflows also differ — Spark Ads use auth codes, Partnership Ads use Meta Business Suite approvals.
Can I run Partnership Ads and Spark Ads at the same time?+
Yes, and this is a common cross-platform strategy. The same creator produces similar content for both Instagram and TikTok, then authorizes both as paid ads. This lets brands reach audiences across both platforms with creator-powered content. OKAD manages cross-platform campaigns with unified reporting.
What formats work with Partnership Ads?+
Reels, Stories, Feed posts (image and video), and Carousels on both Instagram and Facebook. The format of the original creator post determines the available ad placements. Reels are the highest-performing format for most brands in 2026.
How much do Partnership Ads cost?+
Partnership Ads use the same auction system as standard Meta ads. CPMs vary by targeting, placement, and competition. The ad delivery cost is comparable to standard ads, but Partnership Ads typically deliver higher engagement and CTR — meaning the effective cost per engagement or cost per action is lower. Budget for creator compensation separately from ad spend.
Can the creator control which posts get promoted?+
Yes. The creator must explicitly enable 'Allow brand partner to promote' on each individual post. The brand cannot promote a creator's post without this permission. The creator can also revoke access at any time.
How do I find creators for Partnership Ads?+
Options include Meta's Brand Collabs Manager (built into Business Suite), creator marketplaces, agency networks, and direct outreach. The most important criteria are audience overlap with your target demographic and content style that matches the platform's native feel. Follower count is a secondary consideration.
