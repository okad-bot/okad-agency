01 — Prerequisites

What you need before starting Canvas UGC

  • You do NOT need a personal following, expensive equipment, or prior creator experience
  • Minimum gear: smartphone, tripod ($20–$30), and a quiet space with natural light
  • Key skills: speaking naturally on camera, basic video editing, following brand guidelines

Equipment (start minimal)

The biggest myth about content creation is that you need expensive gear. For Canvas UGC, you need exactly three things:

  • Smartphone: Any phone from 2022 or later with a decent front-facing camera. iPhone 13+ or Samsung Galaxy S22+ are more than enough.
  • Tripod: A $20–$30 phone tripod from Amazon. Stable footage beats shaky handheld every time. Get one with adjustable height.
  • Lighting: Natural window light is your best friend and costs nothing. Film facing a window during daytime. A $30 ring light helps for evening sessions.

Optional upgrades (not needed to start): External microphone ($30–$60), ring light ($25–$40), background screen ($30). Total starter kit cost: $20–$30. You probably already have the phone.

Skills you'll develop

You don't need these skills perfected before starting — you'll develop them through practice. But knowing what to work on helps you improve faster:

  • On-camera presence: Speaking naturally, maintaining eye contact with the lens, varying your tone
  • Scriptwriting: Writing concise, engaging scripts that hook viewers in the first 2 seconds
  • Video editing: Basic cuts, text overlays, and pacing using free tools like CapCut
  • Brand interpretation: Translating brand guidelines into authentic, natural-sounding content

Software (all free)

CapCut is the industry standard for Canvas UGC editing — it's free, powerful, and available on both mobile and desktop. For screen recordings (common in tech niches), use your phone's built-in screen recorder. For scripts, a simple notes app or Google Docs works fine.

02 — Step 1

Understand how Canvas UGC works

  • You create videos that are posted on the brand's social accounts, not yours
  • You get paid per view (CPM $2–$6), not per video
  • Volume and consistency matter more than going viral

Before creating your first video, you need to understand what Canvas UGC actually is and how it differs from traditional UGC work. The complete Canvas UGC guide covers this in depth, but here's the essential version.

In traditional UGC, you shoot video and deliver raw footage to a brand. They use it in their paid ads. You get a flat fee per video ($150–$500). The work is project-based — you constantly need to find new clients.

In Canvas UGC, brands give you access to post directly on their social media accounts (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts). The brand's account is the "canvas" where your content lives. You're paid based on how many views your videos generate — typically $2–$6 per 1,000 views (CPM). For detailed earning benchmarks, see our Canvas UGC earnings guide.

This model has three key advantages for beginners:

  • No following needed: Content goes on the brand's account, so your personal follower count is irrelevant
  • Ongoing income: Unlike project-based UGC, you have a continuous relationship with the brand
  • Lower quality bar: Canvas UGC prioritizes authenticity and volume over polished production

03 — Step 2

Pick your niche

  • Choose a niche where you have genuine knowledge or strong interest
  • High-CPM niches (tech, finance, SaaS) pay more but require subject matter fluency
  • Start with one niche — you can expand later

Your niche determines your CPM rate, your audience, and how naturally your content comes across. The best niche is one where you can speak for 60 seconds without a script and sound knowledgeable. Don't chase the highest-paying niche if you have nothing genuine to say about it — viewers can tell.

How to validate your niche

Before committing, do this 10-minute validation exercise: Open TikTok and search for your niche keyword (e.g., "AI tools", "skincare routine"). Watch 20 videos in that niche. Can you make content at this level or better? Do the topics interest you enough to post 15+ videos per month about them? If yes, you've found your niche. If not, try another.

Don't overthink this step. You can always pivot niches after your first month. The important thing is to pick something and start creating.

04 — Step 3

Build a mini portfolio

  • Create 3–5 sample videos before applying to programs — brands want to see what you can do
  • Make the samples look like real brand content: pick real products/apps in your niche
  • Keep them 30–60 seconds, vertical format, with strong hooks

No brand or program will hire you without seeing examples of your work. But you don't need a massive portfolio — 3–5 solid sample videos are enough to get started. For detailed portfolio advice, see our UGC portfolio guide.

What to create for your portfolio

Pick 3–5 real products, apps, or services in your chosen niche. Create sample videos as if a brand had briefed you. These don't need to be posted anywhere — they're just demos of your capability. Here's a practical framework:

  • Video 1: Talking-head review or recommendation ("I've been using [product] for a week and here's what I think...")
  • Video 2: Screen recording walkthrough or demo (for tech niche) or before/after (for beauty/fitness)
  • Video 3: Problem-solution format ("Stop doing [X], try [product] instead")
  • Video 4: Listicle or tips format ("3 things I wish I knew about [topic]")
  • Video 5: Day-in-the-life or routine that naturally features a product

Portfolio presentation

Upload your samples to Google Drive or create a simple portfolio page. Include a brief bio, your niche focus, and the sample videos. When applying to programs, share this link. First impressions matter — make sure the videos load quickly and are organized clearly. Check out real examples in our portfolio examples guide.

05 — Step 4

Find your first brand

  • Join creator networks and agencies that connect creators with Canvas UGC programs
  • Apply directly to brands running Canvas UGC — look for multi-account TikTok/Reels operations
  • Your first brand doesn't need to be perfect — it's a learning opportunity

Finding your first Canvas UGC opportunity is the hardest step because you have no track record yet. Here are the most effective channels, ranked by success rate for beginners:

1. Creator networks and agencies

The fastest path is joining an established creator network that already has relationships with brands running Canvas UGC programs. Agencies like OKAD manage the brand relationships, handle payments, and match you with programs in your niche. You focus entirely on creating content. This is the path we recommend for beginners because it removes the burden of client acquisition and contract negotiation.

2. Direct outreach to brands

Identify brands that are already running Canvas UGC programs. Look for companies with multiple TikTok accounts posting creator-style content (not polished brand videos). Reach out via email or LinkedIn with your portfolio and a short pitch. Keep it to 3–4 sentences: who you are, your niche, a link to your portfolio, and what you're proposing.

3. Creator platforms and job boards

Platforms like Billo, JoinBrands, and Insense occasionally list Canvas UGC opportunities alongside traditional UGC gigs. Filter for "ongoing" or "multiple videos" to find Canvas-style programs versus one-off projects. The pay may be lower through these platforms, but they provide a low-friction entry point.

4. Social media (Twitter/X, Reddit, TikTok)

Search "Canvas UGC hiring" or "looking for Canvas UGC creators" on Twitter/X. Several Canvas UGC program managers post hiring calls regularly. Reddit communities like r/UGCcreators occasionally have opportunity threads. On TikTok itself, search for #canvasugc and connect with creators who share their experience — they often know which programs are hiring.

06 — Step 5

Create your first videos

  • Follow the brand's brief closely but keep the delivery natural and authentic
  • Nail the hook — the first 2 seconds determine whether the video gets views
  • Aim for 30–60 second videos; batch-film 5–10 per session

Once you're accepted into a program, you'll receive a brand brief covering the product, key talking points, content guidelines, and posting schedule. Here's how to turn that brief into performing content:

The video creation workflow

  1. Script the hook and key points

    Write your opening line word-for-word. This is the most important part of the video. Then outline 2–3 key points and your closing statement. Don't script every word — bullet points keep the delivery natural.

  2. Set up and film

    Position your phone on the tripod, check your framing (head and shoulders, slight angle), and verify lighting. Film 2–3 takes of each video. The best take is usually the second one — first take has nerves, third starts to feel rehearsed.

  3. Edit for pacing

    Import into CapCut. Cut dead air, long pauses, and filler words. Add text overlays for key points. Add background music at 10–15% volume. Keep the final video between 30–60 seconds. Faster pacing keeps viewers watching.

  4. Review against the brief

    Watch the final cut and check: Does the hook grab attention? Are all required talking points covered? Does it feel authentic, not salesy? Is it the right length? If yes, submit or post.

Hook formulas that work

Your hook rate (percentage of viewers who stay past the first 1–3 seconds) determines how far the algorithm pushes your video. Here are proven hook templates:

  • "Stop scrolling if you..." — calls out a specific audience segment
  • "I found [something] that..." — creates curiosity about a discovery
  • "This [product] changed how I..." — implies transformation
  • "Nobody talks about [topic], but..." — positions as insider knowledge
  • "Here's why [common belief] is wrong" — creates tension through contrarianism
07 — Step 6

Optimize for views

  • Track which videos perform best and replicate those formats
  • Post at consistent times — the algorithm rewards predictability
  • Respond to comments to boost engagement signals

After your first 10–15 videos, you'll have enough data to start optimizing. Since your earnings are tied directly to views, optimization directly increases your income. Here's what to focus on:

Analyze your performance data

Look at your top 3 performing videos and your bottom 3. What's different? Usually it comes down to: the hook (how compelling were the first 2 seconds?), the topic (was this something viewers actively care about?), and the format (talking head vs. screen recording vs. POV). Double down on whatever your top performers have in common.

Posting timing and frequency

Algorithms reward accounts that post consistently. Aim for a regular posting schedule — ideally once per day or every other day. The best posting times vary by niche and audience, but generally: weekdays between 7–9 AM and 6–9 PM local time for the target audience. Consistency matters more than finding the "perfect" time.

Engagement signals

Comments, shares, and saves tell the algorithm to distribute your content more widely. Encourage engagement naturally: ask questions in your videos, create mild controversy (hot takes), or share surprising information. Reply to comments on your posts — responses boost the video's engagement score and often trigger additional algorithmic distribution.

A/B test your hooks

Try creating two versions of the same content with different hooks. Post them on different days or on different brand accounts. Compare hook rates (viewers who stayed past 3 seconds). Over time, you'll build a mental library of hooks that reliably work in your niche. This single practice can double your average views per video within a month.

08 — Step 7

Scale to multiple brands

  • Once you're consistent on 1–2 accounts, add more brand accounts to multiply income
  • Batch production is the key to managing multiple accounts without burning out
  • Negotiate higher CPM rates after proving consistent performance

Scaling is where Canvas UGC earnings go from side income to serious money. The math is straightforward: if you earn $600/month from one brand account, working with four accounts earns you $2,400/month — assuming similar performance and CPM rates.

When to scale

Don't add accounts too early. Scale when you can consistently produce 10–15 quality videos per month on your current accounts without stress. If you're still figuring out your content style, adding more accounts will spread thin effort across too many places. A good signal: your average views per video have stabilized or are growing, and production feels routine rather than effortful.

Batch production at scale

Batching is non-negotiable at scale. Set aside 2–3 dedicated filming days per week. On each day: script 5–8 videos in the morning, film them all back-to-back in the afternoon, edit them in the evening or the next day. Most creators managing 4+ accounts spend 15–20 hours per week total, not 15–20 hours per account.

Negotiate as you grow

After 2–3 months of consistent performance, you have leverage. Ask for higher CPM rates by showing your track record: total views delivered, average views per video, engagement rates. Brands retain proven creators because recruiting and onboarding new ones costs time and money. Even a $0.50 CPM increase across 300,000+ monthly views adds $150+/month. See our earnings guide for detailed negotiation strategies.

Cross-niche expansion

Once you've mastered one niche, consider expanding to adjacent niches. A tech reviewer can naturally branch into productivity tools, AI tools, and SaaS. A beauty creator can expand to wellness, fitness, or fashion. Each new niche opens up new brand account opportunities. Just make sure you can speak authentically about the new topic.

09 — Mistakes

Common mistakes beginners make

  • Overproducing content instead of shipping fast and iterating
  • Ignoring analytics and repeating underperforming formats
  • Giving up too early — the first month is always the slowest

Perfectionism over volume

The number one mistake is spending 2 hours perfecting a single video when you could create 4 decent videos in the same time. Canvas UGC rewards volume. A "good enough" video that gets posted beats a perfect video still sitting in your drafts. Your tenth video will be dramatically better than your first regardless of how much time you spend on either one.

Sounding like an ad

Viewers scroll past anything that feels like a commercial. The whole point of Canvas UGC is authentic, creator-style content. Talk about the product the way you'd tell a friend about it — with genuine enthusiasm where it's warranted and honest commentary where it's not. Forced enthusiasm is worse than no enthusiasm.

Neglecting the hook

Spending 5 seconds on an intro before getting to the point. In short-form video, you have 1–2 seconds to capture attention. Your hook should be the very first thing out of your mouth. No "Hey guys, welcome back" — jump straight into the value proposition or the surprising statement. Read our hook rate guide for data-backed strategies.

Not tracking analytics

Posting content without reviewing performance data is flying blind. After every batch of 10 videos, review: which hooks worked? Which topics got views? What time of day performed best? The creators who earn the most are the ones who treat this as a data-informed process, not guesswork.

Quitting after month one

Month one is always the worst. The brand account may still be warming up, you're still learning the format, and your content quality hasn't hit its stride yet. Most creators who stick with it see a 2–3x improvement in views by month two. Give yourself at least 60 days before evaluating whether Canvas UGC works for you.

Copying other creators instead of finding your style

It's fine to study what works, but directly copying another creator's style makes your content feel inauthentic. Viewers and algorithms both favor originality. Use other creators for inspiration on formats and topics, but develop your own voice, delivery style, and perspective.

11 — FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a social media following to start Canvas UGC?+
No. Canvas UGC content is posted on brand-owned accounts, not your personal profiles. Your follower count is completely irrelevant. What matters is your ability to create engaging short-form video content. Many successful Canvas UGC creators have zero personal social media presence.
What equipment do I need to start?+
Minimum: a smartphone (2022 or later), a phone tripod ($20-$30), and natural lighting (a window). Optional upgrades include a ring light ($25-$40) and an external microphone ($30-$60). You can start with under $30 in equipment. CapCut (free) is the standard editing software.
How much can I earn as a beginner Canvas UGC creator?+
Beginners typically earn $200-$800 per month in their first 1-3 months. This grows to $800-$2,500/month by month 3-6, and $2,500-$8,000+ after 6 months of consistent work. Earnings depend on CPM rate ($2-$6 per 1,000 views), content volume, and niche selection.
How many videos do I need to create per month?+
Most Canvas UGC programs expect 10-15 videos per month as a minimum. Higher-volume creators producing 20-40 videos per month earn proportionally more. At a batch-production pace, 15 videos takes roughly 6-8 hours per week including scripting, filming, and editing.
What's the difference between Canvas UGC and regular UGC?+
In traditional UGC, you deliver raw video footage and the brand uses it in their paid ads. You get a flat fee per video ($150-$500). In Canvas UGC, you post content directly on brand-owned social accounts and get paid per view (CPM). Canvas UGC offers ongoing income versus one-off payments.
Can I do Canvas UGC alongside a full-time job?+
Yes, most beginners start Canvas UGC as a side hustle. Producing 10-15 videos per month takes 5-8 hours per week if you batch your production. Many creators do all their filming on weekends and edit during weekday evenings.
How long until I get my first payment?+
Typically 30-45 days from your first posted video. Programs usually pay monthly in arrears — you post content in month 1 and receive payment early in month 2. Allow 1-2 weeks for portfolio building and application before that.
What niche should I choose?+
Pick a niche where you have genuine knowledge or strong interest. Tech/SaaS pays the highest CPM ($4-$6) but requires comfort with screen recordings and app demos. Lifestyle and beauty niches have lower barriers to entry but more competition and lower CPM ($2-$3.50). The best niche is one you can talk about naturally.
What if my first videos don't get many views?+
This is completely normal. First-month videos typically get fewer views because the brand account may still be warming up and you're still learning what works. Focus on volume and iteration rather than perfection. Most creators see a 2-3x improvement in views by month two as they refine their hooks and content style.
How do I find Canvas UGC programs that are hiring?+
Three main channels: (1) Join creator networks like OKAD that match you with programs, (2) search social media for 'Canvas UGC hiring' posts on Twitter/X and Reddit, (3) identify brands running multi-account TikTok operations and reach out directly with your portfolio.
